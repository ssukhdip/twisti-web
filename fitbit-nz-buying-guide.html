Fitbit in NZ: The Complete Buying Guide

Fitbit is now owned by Google, and its NZ range has narrowed as Google folds features into Pixel Watch. Here's what's still worth buying, and what it costs.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
How we picked these: We checked live NZ listings at Noel Leeming and PB Tech alongside price-comparison data from PriceSpy and PriceMe to confirm what's actually in stock and what it's selling for, not RRP.

Fitbit's place inside Google now

Google completed its acquisition of Fitbit back in 2021, and since then Fitbit has increasingly become the budget/fitness-first counterpart to Google's own Pixel Watch line, which runs Wear OS and targets smartwatch buyers directly. Fitbit devices still run their own simpler OS, focused on step counting, heart rate, sleep tracking and long battery life rather than apps and notifications. That makes Fitbit relevant in NZ specifically for buyers who want fitness tracking without smartwatch complexity, or a lower price than a Pixel Watch or Apple Watch.

Current NZ lineup

The Fitbit Charge 6 is the flagship fitness band, adding Google Maps and YouTube Music controls plus more accurate heart rate tracking (borrowed from Pixel's sensor tech) over the Charge 5. NZ retailer pricing for the Charge 6 runs from around $244 at PB Tech up toward the mid-$260s at other stores, depending on colour and sales. The Fitbit Inspire 3 sits below it as the budget entry point for basic step/sleep tracking, usually well under $150. The Fitbit Versa and Sense ranges cover the smartwatch-style form factor with a screen large enough for notifications, sitting in the middle of Fitbit's range and Noel Leeming's regular stock list, though newer Versa/Sense generations have slowed as Google leans more on Pixel Watch for that tier.

Fitbit Premium subscription

A chunk of Fitbit's more useful features — detailed sleep score breakdowns, guided programs, and advanced health metrics — sit behind the Fitbit Premium subscription, priced similarly to other wellness app subscriptions internationally (Google typically bundles a free trial period with new device purchases). Basic step count, heart rate and general sleep tracking work fine without a subscription, so it's not a mandatory cost, but if you want the analytics Fitbit is known for, budget for it.

Where retailers differ

PB Tech tends to list the sharpest prices on current-generation Fitbit trackers like the Charge 6. Noel Leeming carries the widest in-store range and colour options, useful if you want to see the band in person before buying, and often bundles in accessories like charging cables. Because Fitbit's NZ range has been quietly shrinking as Google prioritises Pixel Watch, availability of older Versa/Sense models can be inconsistent — check stock before assuming a specific model is still sold new.

Fitbit vs Pixel Watch vs Apple Watch

If you're inside the Android/Google ecosystem and want deep smartwatch functionality (apps, calls, full notification handling), the Pixel Watch is the better buy and now shares a lot of Fitbit's health-tracking engine anyway. Fitbit still wins on battery life — a Charge 6 lasts about a week per charge versus roughly a day for most smartwatches — and price. iPhone owners should just buy an Apple Watch, since Fitbit's iOS integration is more limited than either Wear OS or watchOS devices.

Who should buy Fitbit in NZ

  • Want simple, accurate fitness tracking with a week of battery life: Fitbit Charge 6.
  • Budget tracker, basics only: Fitbit Inspire 3.
  • Want a proper smartwatch inside the Google ecosystem: skip Fitbit's Versa/Sense line and buy a Pixel Watch instead.
