Steam Deck vs ROG Ally

Valve's Steam Deck OLED and ASUS's ROG Ally X are still the two default answers when someone asks "which handheld gaming PC should I buy" — and in New Zealand, the price gap between them is bigger than most spec sheets let on.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
How we picked these: We compared Valve and ASUS's official specifications against independent benchmark testing, and checked current New Zealand retail pricing rather than US launch prices, which don't translate directly.

The short version

The Steam Deck OLED is the cheaper, longer-battery-life, better-screen option running Valve's own SteamOS, built for people who mostly want to play their existing Steam library on the couch or on a plane. The ROG Ally X is the faster, heavier, more expensive option running full Windows 11, aimed at people who want higher frame rates, Xbox/Game Pass integration, and the flexibility (and hassle) of a real Windows PC in handheld form.

Performance

The Ally X uses newer Zen 4-derived AMD silicon with an RDNA 3 GPU block and 24GB of RAM, against the Steam Deck OLED's older Zen 2 + RDNA 2 APU with 16GB of RAM. In real-world testing this translates to the Ally X pulling ahead in AAA titles — reviewers have measured roughly 30-50% higher frame rates in demanding games at matched settings. If raw performance in newer, heavier titles is the priority, the Ally X wins clearly.

Display and battery

The Steam Deck OLED's 7.4-inch HDR OLED panel is the standout feature: perfect blacks, 1,000 nits peak brightness, and genuinely excellent for a handheld at this price. The Ally X's 7-inch IPS panel runs a sharper 1920×1080 at up to 120Hz, so it's smoother in fast-paced games even if the OLED's contrast and colour still look better in mixed lighting.

Battery capacity favours the Ally X on paper (80Wh vs the Deck's 50Wh), but that's largely offset by the Ally X's more power-hungry chip — real-world battery life between the two ends up much closer than the raw Wh numbers suggest, and the Deck often lasts longer per charge in less demanding titles.

Operating system: the part that actually changes how you use it

This is the detail that matters more than any spec. SteamOS on the Deck is built for handheld use from the ground up — it boots straight into a controller-friendly interface, sleeps and wakes instantly, and just works. Windows 11 on the Ally X gives you access to any PC game store (Xbox app, Epic, Battle.net) and background apps, but you're still dealing with Windows update prompts, a desktop that wasn't designed for a small touchscreen, and less consistent sleep/wake behaviour. Some buyers value the openness; others find it genuinely annoying on a handheld.

Price in New Zealand

This is where the decision often gets made for people. In NZ, the Steam Deck OLED typically runs from around $1,650 for the 512GB model up to roughly $1,900 for 1TB. The ROG Ally X is considerably more expensive locally, generally listed from around $2,000. That's a meaningfully larger gap than the US pricing you'll see quoted in overseas reviews, so it's worth comparing current NZ retailer listings rather than assuming US prices apply here.

Who should buy the Steam Deck

Anyone with an existing Steam library, anyone who wants the simplest possible handheld experience, and anyone price-conscious. The OLED screen alone makes it feel like the more premium object despite being the cheaper machine.

Who should buy the ROG Ally X

Anyone who wants maximum frame rates in current AAA games, needs access to non-Steam storefronts (especially Xbox Game Pass), or already lives comfortably in the Windows ecosystem and doesn't mind trading some simplicity for flexibility and horsepower.
