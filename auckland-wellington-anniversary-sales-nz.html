Anniversary Day Sales NZ Explained

New Zealand's regional anniversary days create a rolling series of long weekends through the first few months of the year, and retailers often use them as an excuse for a sale. Here is what to expect and how to approach them.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

What Anniversary Day actually is

Anniversary Day is a regional public holiday observed on different dates depending on where you live, commemorating the founding of each province. Auckland, Wellington, Nelson, Otago, Southland and other regions each get their own date, mostly clustered between January and March. Because the holiday is regional rather than national, retail sales tied to it can look quite different depending on which part of the country you are in.

How retailers use these long weekends

A three-day weekend is a reliable trigger for retail promotions, since foot traffic and browsing both tend to increase. National electronics retailers with stores in multiple regions often run "Anniversary Weekend" sales that apply everywhere, even in regions not currently observing the holiday, simply because it is an easy marketing hook. Online-only retailers use the same weekends for similar reasons, regardless of location.

Regional timing to keep in mind

Auckland Anniversary typically falls in late January, Wellington Anniversary usually lands in late January as well, and other regions follow through February and March. If you are watching for a specific sale, check the date for your own region rather than assuming it matches a friend's in another city — national retailers sometimes stagger their promotions to cover each one.

Is the discount usually meaningful?

Anniversary weekend sales are generally smaller than the major calendar events like Boxing Day or Black Friday. Expect modest percentage-off promotions on selected categories rather than storewide blowouts. That said, for a specific item you have been watching, even a modest discount stacked with a already-competitive price can be worth acting on.

Tip: Because these sales are frequent and regionally staggered, there is rarely urgency to buy the moment a sale starts. If you miss an Auckland Anniversary sale, a similar promotion often shows up again within a few weeks for another region's long weekend.

Should you shop around during Anniversary weekends?

These weekends are a reasonable time to check prices, particularly for mid-range electronics like headphones, small appliances and accessories, but they are not typically when retailers release their biggest discounts of the year. Comparing prices across a few retailers before committing is still the most reliable way to know if an "Anniversary Sale" tag reflects a genuinely lower price or just a marketing label.
