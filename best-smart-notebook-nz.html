Best Smart Notebook NZ: Which to Buy

"Smart notebook" covers two very different products — reusable erasable paper notebooks and full e-ink writing tablets like reMarkable. They solve different problems, and one costs ten times more than the other.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Two products, same marketing category

Reusable notebooks (Rocketbook and similar) are physical paper-like pages you write on with a special pen, then wipe clean with a damp cloth — or scan each page with your phone's camera via an app before erasing. They're cheap, simple, and genuinely useful if you just want to reduce paper waste while still keeping a phone-scannable backup of your notes.

E-ink writing tablets (reMarkable, Boox, Kindle Scribe-type devices) are actual digital devices with a screen, storage, and often note organisation, PDF markup and cloud sync. They cost significantly more — often several hundred dollars — but replace an entire paper notebook habit with something searchable, backed up, and infinitely reusable without a pen and wipe cycle.

Which fits how you actually work?

Rocketbook-style reusable notebook: good if you like physical pen-on-paper feel, want an affordable way to digitise notes occasionally, and don't need advanced organisation — just photos of pages saved to cloud storage.

E-ink tablet (reMarkable/Boox-style): worth the price if you take notes constantly — meetings, lectures, project planning — and want everything searchable, organised into folders, and synced automatically without manually snapping photos of each page.

Handwriting-to-text accuracy — the reality

Handwriting recognition on e-ink tablets has genuinely improved, but accuracy still depends heavily on how neat your handwriting is. Clear, deliberate handwriting converts well. Fast, messy note-taking (which is how most people actually write under time pressure) still produces errors that need manual correction. Don't buy a smart notebook expecting perfect transcription — treat the text conversion as a convenient rough draft, not a finished document.

Who actually benefits over a plain notebook or a tablet

The honest answer: fewer people than the marketing suggests. If you already have an iPad or Android tablet with a stylus, a note-taking app like GoodNotes or Notability often does everything a dedicated e-ink device does, minus the calmer paper-like screen and battery life measured in weeks rather than hours. The genuine case for a dedicated e-ink writing tablet is people who find a backlit tablet screen distracting or eye-straining for long writing sessions, or who specifically want a single-purpose device without notifications and apps pulling focus.

  • Students and note-heavy professionals: benefit most from searchable, organised digital notes without the distraction of a general tablet.
  • Casual list-makers and journallers: a cheap reusable paper notebook covers this without the expense.
  • Anyone who already owns a stylus-compatible tablet: probably doesn't need a separate device — a good notes app achieves the same result.
  • People who lose paper notebooks constantly: cloud sync on an e-ink tablet solves that specific problem well.
Battery life reality: e-ink tablets genuinely last weeks on standby thanks to the display tech, but active daily writing use will bring that down significantly — check real user reviews rather than marketing claims.

Where to buy in NZ

PB Tech and JB Hi-Fi both stock reMarkable and some Boox models. Noel Leeming and Harvey Norman carry more mainstream tablet options if you're leaning toward a general tablet-plus-stylus setup instead. Reusable paper notebooks are widely available through stationery retailers and Whitcoulls.
