Apple Watch vs Garmin for running: which one actually makes you faster

Not "which watch is better" — which one gives a runner the data and reliability that matters on race day. Here's the honest breakdown.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

The short version

For casual and social runners who already live in the Apple ecosystem, Apple Watch Series 11 covers the basics well and integrates beautifully with your iPhone. For anyone training seriously — chasing a marathon PB, following a structured plan, or racing multisport — Garmin (Forerunner or Fenix, depending on budget) is built specifically for this and it shows in the depth of the data.

GPS accuracy on the run

In independent half-marathon testing, a Garmin watch logged 13.15 miles against an official 13.1-mile course, while an Apple Watch logged 13.22 miles on the same run — Garmin was marginally more accurate. Garmin's mid-range and premium running watches use multi-band GPS with SatIQ, purpose-built to hold a satellite lock through forest cover, urban canyons and tunnels. Apple Watch Ultra 3 closes the gap with dual-frequency GPS, but the standard Series 11 lags slightly behind Garmin's dedicated running hardware. For a weekly park run, you won't notice the difference. For a trail race through dense bush, you probably will.

Training metrics that actually change what you do next

This is Garmin's biggest advantage for serious runners: training load, recovery time advisor, race predictor, VO2 max trends, and running dynamics like ground contact time and vertical oscillation — all designed to tell you whether to push hard tomorrow or take a rest day. Apple Watch's Training Load feature (introduced more recently) is a genuine step forward, but it's still a simpler, single-number view compared to Garmin's layered training science. If you follow a structured plan from Garmin Coach, a certified coach's TrainingPeaks account, or just like nerding out on your own data, Garmin gives you more to work with.

Race-day battery life

A marathon plus travel and warm-up time can easily eat 6-8 hours of battery. Apple Watch Series 11's 24-hour rating handles that fine for most people, but if you're doing an ultramarathon or multi-day stage race, its battery simply won't last — this is squarely Garmin territory, where even mid-range watches run for days in GPS mode and premium models stretch into a week or more with battery-saving satellite settings.

Running app ecosystem

  • Apple Watch — native support for Strava, Nike Run Club and other third-party apps, tight Health app integration, and Workout Buddy (powered by Apple Intelligence) for in-ear coaching cues during a run.
  • Garmin — Garmin Connect is purpose-built around structured training: custom workout builder, adaptive training plans, race predictor, and Strava sync as standard. The Connect IQ app store adds data fields and watch faces built specifically for runners.
The real decision point isn't the watch — it's whether you'll actually use the extra data. Garmin's training metrics are only valuable if you look at them and adjust your training. If you just want a Strava-friendly pace and distance tracker with great everyday smartwatch features, Apple Watch Series 11 does that job well and you won't miss what you're not using.

The verdict

If you're a casual-to-committed runner who's already an iPhone owner and wants one watch for running, notifications and daily life — get the Apple Watch Series 11. It's genuinely good for running now, not just an afterthought.

If you're training for something specific — a marathon block, a triathlon, an ultra — and want data that actually informs your training decisions, plus the battery life to survive long events — get a Garmin. A Forerunner is the running-focused sweet spot; step up to Fenix if you also want rugged multisport and navigation features.
