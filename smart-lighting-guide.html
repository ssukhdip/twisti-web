Smart Lighting Explained

Smart lighting is the most popular smart home starting point for a reason — it's cheap to try, easy to install, and the payoff (dimming, colour, scenes, schedules) is immediate. The confusing part is picking the right underlying tech before you've bought a dozen bulbs.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

The three ways smart bulbs connect

Every smart bulb or switch uses one of three wireless approaches, and the choice affects reliability, hub requirements and cost more than any brand name does.

  • Wi-Fi bulbs (e.g. TP-Link Tapo, most budget brands): connect directly to your home Wi-Fi, no hub required. Simplest to set up out of the box, but each bulb is a separate device on your router, which can slow things down or cause dropouts if you have many, and they tend to use more power just staying connected than Zigbee alternatives.
  • Zigbee bulbs (e.g. Philips Hue, IKEA Tradfri): use a low-power mesh protocol instead of Wi-Fi, which needs a hub or bridge to translate between Zigbee and your home network/app. More reliable at scale (bulbs relay signal to each other, strengthening the mesh as you add more), lower power draw, but that extra hub is a mandatory upfront cost. See our Zigbee explainer for how the protocol itself works.
  • Bluetooth bulbs: connect directly to your phone at short range, no hub, no home Wi-Fi dependency — but limited range and typically no remote (away-from-home) control unless paired with a separate bridge. Less common for whole-home setups, more common for single-room budget kits.

Do you need a hub?

Wi-Fi bulbs: no. Bluetooth bulbs: no, but limited. Zigbee bulbs: yes, always — this is the detail that catches first-time buyers out, since a Zigbee bulb bought on its own is essentially inert until paired with a compatible bridge (Hue Bridge, IKEA Dirigera, or a general-purpose Zigbee hub like the ones used with Home Assistant). Once you have one hub, though, it can typically support dozens of bulbs, sensors and switches — the hub cost is a one-off, not per-bulb.

Common gotcha: people buy a single Zigbee bulb expecting it to work like a Wi-Fi bulb straight out of the box, then find out it needs a $50-100 bridge to do anything beyond turning on and off from the manual switch. Check whether a bulb's ecosystem requires a hub before you buy just one to test the water.

Dimming, colour and scenes

Nearly all smart bulbs dim smoothly via the app rather than needing a compatible dimmer switch (important, since many older NZ homes have simple on/off wall switches, and you'll want to either replace the switch or keep it always-on and control brightness only through the app). Colour-changing bulbs (RGB + tunable white) let you shift from warm white for evenings to daylight white for tasks, and set up automated or manual scenes — a "movie night" scene dimming everything to warm low light, or a "morning" scene ramping up brightness gradually as an alarm.

Retrofit bulbs vs hardwired smart switches

This is a genuinely important decision, more than brand choice. Retrofit smart bulbs screw into your existing light fittings — no electrician needed, fully reversible, and portable if you move house. The catch: if anyone in the house flicks the physical wall switch off, the bulb loses power entirely and becomes uncontrollable until it's switched back on — a common source of "why doesn't my smart bulb work" frustration in shared households.

Hardwired smart switches replace the wall switch itself, keeping the existing (dumb) bulbs but making the switch smart — solving the "someone turned it off at the wall" problem, at the cost of needing a licensed electrician for installation (as with any mains wiring work in NZ) and being fixed in place rather than portable.

Which NZ brands are actually available

Philips Hue (Zigbee, needs Hue Bridge, widest ecosystem and best build quality, priced at a premium), IKEA Tradfri (Zigbee, needs Dirigera hub, budget-friendly and available at IKEA NZ), and TP-Link Tapo (Wi-Fi, no hub, cheapest entry point, widely stocked at JB Hi-Fi, Noel Leeming and PB Tech) cover most of the market here. All three now offer Matter-compatible options to varying degrees — see our Matter explainer if cross-ecosystem compatibility matters to your setup.

Do you actually need it?

Bathroom and laundry lights rarely benefit from being smart — you flick them on and off in seconds and colour/dimming adds little. Living rooms, bedrooms and outdoor lighting are where smart lighting earns its keep: dimming for evenings, scheduling for security while away, and colour scenes for ambience. Start with the rooms you actually spend evening time in rather than converting the whole house at once.

Quick decision guide

  • Just want to try it cheaply: a Wi-Fi bulb like Tapo, no hub needed.
  • Building a bigger, more reliable setup: Zigbee (Hue or Tradfri) plus a hub.
  • Shared household, switches get flicked off: consider hardwired smart switches instead of bulbs.
  • Want everything to work with any app: prioritise Matter-labelled products.
