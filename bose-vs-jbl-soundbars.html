Bose vs JBL Soundbars

Bose sells fewer models but polishes them hard; JBL sells a huge range with more bang-per-dollar at the low end. Here's how they actually compare for NZ lounges.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
How we picked these: Compared Bose's Smart Soundbar range (including the Smart Soundbar 600/700/Ultra) against JBL's Bar series (Bar 500 through Bar 1300X), using Bose NZ's official pricing and published international JBL pricing, cross-checked against NZ retailer listings.

The two lineups

Bose's current soundbar range centres on the Smart Soundbar (entry), Smart Soundbar 600, and the flagship Smart Ultra, all supporting Dolby Atmos and Bose's own "Personal Surround Sound" spatial audio processing that's tuned specifically for the listener's seating position. JBL's Bar range is much broader, spanning affordable 2.0 setups up to the Bar 1300X — a full system with a wireless subwoofer and detachable rear surround speakers you can pull off the bar and place around the room for genuine surround sound.

Pricing in NZ

The Bose Smart Soundbar 700 lists around $1,134.95 NZD through NZ retailers, with the newer Smart Ultra pitched as Bose's premium option at a similar or higher price point via bose.co.nz. JBL's range is priced to compete across more tiers — the Bar 500 sits in the mid-range, while the flagship Bar 1300X (with detachable surrounds and a wireless sub) typically lands in similar territory to Bose's higher-end models once landed in NZD, though JBL's mid-tier options usually undercut Bose at equivalent feature levels. Exact NZ pricing fluctuates by retailer — JB Hi-Fi, Noel Leeming, and Bose's own NZ store are the main channels for both brands.

Sound quality reputation

Bose has a long-standing reputation for tight, well-balanced sound tuning even from compact hardware — it doesn't always have the specs on paper of pricier systems, but reviewers consistently rate Bose's dialogue clarity and room-filling presence highly for its size. JBL's flagship Bar 1300X system takes the opposite approach: bigger hardware, a proper wireless subwoofer, and detachable surround speakers that deliver a more genuinely immersive, discrete-surround experience — closer to a real home theatre setup than a single-bar solution.

Features that matter day-to-day

  • Voice assistant support: Bose soundbars typically include built-in Alexa and Google Assistant compatibility depending on model; JBL's higher-end bars also support Chromecast built-in and AirPlay 2 on many models.
  • Detachable surrounds: a genuine JBL Bar 1300X advantage — the rear speakers pop off the main bar and run on battery for a few hours, letting you place them properly around a room without extra wiring.
  • App control and room calibration: both brands offer app-based EQ and room calibration; Bose's implementation is generally considered slightly more polished and simpler to use.

Which one to buy

  • Want the most polished sound from a single compact bar, minimal fuss: Bose.
  • Want a genuine surround system with detachable rear speakers and a proper subwoofer, and don't mind more setup: JBL Bar 1300X or similar high-end Bar model.
  • Budget-conscious but still want Dolby Atmos: JBL's mid-range Bar models generally offer more features per dollar than Bose's entry-level Smart Soundbar.
