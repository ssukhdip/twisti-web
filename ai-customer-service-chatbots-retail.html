AI Customer Service Chatbots: Do They Actually Help?

Nearly every major NZ electronics retailer now greets you with a chat bubble before a human. Here's what these bots are actually good at, and when they waste your time.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Why every retailer has one now

Retailers like PB Tech, Noel Leeming, JB Hi-Fi and Harvey Norman have all rolled out AI-driven chat support over the past couple of years, typically layered on top of their existing help-desk software. The pitch is simple: handle the repetitive stuff automatically — order status, warranty basics, store hours, stock checks — so human staff can focus on trickier problems. For simple questions this generally works well and can be faster than waiting on hold.

Where they genuinely save time

If your question has a factual, lookup-style answer, a chatbot is often the quickest path to it. Good use cases include:

  • Checking whether an item is in stock at a specific store
  • Tracking a courier delivery or order status
  • Finding a product's basic specs or compatible accessories
  • Locating a store's return policy or opening hours

These are pattern-matching tasks the bot can usually resolve in a message or two, without you needing to speak to anyone.

Where they fall short

Chatbots tend to struggle once a question requires judgement, negotiation, or knowledge of an edge case that isn't in their training data. Common frustration points reported by shoppers include faulty-item claims that need a case-by-case decision, price-match requests, and anything involving a partially damaged or opened product. In these situations the bot will often loop you through generic troubleshooting steps before eventually offering a human handoff — which can feel slower than just asking for a person up front.

Bot vs human: a rough guide

Use the chatbot for stock checks, order tracking, and basic spec questions. Skip straight to a human (or phone support) for warranty disputes, refunds on damaged goods, and anything involving money back. Most NZ retailer chat widgets have a "talk to a person" option — look for it early rather than after several failed attempts.

Spotting a chatbot vs a real person

Some retailers label their bot clearly ("Hi, I'm an AI assistant"), but not all do. If responses feel oddly fast, overly generic, or keep repeating the same phrasing regardless of what you type, you're likely talking to a bot. That's not necessarily bad — it just means you should keep your questions simple and factual rather than trying to negotiate or explain a nuanced situation.

Tip: If a chatbot can't resolve your issue within two or three exchanges, ask directly for a human agent or the store's phone number rather than continuing to rephrase your question — this usually gets you to a resolution faster.

The bottom line for NZ shoppers

AI chatbots are a genuinely useful first stop for quick, factual questions, and they've cut down wait times for simple queries across most major NZ electronics retailers. But they're not a replacement for human support when something has actually gone wrong with an order. Knowing which bucket your question falls into before you start typing will save you the most time.
