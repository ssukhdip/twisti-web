Meta Quest 3S NZ Price Guide

Meta's Quest 3S brought mixed-reality passthrough down to a genuinely affordable price — here's exactly what it costs to buy in New Zealand.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
How we picked these: Pricing collected from current NZ retailer stock at JB Hi-Fi, Noel Leeming, and The Warehouse, checked against Meta's official specification pages.

What the Quest 3S is

The Meta Quest 3S is the budget sibling to the pricier Quest 3, trading a slightly lower-resolution pancake lens setup for a much lower price while keeping the same Snapdragon XR2 Gen 2 chipset and full-colour mixed-reality passthrough. For most people who just want to try VR and mixed reality without committing to flagship pricing, it's the sensible starting point.

Current NZ pricing

The Quest 3S starts at around $589 NZD for the 128GB model, with a 256GB configuration around $729 NZD. It's widely stocked at JB Hi-Fi, Noel Leeming, and The Warehouse, with Mighty Ape also carrying it online and occasionally bundling a game voucher.

Quest 3S vs Quest 3

The full Quest 3 (from around $829 NZD) uses pancake lenses for a slimmer headset and sharper edge-to-edge clarity, plus a higher-resolution passthrough camera for mixed reality. The 3S uses Fresnel lenses instead, which are bulkier and have a smaller sweet spot for sharp focus, but perform almost identically for most games and apps at a genuinely lower price. Unless you're particularly sensitive to lens clarity or plan heavy mixed-reality use, the 3S saves a meaningful amount of money for a very similar experience.

Storage: is 128GB enough?

128GB is workable if you mostly play a handful of games at a time and don't mind uninstalling between sessions, but VR titles are growing in file size and a library of five or six bigger games will fill it quickly. If budget allows, the 256GB model is the safer long-term choice, especially for households sharing one headset across multiple people's game libraries.

What's included in the box

The Quest 3S ships with two Touch Plus controllers, a charging cable, and a soft strap (rather than the more comfortable Elite Strap, which is a separate purchase for around $79 NZD). Most reviewers recommend budgeting for the Elite Strap upgrade if you plan sessions longer than 30 minutes, since the stock strap tends to slip during active gameplay.

No PC required, but it helps

The Quest 3S works fully standalone, but it can also connect to a gaming PC via Meta's Air Link software or a USB-C cable for PC VR titles with more demanding graphics. This flexibility is one reason it out-competes fully standalone-only headsets from other brands at a similar price.

Who it suits

  • First-time VR buyers wanting to try the category without the Quest 3's premium price.
  • Families wanting a shared headset for casual gaming and fitness apps.
  • PC gamers wanting an affordable way to also try PC VR via Air Link.

Where to buy in NZ

JB Hi-Fi and Noel Leeming run the most frequent bundle deals, often including a game credit or an extra controller grip. The Warehouse tends to be competitive on the base 128GB model specifically, particularly around school-holiday sales periods.
