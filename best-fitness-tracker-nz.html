Best Fitness Tracker NZ

If you want fitness and sleep tracking without a full smartwatch's bulk, price or battery anxiety, these are the bands and trackers actually worth buying in NZ right now.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
How we picked these: We compared devices stocked at Noel Leeming, PB Tech and JB Hi-Fi NZ, plus the subscription-based Whoop and Oura Ring, weighing upfront cost, ongoing subscription fees, battery life and what each device is actually good at.

Fitbit Charge 6 -- the mainstream default

The Fitbit Charge 6 remains the most recognisable fitness band in NZ, stocked at Noel Leeming and PB Tech, with PriceSpy showing a best price around $266 NZD and RRP typically a bit higher depending on colour. It tracks heart rate, sleep stages, SpO2, and now includes Google Maps and YouTube Music controls plus built-in GPS, so you can leave your phone behind on a run. Battery life is a real-world 6-7 days with the always-on display disabled, dropping to 4-5 with it on. The catch is that some of Fitbit's more detailed health metrics now sit behind a Fitbit Premium subscription, so factor that into the true cost if you want the full experience.

Xiaomi Smart Band -- best budget option by a distance

Xiaomi's Smart Band line is the value pick in NZ. PB Tech has listed the Xiaomi Smart Band 9 Active from around $59 NZD and the Pro version from around $159 NZD, undercutting Fitbit by a wide margin. Battery life is the standout feature -- typically 14-21 days per charge depending on model and display usage, versus less than a week for the Fitbit and Apple ecosystem bands. Tracking accuracy for steps and heart rate is respectable for the price but a step behind Fitbit and Garmin for things like sleep stage detail and workout auto-detection. For someone who just wants steps, basic heart rate and long battery life without ongoing costs, it's hard to beat on price.

Whoop -- subscription-only, built for training load

Whoop doesn't have an upfront device cost -- the band comes bundled with the subscription, sold in NZ through JB Hi-Fi. The current Whoop Peak plan auto-renews at roughly $489 NZD a year after the first year, with monthly payment options at a higher effective rate. Whoop has no screen at all, which forces you into the app for everything, but it's genuinely excellent at strain and recovery scoring for people doing structured training -- gym, running, cycling -- who want daily guidance on how hard to push. It's a poor fit for someone who just wants a simple step counter, given the ongoing cost.

Oura Ring -- for people who won't wear a wrist band

The Oura Ring trades wrist real estate for a titanium ring, which some people find far more comfortable to sleep in than a wristband. Pricing is typically $299-349 USD upfront for the ring itself (Oura doesn't have a dedicated NZ storefront with NZD pricing at time of writing, so budget on current exchange rates plus possible import GST -- realistically $500-600 NZD landed), plus a subscription of $5.99 USD a month for full feature access. It's excellent specifically for sleep tracking and readiness scoring, less useful if you want live workout stats on your wrist since there's no display.

Which one should you actually buy

  • Want it simple and cheap: Xiaomi Smart Band 9 Active, around $59 NZD, no subscription.
  • Want the best all-rounder with GPS: Fitbit Charge 6, around $266 NZD, optional Premium subscription.
  • Serious about structured training and recovery: Whoop, subscription-only from roughly $489 NZD/year.
  • Prioritise sleep tracking and discretion: Oura Ring, ring form factor, roughly $500-600 NZD landed plus monthly fee.
