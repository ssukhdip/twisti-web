BUYING GUIDE

Best Bike Computer NZ: Which One to Buy

Your phone already has GPS, so why spend $200-$900 on a dedicated bike computer? For some riders it's genuinely worth it — for others it's a solved problem already sitting in their jersey pocket.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Phone app or dedicated computer?

Strava, Komoot and Ride with GPS on your phone will get most casual riders through a Sunday loop with zero extra spend. The catch is battery drain — running GPS and a screen for three hours can flatten a phone fast, especially in cold Otago or Central North Island mornings when batteries already underperform. A phone mounted on your bars is also more exposed to a hard crash than a purpose-built unit, and glare on a phone screen in bright NZ summer sun is genuinely worse than on a dedicated head unit's transflective display.

A dedicated bike computer earns its keep once you're riding regularly, want turn-by-turn navigation on unfamiliar routes, or you're training with a heart-rate strap, power meter or cadence sensor. Once you're pairing multiple sensors, a phone app becomes fiddly and battery life gets worse, not better.

What features actually matter

Ignore the spec-sheet arms race and focus on three things: battery life for the rides you actually do, screen readability in direct sun, and whether it pairs with the sensors you already own or plan to buy.

Casual rider vs serious cyclist

Casual commuter or weekend rider: a basic GPS computer with route tracking and simple navigation (Garmin Edge Explore-tier, Wahoo Elemnt Bolt-tier) covers everything you need. You don't need power meter compatibility or mapping for multi-day bikepacking trips.

Serious or training-focused cyclist: look for ANT+ and Bluetooth sensor support, structured workout compatibility, and proper turn-by-turn routing with rerouting if you miss a turn. This is where Garmin's Edge range and Wahoo's Elemnt Roam/Bolt line earn their higher price.

Garmin vs Wahoo ecosystem lock-in

If you already own a Garmin watch, staying in the Garmin ecosystem means your training data, Body Battery and recovery metrics all sync into Garmin Connect without extra apps. Wahoo's Elemnt units have a reputation for a simpler, more reliable interface with fewer menus, but the ecosystem is thinner if you're also using a Garmin watch elsewhere. There's no wrong answer here — it mostly comes down to what you're already invested in.

Navigation is the feature worth paying for

If you ride the same three routes every week, navigation is irrelevant and you're wasting money on a mapping unit. If you travel for events, ride unfamiliar trails, or do longer touring rides, proper turn-by-turn navigation with offline maps is the single feature that justifies stepping up a tier. Cheaper units often only show a breadcrumb trail rather than real street/trail names, which is far less useful when you're actually lost.

Power meter pairing: if you're training with a power meter, check ANT+ compatibility specifically — some budget computers only support Bluetooth sensors, which can drop connection more easily mid-ride than ANT+.

Where to buy in NZ

  • PB Tech — broad range of Garmin and Wahoo units, often competitive on price for the Edge and Elemnt lines.
  • JB Hi-Fi — carries mainstream Garmin models, worth checking for bundle deals with accessories.
  • Bike-specific retailers (Torpedo7, R&R Sport, local bike shops) — better for fitting, sensor setup help, and warranty support if something goes wrong mid-ride.
  • Noel Leeming / Harvey Norman — less specialised in cycling gear but occasionally stock entry-level Garmin units at good prices during sales.

Whichever you choose, compare prices across retailers before buying — the same Garmin Edge model can vary by $50-100 between stores depending on who's clearing stock.
