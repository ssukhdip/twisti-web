Anker SOLIX Power Station NZ: Price, Specs & Where to Buy

Anker's SOLIX range covers everything from a phone-charger-sized battery to a whole-house backup unit. Here's what's actually worth buying in New Zealand, and what it costs.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
How we picked these: We looked at Anker's official NZ storefront (ankersolix.co.nz), NZ retailers stocking SOLIX gear, and published capacity/output specs. Pricing below reflects NZD listings checked in July 2026 — power station pricing moves often with sales, so treat these as a guide rather than gospel.

What "SOLIX" actually covers

SOLIX is Anker's branding for its solar and battery ecosystem, which spans small portable power stations, panels, and larger fixed home-battery systems. For most Kiwi buyers — campers, tradies, and anyone prepping for outages — the portable power station lineup is the relevant bit. It's built around LiFePO4 (lithium iron phosphate) cells, which last longer and handle heat better than the older lithium-ion packs used in cheaper competitors.

The capacity tiers, in plain terms

Anker sells these largely by watt-hours (Wh, how much energy is stored) and watts (W, how fast it can push that energy out). A rough guide for NZ buyers:

  • Under 300Wh (SOLIX C300 series): runs a phone, laptop, and small appliances for a day. Good for weekend camping or as a desk backup. NZ pricing for the C300 DC and C300 sits around $379–$499, often discounted from $479–$599 RRP.
  • 1,000Wh class (SOLIX C1000): the sweet spot for most households — enough to run a fridge for several hours, charge power tools, or keep a CPAP machine going overnight. Listed around $1,499 in NZ, down from $1,599.
  • 2,000Wh+ class (SOLIX F2000 and up): genuine blackout-backup territory, capable of running multiple appliances at once. NZ pricing lands around $2,799.

Anker also sells larger SOLIX F3800-class units and home battery systems for whole-house backup, but those are a different purchase decision entirely — more like a solar installation than an electronics buy — so we're not covering them here.

Solar charging: does it actually work in NZ?

Every SOLIX power station accepts solar input via Anker's own folding panels (sold separately, typically 100W–200W per panel). Realistically, a 200W panel in good NZ sun will take several hours to meaningfully top up a 1,000Wh unit — solar charging is a slow-and-steady backup, not a quick top-up. It's most useful for multi-day off-grid trips or extended power cuts, where you can leave a panel out during daylight and trickle-charge over a few days. For a weekend away, charging from your car or a wall outlet before you leave is far more practical.

Who should actually buy one

The C300-class units make sense for casual campers, photographers needing to charge drone batteries and laptops in the field, or as an emergency phone/laptop backup during a storm-related outage — a common enough occurrence in NZ regions prone to wind and flooding events. The C1000 and above are worth it if you want genuine peace of mind during extended outages, run a home office that can't afford downtime, or want to power camping fridges and induction cooktops away from mains power.

If you just need to keep a phone charged, a $50 power bank does the job for a fraction of the price — SOLIX units are for when you need to run actual appliances.

Where to buy in NZ

Anker sells directly through its NZ storefront (ankersolix.co.nz and anker.com/nz), and stock also shows up through specialist retailers like Portable Power NZ, GearUp NZ, and Actiontech. Prices fluctuate with sales fairly regularly — Anker runs frequent promotions — so it's worth comparing listings rather than buying the first one you see.

What to check before buying

  • Output ports: confirm it has enough AC outlets and USB-C PD ports for what you actually plan to run simultaneously.
  • Warranty: most SOLIX units carry 3–5 year warranties in NZ — worth confirming with the specific retailer, as terms can vary from the direct Anker store.
  • Weight: the C1000-class units weigh around 13kg — fine for car camping, less fun if you're carrying it any distance.
