Dyson V15 vs Gen5detect

Same brand, one generation apart, and a genuinely tricky price gap. Here is what actually changed and whether it is worth paying flagship money for.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

The question everyone in NZ is actually asking

You are not choosing between two different brands here, you are choosing between two generations of the same vacuum, and Dyson has not made that decision easy. The V15 Detect has been around long enough that it regularly gets discounted below $900, while the Gen5detect sits as Dyson's current flagship at a genuinely steep price. So the real question is not which one is better, it is whether the Gen5detect's improvements are worth paying full flagship money for when the older model still does the job brilliantly.

We will say this up front: this is one of the closer generational jumps Dyson has made in years. Unlike the leap from the V11 to the V15 (which added the laser dust detector and the LCD screen), the V15 to Gen5detect jump is mostly under the hood. It is a real upgrade, but it is an incremental one, not a reason to feel bad about buying the older model.

Suction power: bigger number, smaller real-world gap

On paper, the Gen5detect wins clearly: 280 air watts versus the V15's 240AW. That is roughly a 17 percent bump, and Dyson has genuinely improved the motor design (a new generation of their digital motor spins faster and more efficiently) rather than just cranking the power draw. In everyday testing though, both vacuums ace suction tests and pick up embedded grit from carpet with ease on their higher power settings. The extra headroom on the Gen5detect shows up mostly in Boost mode runtime and in slightly better performance on thick, deep-pile carpet - not in a night-and-day difference on hard floors or low-pile carpet, which is what most Kiwi homes actually have.

Dyson V15 Detect - the value pick

NZ price: typically $850 to $900, often discounted further during sales.

240AW max suction, laser dust detection on the Fluffy head, the LCD screen showing particle counts, and around 60 minutes of runtime on Eco mode (much less on Boost). This is still a genuinely excellent vacuum - it was Dyson's flagship for years for good reason, and nothing about it has aged badly. If you already own one, there is no compelling reason to upgrade.

Dyson Gen5detect - the flagship

NZ price: typically $1,300 to $1,500 depending on the bundle (some come with extra tools or the wall dock).

280AW max suction, a finer 0.1-micron HEPA filtration seal (versus 0.3 micron on the V15 - genuinely meaningful if you have allergies or asthma in the house), up to 70 minutes of runtime thanks to a new 10-cell battery, and a slightly refined dust detection system that also displays particle size on screen. It is about 90 grams heavier than the V15, which you will notice on longer cleaning sessions or if you are cleaning stairs a lot.

Battery and runtime: the Gen5detect actually pulls ahead here

This is where the newer model earns its keep. The Gen5detect's 10-cell battery genuinely extends usable runtime, and more importantly, Dyson tuned the power delivery to fade less as the battery drains - a common complaint with older cordless vacuums is that suction visibly drops in the last 20 percent of battery. If you have a larger home (three-plus bedrooms, open-plan living) and you are doing a whole-house clean in one session without stopping to recharge, the Gen5detect's extra 10 minutes matters more than it sounds like on paper.

Filtration: worth paying for if anyone in your house has allergies

The 0.1-micron seal on the Gen5detect versus 0.3 micron on the V15 is a genuine, not marketing-fluff, difference. Both are HEPA-sealed and will noticeably reduce dust and allergens compared to a bagged vacuum with a leaky seal, but if hay fever, asthma, or a shedding cat are a daily issue in your house, the Gen5detect's finer filtration is the single strongest argument for spending the extra money.

Weight, ergonomics and bin capacity

Both vacuums share the same basic ergonomics - trigger-operated power, the same wand and head attachment system, similar bin-empty mechanism. The Gen5detect is about 90g heavier in the hand, which will not bother most people but is worth knowing if you already find the V15 tiring on extended sessions or on stairs. Bin capacity is essentially identical between the two (0.76 litres), so neither one solves the empty-it-every-session annoyance that all Dyson cordless vacuums share compared to bagged uprights.

Debunking the suction-watts myth

It is tempting to assume the Gen5detect must clean noticeably better because 280AW beats 240AW on a spec sheet, but air watts measure the vacuum motor's raw power at the wand, not the cleaning performance you get at the floor. Brush head design, seal quality against the floor, and airflow path all matter as much or more than the peak number. Independent testing repeatedly finds both vacuums pick up debris at essentially the same rate on hard floors and short-pile carpet - the extra suction on the Gen5detect shows up mainly as more headroom on thick carpet and slightly better runtime under load, not a transformed cleaning experience.

Verdict: who should buy which

  • Buy the V15 Detect if: you want Dyson-level cleaning without flagship pricing, your home is mostly hard floors or low-pile carpet, and nobody in the house has serious allergies. At $850 to $900 it remains one of the best value cordless vacuums on the market, full stop.
  • Buy the Gen5detect if: you have allergies or asthma in the household and want the finer filtration, you have a larger home and want the extra runtime, or you have thick carpet throughout and want the additional suction headroom.
  • Already own a V15? Do not upgrade. The gap is real but incremental, and your money is better spent elsewhere.
Worth knowing: Dyson regularly discounts the V15 Detect once a newer model launches, and refurbished/ex-demo units through NZ retailers can bring it well under $800. That price gap versus the Gen5detect only gets wider over time, so if budget is the deciding factor, patience pays off.
