Price history tracking for NZ retailers: what's actually available

CamelCamelCamel is a well-known tool for tracking Amazon price history, but it doesn't cover PB Tech, Noel Leeming, JB Hi-Fi or Harvey Norman. Here's what NZ shoppers actually have to work with.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Why the Amazon tools don't help here

CamelCamelCamel and similar tools built their entire model around Amazon's product catalogue and public pricing API access. That works because Amazon is a single, enormous, consistently structured marketplace. New Zealand's electronics market is the opposite — spread across half a dozen independent retailers, each with their own site structure, promotional calendar, and pricing behaviour. There's no single dominant marketplace to build a tool around, so a direct NZ equivalent of CamelCamelCamel was never going to look the same.

Some Kiwi shoppers do use Amazon Australia or Amazon US for niche items and can use CamelCamelCamel for those specific purchases. But for the bulk of everyday electronics buying — TVs, laptops, appliances, phones — the useful price history has to come from the NZ retailers themselves.

What price history actually looks like for NZ retailers

Rather than one universal price-history graph like Amazon shoppers get, NZ price tracking generally works by monitoring each retailer's product pages directly and building a history from repeated checks over time. This is exactly the approach comparison sites like Twisti use — pulling current pricing across multiple retailers on the same product so you can see not just today's price but how it's moved recently.

Amazon-style tracking vs multi-retailer NZ tracking

Amazon-style tools answer one question: "is this cheaper than it's been before, at this one store?" A multi-retailer NZ comparison answers a more useful question for most Kiwi shoppers: "who is cheapest right now, and how does that compare to recent prices across all the stores that sell it?" The second question matters more here because the cheapest seller for a given item changes retailer far more often than it does on Amazon.

Building your own price awareness

Until an NZ retailer opens up the kind of public pricing data Amazon does, the practical approach is a combination of tools:

  • Use a comparison site to see current pricing across NZ retailers side by side, rather than checking each store individually.
  • Set alerts on specific products so you're notified of changes rather than needing to check manually.
  • Pay attention to recurring sale periods — Boxing Day, Black Friday, and end-of-financial-year runs — since NZ retailers are fairly consistent about when they discount, even without public price history data to prove it.
Caveat: No NZ tool currently offers the multi-year historical price charts CamelCamelCamel provides for Amazon. Price tracking here is generally more recent-window (weeks to a few months) rather than years of history, so treat any "lowest ever" claim with a healthy amount of scepticism unless the source shows its data.

The bottom line

There's no direct NZ clone of CamelCamelCamel, and there probably won't be one soon, because the market structure is fundamentally different. What NZ shoppers have instead is multi-retailer price comparison, which for most buying decisions is actually the more useful tool — it tells you who to buy from today, not just whether today is historically cheap.
