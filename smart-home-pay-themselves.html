Smart Home Devices That Pay for Themselves

Not every smart home gadget saves money — some are pure convenience. Here's which categories genuinely earn back their cost, and roughly how.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Devices with a real payback

  • Smart plugs: cheap, and directly cut standby power on whatever's plugged into them — one of the fastest categories to pay for itself.
  • Smart thermostats: scheduling heating around when you're actually home, rather than running constantly, is the single biggest lever in most households' power bills.
  • Whole-home energy monitors: don't save power directly, but consistently reveal which appliances are actually driving the bill — informing decisions that do save money.
  • Smart LED bulbs with scheduling: LEDs alone already cut lighting costs versus older bulbs; scheduling and motion-based automation trims further waste from lights left on in empty rooms.
  • Smart sprinkler/irrigation controllers: for anyone with a garden, weather-aware watering schedules avoid the classic waste of sprinklers running during or right after rain.

Devices that are more about convenience

Video doorbells, smart speakers, and robot vacuums are genuinely useful, but their value is mostly time, security, and convenience rather than a direct reduction in ongoing bills. Worth buying for what they actually offer, rather than expecting them to pay for themselves the way a thermostat or smart plug does.

The general rule: devices that reduce how long something runs unnecessarily (heating, standby power, watering) tend to have the clearest payback. Devices that add a new capability rather than automating an existing waste are worth buying for the capability itself, not as a savings plan.
