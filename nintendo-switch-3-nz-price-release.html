What comes after Switch 2? Early rumours, sorted from real to speculative

Nintendo Switch 2 is already out and on shelves in NZ. Talk of what Nintendo does next is inevitable, but almost none of it is confirmed — here's what to actually pay attention to.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Let's be clear about where things stand

Nintendo Switch 2 launched in 2025 and is the current-generation console. There is no confirmed "Switch 3" or successor console — no announced name, no confirmed specs, no release window. Any headline suggesting otherwise is getting ahead of what Nintendo has actually said. This article looks at the kind of early, informal speculation that inevitably starts circulating once a console has been out for a while, not at any real product announcement.

Why speculation starts this early

Nintendo's console generations have historically run for several years each, and the gaming press tends to start floating "what's next" pieces well before there's anything substantive to report, partly because patent filings, hardware supplier chatter, and executive interviews get parsed for hints. None of that constitutes a confirmed roadmap — it's pattern-reading, and it's often wrong on specifics.

For Switch 2 specifically, given it only recently launched, genuine successor rumours this early in its lifecycle should be treated with extra scepticism. Consoles typically get several years of new game releases and possible hardware revisions (like a lighter or OLED variant) before a true successor arrives.

Hardware revision vs. full successor — worth distinguishing

Nintendo has a track record of releasing revised versions of existing hardware (different screen tech, size, or battery life) partway through a console's life, separate from launching an entirely new generation. If you see rumours right now, they're far more likely to eventually describe a Switch 2 revision than a brand new console, simply based on how recently Switch 2 launched.

What we'd actually watch for

  • Official statements from Nintendo at investor briefings or press events — the only genuinely reliable source
  • Confirmed game release slates, which indicate how long Nintendo expects the current console to remain central
  • Credible reporting that clearly separates "sources say" from "Nintendo confirmed"

Anything claiming specific NZ pricing or a release date for a next-generation console at this point should be treated as entirely speculative, since Nintendo hasn't even signalled a timeline publicly.

Our take: With Switch 2 still early in its life, meaningful successor news is very unlikely in the near term. If you're deciding whether to buy a Switch 2 now, base that decision on the current console's merits and pricing, not on rumours of what might replace it years from now.

Should you wait to buy?

If you're after a current-gen Nintendo console, waiting for an unconfirmed, unannounced successor makes little sense given how early Switch 2 is in its cycle. It's more useful to track actual price movements on Switch 2 itself — consoles do sometimes see price adjustments or bundle changes as the range matures — than to wait on speculation with no confirmed timeline.
