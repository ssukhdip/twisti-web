Dumbphones NZ: Are They Worth Buying in 2026?

A small but genuine trend: people deliberately swapping their smartphone for something closer to a 2005-era phone. Here's what devices like the Light Phone actually do, what you give up, and whether it's realistic in NZ.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Why people are downgrading on purpose

The pitch behind dumbphones like the Light Phone II/III, Punkt MP02, and even Nokia's revived button phones is simple: a phone that makes calls and sends texts, and deliberately does very little else. No infinite scroll, no notification firehose, no app store rabbit hole. For people who've found their smartphone usage has become compulsive rather than useful, the appeal isn't nostalgia — it's removing the temptation entirely rather than relying on willpower or screen-time limits that are easy to override.

It's a genuinely different approach to digital wellbeing than the usual "just use Focus Mode" advice, because it makes distraction physically impossible rather than one tap away.

What you actually give up

No or extremely limited maps navigation, no banking apps, no camera worth the name on most models, and often no way to receive photo messages properly. Two-factor authentication apps and some delivery/rideshare services that assume a smartphone can become genuinely awkward. This isn't a minor inconvenience for most people in 2026 — it's a real functional trade-off, not just "less social media."

Who this genuinely suits

  • Weekend/holiday use: the most realistic entry point — carry a dumbphone away from work and keep the smartphone at home, rather than switching entirely.
  • Teens' first phone: a genuinely popular use case for parents wanting calls/texts without full internet access from day one.
  • Compulsive scrollers who've tried everything else: if app limits and grayscale mode haven't worked, removing the capability entirely is a more drastic but more effective option.
  • Not: anyone who relies on their phone for work email, 2FA, or navigation day-to-day — the friction there is real, not exaggerated marketing copy.
NZ note: Most dumbphones are imported directly from the manufacturer rather than stocked by local retailers, so factor in shipping time and GST on import when comparing the sticker price to a mainstream phone.

The middle-ground option

If going fully dumbphone feels too extreme, several Android launchers (Before Launcher, Indie) and even some phone manufacturers now offer a genuine minimal home-screen mode that hides most apps behind a plain list. It's not as effective as a hardware constraint, but it costs nothing to try before spending money on a second device.
