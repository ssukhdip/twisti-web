Nanoleaf NZ Buying Guide

Nanoleaf turned decorative lighting into a genuine smart home category, and its NZ range now covers everything from gaming-room panels to plain bulbs.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
How we picked these: We compared current NZ pricing at Noel Leeming, JB Hi-Fi, and Mighty Ape, and weighed installation effort against visual impact for each product line.

What Nanoleaf actually sells

It's easy to think of Nanoleaf as just the triangular wall panels, but the NZ range now spans light strips, smart bulbs, and desk-lamp style Lines panels too. Nanoleaf is stocked through Noel Leeming, JB Hi-Fi, and Mighty Ape, with the widest colour and starter-kit selection typically found online rather than in-store.

Shapes and Elements panels: the statement wall

The Nanoleaf Shapes Triangles starter kit (around $279 NZD for 9 panels at JB Hi-Fi) remains the go-to for a feature wall or gaming setup, with full RGB colour and music/game sync. If you want a more subdued, warm-wood look rather than glowing colour panels, the Elements Hexagons kit (around $329 NZD) uses a wood-textured frame with warm white lighting only — better suited to a living room than a gaming room.

Lines: for a cleaner, more minimal look

The Nanoleaf Lines starter kit (around $259 NZD at Noel Leeming) trades the geometric panel shapes for slim light bars that can be arranged in more architectural patterns. This is the pick for anyone who found the triangle panels too busy but still wants full-colour ambient lighting.

Essentials bulbs and strips: the budget entry

Not every room needs a feature wall. The Nanoleaf Essentials A19 bulb (around $39 NZD each) or a twin-pack (around $69 NZD) gives Matter-compatible colour lighting for a bedside lamp or hallway fitting without any panel installation. The Essentials Lightstrip (around $89 NZD for 2m) works well behind a TV or under kitchen cabinetry.

Skylight for ceiling installs

The newer Skylight panels (from around $219 NZD per starter set) are designed for flush ceiling mounting rather than a wall, aimed at new-build homes or renovations where an electrician is already running cabling.

Matching product to room

  • Gaming room or feature wall: Shapes Triangles or Lines, for music/game sync effects.
  • Living room, subtler look: Elements Hexagons for the wood-frame warm lighting.
  • Bedroom or rental (no wall mounting): Essentials bulbs, fully reversible.
  • Kitchen or TV backlighting: Essentials Lightstrip.

Matter and HomeKit compatibility

Most current Nanoleaf products support Matter, meaning they'll pair with Apple Home, Google Home, or Amazon Alexa without a separate Nanoleaf-only hub. Older Aurora-era panels (pre-2021) do not support Matter and require the legacy Nanoleaf app, worth checking if buying second-hand.

Expansion pack costs add up

Starter kits only cover a small area — expansion packs for Shapes Triangles run around $59 NZD for 3 extra panels. Budget for at least one expansion pack if you're covering a full wall rather than a small accent area.

Where to buy in NZ

JB Hi-Fi and Noel Leeming both run regular Nanoleaf bundle discounts, particularly around gaming-hardware sales periods. Mighty Ape is worth checking for the Essentials bulb range specifically, where pricing is often a few dollars lower.
