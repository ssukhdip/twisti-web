Sonos Era NZ: Price, Models & Where to Buy

Sonos Era speakers replaced the long-running One and Five lines. Here's how the models differ, what they typically cost in NZ, and where to shop.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Era 100 vs Era 300: what's different

The Sonos Era range generally comes in two sizes. The smaller Era 100 is a compact, single-room speaker aimed at replacing the old Sonos One — good for a bedroom, kitchen, or office. The larger Era 300 is built around spatial audio with multiple angled drivers, positioned as a premium option for home theatre setups and Dolby Atmos Music playback. The 300 is typically priced well above the 100, reflecting the added drivers and processing power.

Key features across the range

  • Wi-Fi and Bluetooth connectivity on both models, useful for quick source switching
  • Trueplay tuning, which adjusts sound output to your room's acoustics
  • Support for major streaming services through the Sonos app
  • Stereo pairing and surround-sound integration with Sonos soundbars

Both models can be used standalone or as part of a wider Sonos multi-room system, which is one of the platform's biggest draws for buyers already invested in Sonos gear.

Where to buy Sonos Era in NZ

Sonos products are typically stocked by PB Tech, Noel Leeming, JB Hi-Fi, Harvey Norman, and specialist hi-fi retailers, as well as Sonos's own NZ online store. Because Sonos maintains fairly consistent recommended retail pricing, the biggest savings usually come from sales periods (Black Friday, Boxing Day) rather than everyday price differences between retailers — though it's still worth comparing before buying.

Do you need Era 300's spatial audio?

If you mainly listen to podcasts, radio, or standard stereo music, the Era 100 covers the core use case well at a lower price. The Era 300's spatial audio processing is most noticeable with music mixed specifically for Dolby Atmos or similar formats — if your listening library isn't in that format, you may not hear the full benefit of the extra cost.

Building a multi-room setup

One of the main reasons buyers choose Sonos over cheaper alternatives is the ecosystem — Era speakers can be grouped with other Sonos products (soundbars, subwoofers, older One/Five speakers) into synced multi-room audio, controlled from a single app. If you're starting from scratch, it's often more cost-effective to begin with one or two Era speakers and expand later rather than buying a full system upfront.

Before you buy: Check current Sonos app compatibility and firmware requirements if you're adding an Era speaker to an older existing Sonos system — very old legacy products have occasionally had integration limitations.

Getting the best NZ price

Sonos gear rarely sees dramatic day-to-day price swings between NZ retailers, but bundle deals (speaker plus soundbar, or twin-pack stereo pairs) can offer meaningful savings over buying separately. Comparing current listings before purchase is still the simplest way to make sure you're not missing a better bundle elsewhere.
