Smart Blinds Explained

You don't need to replace your curtains to automate them. Here's the difference between retrofit motors and full replacement blinds, with real NZ pricing.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
Two different products, same shelf: "Smart blinds" covers both complete replacement roller blinds with a motor built in, and small retrofit motors that clip onto curtain rails or blind chains you already own. They solve the same problem at very different price points.

Retrofit: motorising what you already have

SwitchBot Curtain 3 is the best-known retrofit option — a small motor that clips onto an existing curtain rail and drives your curtains open and closed on a schedule or via app/voice. In NZ it's sold through Mighty Ape, with the rod version listed around NZ$179, plus SwitchBot's Hub 2 (roughly $69–90) if you want voice control or off-network scheduling. It needs a compatible rail (round or U-rail) and works with existing fabric curtains rather than roller blinds.

Aqara's Roller Shade Driver E1 takes a similar approach but for chain-driven roller blinds and vertical blinds — it clips onto the existing bead chain and turns it automatically, run through an Aqara hub, typically the Hub M2 (around NZ$84–120 depending on retailer/sale). This is generally cheaper than replacing blinds entirely if you already have a decent chain-driven blind.

Full replacement: IKEA Fyrtur and similar

IKEA's Fyrtur is a complete blackout roller blind with the motor built into the roller tube, sold in NZ through IKEA's own store and website. It requires IKEA's DIRIGERA hub (around NZ$100–130) to schedule and voice-control it, though it can run standalone with the included remote without a hub. Because it replaces the blind itself rather than motorising an old one, it's the tidier-looking option, but only makes sense if you're due to replace blinds anyway or want blackout specifically.

Real NZ pricing snapshot

  • SwitchBot Curtain 3 — around $179 per rod motor (Mighty Ape), plus optional Hub 2.
  • Aqara Roller Shade Driver E1 — roughly $80–150 per motor, requires an Aqara hub.
  • IKEA Fyrtur — blind + motor bundled, typically $150–230 per window depending on size, plus DIRIGERA hub if you want app/voice control.

What actually matters when choosing

If you like your current curtains or blinds and just want them automated, retrofit (SwitchBot or Aqara) is cheaper and non-destructive. If you're renovating or replacing blinds anyway, and want blackout for a bedroom, Fyrtur-style replacement blinds are tidier. Check the rail/chain type before ordering — round rails, U-rails, and different bead-chain sizes are not universally compatible, and returns on made-to-measure blinds can be limited.

Battery life and maintenance

Both SwitchBot Curtain and Fyrtur use rechargeable built-in batteries rather than disposables — SwitchBot rates its Curtain 3 battery at around 8 months per charge under typical twice-daily use, and Fyrtur's battery is commonly reported to last 6 months or more between charges. Aqara's E1 driver runs on 4x AA batteries lasting roughly a year with normal use.
