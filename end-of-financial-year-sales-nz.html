End of financial year sales NZ: what actually goes on sale

Every March, "EOFY sale" banners go up across every major NZ retailer. Some of it is genuinely worth buying into. A fair chunk of it is business-as-usual pricing with a new sticker.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Why EOFY sales exist

New Zealand's tax year runs to 31 March for most businesses, and retailers use the lead-up to clear stock off the books before the new financial year starts. That's a genuine business incentive — inventory sitting on shelves is money tied up, and there's real pressure to move it before the year closes. This is different from Black Friday or Boxing Day, which are consumer-driven shopping events; EOFY is more of an accounting-driven clearance push, which is why the discounting behaviour can look a bit different.

What genuinely tends to discount

Categories where retailers are trying to clear older stock ahead of new model arrivals tend to see the most genuine movement — think last season's TV lineup, older laptop models before a new chip generation lands, and general whiteware or appliances where a showroom needs floor space. Business-focused retailers, in particular, often run genuine EOFY promotions on computers, monitors and office gear, partly aimed at business customers doing end-of-year equipment purchases for tax reasons.

  • Previous-generation laptops and desktops, especially just before new chip releases
  • TVs and appliances nearing the end of a model cycle
  • Office and business-oriented electronics (monitors, printers, accessories)

What tends to be less genuine

Not everything under an EOFY banner is actually discounted from its recent average price. It's common for some "sale" pricing to be set at, or barely below, a price the item has sat at for weeks anyway — a practice sometimes called "was/now" pricing where the "was" price wasn't really representative. Newly released or high-demand products rarely see meaningful EOFY discounts, since there's little inventory pressure to move them.

Genuine EOFY clearance vs. sale-branded business as usual

A genuine EOFY deal shows a real drop against that product's typical price over the past few months — check a price history chart to confirm. A sale-branded item with a flat price history for the last quarter is just wearing a EOFY sticker; the "was" price may be inflated or simply the everyday price.

How to actually verify a deal

The only reliable way to know if an EOFY deal is real is to check the product's price history over the preceding months, not just trust the percentage-off badge on the page. A genuine discount shows as a real dip below the recent average; a fake one shows a flat line with a new label slapped on top.

Worth knowing: Retailers aren't obligated to run EOFY sales every year, and the scale of discounting varies by category and by how much stock a retailer is actually trying to shift. Don't assume every category will have deals — check current pricing rather than assuming based on past years.

The bottom line

EOFY sales are worth watching, particularly for laptops, computers and end-of-cycle appliances, but they're not uniformly good value across every category. Check the actual price history before treating an EOFY badge as proof of a genuine discount.
