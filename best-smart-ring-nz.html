Best Smart Ring NZ: Which One to Buy

Oura Ring, Samsung Galaxy Ring and Ultrahuman all track sleep and recovery, but they differ hugely on subscription costs, sizing, and battery life. Here's what actually matters when picking one.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Sizing matters more than with a watch

Unlike a smartwatch strap you can adjust yourself, a smart ring only works properly if it fits snugly without being tight — too loose and the sensors lose contact with skin and give inaccurate readings; too tight and it's uncomfortable for all-day, all-night wear. All three brands sell sizing kits (plastic dummy rings you wear for a few days before your real ring ships) and it's genuinely worth using them rather than guessing based on your normal ring size, because smart rings are bulkier than a standard band and often fit differently. Finger size also fluctuates through the day with temperature and hydration, so sizing kits usually recommend checking fit at a few different times, not just once in the morning.

The subscription question

This is the biggest practical difference between these three rings and worth understanding before you buy. Oura requires an ongoing app subscription to access most of its detailed insights (sleep stages, readiness scores, trends over time) — without it, the ring itself still collects data but the app locks a lot of that analysis behind the monthly fee. Samsung's Galaxy Ring, by contrast, doesn't require a separate subscription for its core tracking and integrates into the free Samsung Health app, though it works best paired with a Samsung phone. Ultrahuman has generally positioned itself as subscription-free as well, giving you full access to its metrics without an ongoing fee. If the idea of paying monthly indefinitely for a ring you already bought outright bothers you, that alone might decide this for you.

Quick comparison

  • Oura Ring: Most mature sleep/readiness algorithms and largest research backing, but requires an ongoing app subscription for full features.
  • Samsung Galaxy Ring: No separate subscription, works with any phone but is genuinely best paired with a Samsung device for full feature access.
  • Ultrahuman Ring: Subscription-free, strong metabolic and glucose-adjacent tracking options (with add-on hardware), a slightly less polished app than Oura's.
  • Battery life: All three typically run several days to about a week per charge, depending on ring size and how much sensing is active.
  • Charging: All use small proprietary charging cases or docks — losing the charger is a genuine inconvenience since replacements aren't always cheap or quick to source in NZ.

Which tracking features actually matter day to day

Sleep staging (light, deep, REM) and a simple daily readiness or recovery score are the features people actually check and act on — deciding whether to push a workout or take it easy, or noticing a pattern of poor sleep after certain habits. Continuous heart rate and heart rate variability trends over weeks matter more than any single night's number. Skin temperature tracking is genuinely useful for spotting oncoming illness or cycle-related patterns a few days before you'd otherwise notice. Step counting and activity rings, on the other hand, are handled just as well by a phone or a cheap fitness band — it's not a reason to choose a ring specifically. If you're buying mainly for sleep and recovery insight, any of the three will do the job; the subscription model and how well it plays with your existing phone should be the deciding factors, not marginal sensor differences.

Worth noting: smart rings are metal or resin and can trigger metal detectors, and some models aren't rated for high-impact contact sports — check the manufacturer's activity guidance if that's part of your routine.

Bottom line

If you want the most established sleep and readiness data and don't mind an ongoing subscription, Oura remains the benchmark. If you're already in the Samsung ecosystem and want to avoid a subscription, Galaxy Ring is the natural choice. If you want subscription-free tracking regardless of phone brand, Ultrahuman is worth a serious look. Try the sizing kit before committing either way — a ring that doesn't fit well undermines all of this regardless of which brand you pick.
