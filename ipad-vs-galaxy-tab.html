iPad vs Galaxy Tab

Samsung throws in the stylus for free and has the better screen at the base tier - but the software gap is still the thing that should decide this one.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

What we're actually comparing

Apple's 2026 iPad range spans the base iPad (from around $649), iPad Air with M4 (from around $1,249), and iPad Pro with M5 (from around $1,749 for 11-inch, up past $2,800 for a well-specced 13-inch). Samsung's answer is the Galaxy Tab S11 series - Galaxy Tab S11 (from around $1,399) and Galaxy Tab S11 Ultra (from around $1,899 up to $2,400-plus for higher storage), both including an S Pen in the box. Tablets are a more decisive purchase-intent category than phones - most people buying one already know roughly why (kids' device, drawing tablet, laptop replacement, media consumption), so the right answer here depends heavily on which of those you actually are.

Software: the single biggest difference in this comparison

iPadOS still feels more like a tablet OS, Android still feels like a stretched phone OS

This is the one area where the two platforms haven't converged much despite years of updates on both sides. iPadOS 19's windowing, stage manager, and multitasking has matured into something genuinely usable for real work, and the App Store's tablet-optimised app selection remains meaningfully deeper than Android's - plenty of major productivity and creative apps still ship an iPad-first or iPad-only version. Samsung's One UI for Tablet has improved a lot (DeX mode gives you a genuine desktop-like layout when you want it), but the underlying issue persists: a large share of Android apps are still just stretched phone layouts, not built for a tablet screen. If tablet-native app quality matters to your use case - this is especially true for drawing, video editing, and note-taking apps - iPadOS remains ahead.

The stylus experience

Apple Pencil Pro vs the S Pen

Samsung includes the S Pen in the box on every Galaxy Tab S11 model - Apple sells the Apple Pencil Pro separately (around $219 extra on iPad Air and Pro; the base iPad only supports the cheaper, less capable USB-C Pencil). That's a real cost difference worth factoring into your budget. On raw feel, both styluses are excellent with low latency and good palm rejection. The software behind them differs: Apple Pencil's hover and squeeze gestures are tightly integrated into a smaller number of polished first-party and pro creative apps (Procreate remains the standout reason many illustrators pick iPad specifically). The S Pen's air-gesture and note-taking features (Samsung Notes, screen-off memo) are more feature-packed out of the box, useful if you want a stylus that does more without buying separate apps.

Build quality and display

Closer than people expect

Samsung's Dynamic AMOLED 2X panels on the Galaxy Tab S11 and S11 Ultra are objectively excellent - better contrast and deeper blacks than the iPad Air's Liquid Retina LCD panel, and on par with or slightly ahead of the iPad Pro's tandem OLED in some lighting conditions. Both companies use armour-grade aluminium frames and both lines carry IP68 ratings on the higher-end models now, which wasn't true even two years ago (tablets used to lag behind phones on water resistance). If display quality alone is your deciding factor, Samsung's AMOLED technology gives it a genuine edge on the base and mid-tier models - Apple only matches that with the pricier OLED iPad Pro.

Ecosystem and multi-device use

This decision usually isn't actually about the tablet

If you own a MacBook and an iPhone, an iPad slots in with Universal Control, Handoff, and Sidecar (using the iPad as a second Mac display) in ways that just work with zero setup. If you're on Windows and Samsung phones, the Galaxy Tab S11 pairs into Samsung's Multi Control and Second Screen features similarly well, plus Samsung DeX for a genuine desktop-style workspace when you connect a keyboard. Buying a tablet from a different ecosystem than your phone and laptop is not a disaster, but you lose most of the cross-device conveniences that make either tablet feel like more than a big screen - this is the single biggest factor to weigh, more than any spec on the sheet.

AI features

Less relevant here than on phones, honestly

Apple Intelligence is available on iPad Air (M4) and iPad Pro (M5), covering writing tools, Image Playground, and Genmoji - genuinely handy, but the base iPad still lacks the RAM to run the full Apple Intelligence feature set, which is worth checking before you buy the cheapest model expecting the same AI features shown in Apple's marketing. Samsung's Galaxy AI on the Tab S11 series includes Circle to Search, note summarisation, and translation, powered by Gemini the same way it is on Samsung phones. On a tablet, most people's actual AI usage is lighter than on a phone - searching, summarising documents, translating - and both platforms handle that adequately. Neither company's tablet AI story should be a major factor in your decision.

Common misconceptions worth debunking

  • A tablet with a keyboard case replaces a laptop. For document editing, email, and browsing, sure. For anything requiring proper multi-window professional software, file management, or peripheral support, both iPadOS and Android tablet software still fall short of a real laptop - this hasn't fundamentally changed despite years of this-is-basically-a-laptop-now marketing from both companies.
  • The S Pen being included makes the Galaxy Tab better value automatically. It's a genuine cost advantage (roughly $200+ NZD saved versus buying an Apple Pencil Pro separately), but only if you'll actually use a stylus regularly - factor it in as a real saving, not just a bonus.
  • Bigger screen numbers mean a better tablet for everyone. The Galaxy Tab S11 Ultra's 14.6-inch screen and iPad Pro 13-inch are genuinely excellent for split-screen work and drawing, but they're heavier and less comfortable for one-handed reading or couch use than the 11-inch options - match screen size to how you'll actually hold it, not just what looks impressive in a spec comparison.
  • iPads don't get real multitasking. This was a fair criticism years ago; iPadOS's current Stage Manager and windowing genuinely support proper multi-app workflows now, though it took Apple longer to get there than most people expected.
Worth knowing: the base iPad (non-Air, non-Pro) is the single best value tablet on this list for casual use - Netflix, browsing, kids' apps - at around $649, and buying an Air or Pro tier just in case for a casual user is money most people don't need to spend.

The verdict

Buy an iPad if...

  • You're already a Mac and iPhone household and want cross-device features to just work.
  • You draw or do creative work - Procreate and the wider iPad-first creative app catalogue is still the deeper library.
  • You want the base model for genuinely casual use and don't want to pay for features you won't touch.
  • Long-term software support matters - Apple's tablets get updates for many years after release.

Buy a Samsung Galaxy Tab S11 if...

  • You want the S Pen included without an extra purchase.
  • You want the best display technology at the base tier - Samsung's AMOLED beats Apple's entry-level LCD screens.
  • You're a Windows and Samsung phone household wanting matching cross-device features.
  • DeX's desktop-style mode genuinely appeals to you as a lightweight laptop substitute.

Our honest take: for pure creative and productivity work, the iPad's app ecosystem still gives it the edge, and it's the safer buy if you're inside Apple's ecosystem already. But the Galaxy Tab S11 is the better value proposition specifically because of the included S Pen and its excellent AMOLED display, and it's a genuinely strong pick for anyone already on Android or Windows who wants a proper tablet rather than a stretched-out phone.
