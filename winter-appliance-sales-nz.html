Winter appliance sales NZ: heaters, heat pumps and dehumidifiers

Waiting for winter to buy a heater is a bit like waiting for summer to buy an air conditioner — you're shopping at peak demand, not peak discount. Here's what actually happens to pricing on these categories.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Why winter isn't the cheapest time to buy a heater

Heating appliances — heaters, heat pumps, electric blankets, dehumidifiers — follow a predictable seasonal demand curve in NZ, and retailers price accordingly. When everyone needs a heater at the same time (typically once the first real cold snap hits, from May through July), retailers have less reason to discount, because the item is going to sell anyway. This is the same dynamic that keeps air conditioner prices firmer in summer.

The flip side is that the off-season — spring and early autumn — is often when these categories see genuine clearance-style discounting, since retailers are moving stock ahead of a quiet demand period rather than racing to capture buyers who need something today.

Heat pumps specifically

Heat pumps are a bigger purchase with a longer sales cycle than a portable heater, and they often involve installation booked separately from the unit purchase. Because installers get genuinely busy during the cold months, even if you find a good price on the unit itself in winter, you may face longer wait times for installation. Shoulder seasons (autumn and spring) tend to offer both better pricing and more installer availability.

Buying in winter vs buying in the off-season

Buying a heater or heat pump mid-winter gets you the item fast, but usually at closer to full price with less installer flexibility. Buying in autumn or spring, ahead of the season, generally gets you better pricing and more room to book installation on your schedule — the trade-off is you need to plan ahead rather than reacting to the first cold night.

Dehumidifiers

Dehumidifiers see solid demand through NZ's damp winter months, particularly in southern and colder regions, but they don't have quite the same "need it tonight" urgency as a heater during a cold snap — which means there's a bit more pricing flexibility even in-season. Watch for post-winter clearance too, as retailers make room for spring/summer appliance stock.

Caveat: If your heater breaks mid-winter and you need a replacement immediately, don't wait for a hypothetical better price later in the season — a working heater now is worth more than a marginal saving in three months. Seasonal timing advice applies to planned purchases, not emergency replacements.

The practical approach

If you can plan ahead, buying heating appliances in autumn (before the cold sets in and demand spikes) or comparing pricing right at the tail end of winter as retailers start clearing stock for spring tends to beat buying at the peak of a cold snap. If you need something now because of genuine need, prioritise having a working appliance over chasing the theoretical best price.
