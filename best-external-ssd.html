BUYING GUIDE

Best External SSD (NZ, 2026)

Real-world speed depends on your own ports as much as the drive - here's what to check before paying for speed you might not get, with real NZ pricing.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

What actually matters

Three things decide whether an external SSD is worth it: sequential read/write speed (matters a lot if you're editing video or moving huge files, matters far less if you're just backing up documents and photos), durability/drop rating (worth paying for if it's living in a bag rather than a drawer), and - the thing that's changed the most heading into mid-2026 - capacity-per-dollar, because NAND flash prices have climbed noticeably this year on global supply constraints. Expect to pay more for the same capacity than you would have eighteen months ago, across every brand.

Also worth knowing: USB standard matters more than the brand name. A drive rated USB 3.2 Gen 2x2 can hit up to 2,000MB/s, but only if the cable, port and drive all support that standard - plug the fastest SSD into an older USB-A port and you'll get a fraction of the rated speed.

Our picks

Samsung T9 - 1TB around $189-$219 NZD, 2TB around $299-$349 NZD, 4TB around $549-$599 NZD

The benchmark for a reason: up to 2,000MB/s sequential speeds, a compact rubberised body, and a drop rating (up to 3m) that means it survives actually being carried around rather than just sitting on a desk. Who it suits: video editors and photographers who need genuine speed and don't want to think twice about durability. The trap: hitting the full 2,000MB/s requires a USB 3.2 Gen 2x2 port - most laptops still only offer Gen 2, which halves the real-world speed you'll see, so check your own ports before paying for speed you can't use.

SanDisk Extreme Portable SSD - 1TB around $159-$189 NZD, 2TB around $249-$289 NZD

IP65 water and dust resistance is the standout here - it's the one on this list you can actually get rained on or dusty without worrying. Sequential speeds around 1,050MB/s are plenty for most everyday use, backups, and photo transfer. Who it suits: anyone who wants a rugged everyday drive for travel or fieldwork rather than the absolute fastest numbers. The trap: it's not as fast as the T9 for large video files - if you're editing 4K footage directly off the drive, you'll feel the difference.

Crucial X9 Pro - 1TB around $149-$179 NZD, 2TB around $239-$269 NZD

The value pick - solid 1,050MB/s speeds, a compact aluminium shell, and consistently among the cheaper options per GB at any given capacity. Who it suits: students and everyday users who want reliable backup storage without paying for features they won't use. The trap: no meaningful drop or water rating - treat it gently, it's built for a bag pocket, not a work site.

Crucial X10 Pro - around $329-$379 NZD for 2TB

Aimed at creative professionals, with Thunderbolt 3/USB4 support pushing speeds up past 2,000MB/s on compatible ports, plus a metal unibody design that doubles as passive cooling under sustained transfers. Who it suits: anyone doing serious video work who's tired of drives throttling mid-transfer on long 4K/8K exports. The trap: you need a Thunderbolt or USB4 port to get the headline speed - on an older USB-C laptop it'll still work, just well under its potential.

Common mistake: buying based on rated speed alone without checking your own laptop's USB port generation. A $350 drive plugged into a basic USB-C port performs identically to a $200 drive on the same port - the expensive one only pulls ahead once the port itself can keep up.

For most people backing up photos and documents, the Crucial X9 Pro or SanDisk Extreme covers it without overpaying for speed you won't use. Only step up to the T9 or X10 Pro if you're actually moving large video files regularly.
