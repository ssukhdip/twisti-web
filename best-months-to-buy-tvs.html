MONEY-SAVING GUIDE

Best Months to Buy TVs

TVs run on one of the most predictable annual cycles in electronics. Here is exactly how it works and when it pays off for NZ buyers.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

The TV industry runs on a strict annual clock

Almost every major TV brand - Samsung, LG, Sony, TCL, Hisense - announces its new model-year lineup at CES in January, then rolls new stock into stores globally between March and June. That means the previous year's models are locked in place from launch until the next January, at which point they instantly become "last year's TV" even if nothing about them has changed.

This creates a very reliable price curve: new models launch at a premium price in the first half of the year, hold roughly steady through the middle, then get progressively discounted as the next CES announcement approaches and retailers need shelf space.

The cycle month by month

  • January-March: CES announcements land, but new stock has not hit NZ shelves yet - meanwhile last year's models start getting marked down to clear space.
  • April-June: New model-year TVs arrive in NZ stores at full price. Outgoing models are now genuinely discounted and represent the best value-to-spec ratio of the year for last year's flagship features.
  • July-October: A relatively quiet period - new stock is settled in at full price, older stock is mostly cleared. Prices are fairly static.
  • November-December: Black Friday and Boxing Day. This is when retailers discount current-year stock hardest, because they want the cash before calendar year-end and before the next CES cycle resets expectations.

Two different ways to win on a TV

Buy last year's flagship in April-June: you get last year's top-tier panel technology - often mini-LED or OLED - at a steep discount to its original price, because the brand is clearing it to make room for the new lineup.

Buy the current model on Black Friday or Boxing Day: you get this year's actual newest model, discounted as part of the year's two biggest sale events, but rarely as deep a percentage cut as a superseded model gets.

Panel technology changes the calculation

Not every TV generation is a meaningful upgrade. If a brand's new mini-LED or OLED panel for the year is only a marginal improvement on the previous generation, buying the outgoing model in the April-June window can get you 90% of the picture quality for 60-70% of the price. Check what has actually changed - sometimes it's mostly a smart-platform and remote refresh rather than the panel itself, in which case last year's model is the easy pick every time.

How this differs from the general sale calendar

Our broader seasonal sale calendar covers Black Friday, Boxing Day and back-to-school across every category. TVs are a special case because the CES/model-year cycle creates a second, independent discount window in autumn (April-June NZ time) that has nothing to do with the big retail calendar events - it is driven purely by the manufacturer's release schedule.

Common trap: a "was $2199, now $1599" sticker on a TV in November can still be a worse deal in absolute dollars than the same-spec previous-generation model that quietly dropped to $1399 back in May. Always check the price history rather than trusting the size of the sale banner - see our price tracking guide for how.

What to actually do

If you need a TV now and do not care about having the absolute newest model, shop in April-June for last year's flagship at its steepest markdown. If you want the newest technology, wait for Black Friday or Boxing Day and expect a genuine but smaller percentage discount. Either way, check price history before buying - a big banner in November does not always beat a quiet drop in May.
