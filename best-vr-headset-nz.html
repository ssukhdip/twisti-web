Best VR Headset NZ

VR headsets range from a $689 standalone device you can use anywhere to a $2,000+ PC rig that needs a serious gaming PC behind it. Here's what actually suits different NZ buyers.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
How we picked these: Based on current NZ pricing from Meta's NZ store, PriceMe and JB Hi-Fi NZ, plus PlayStation NZ's official pricing. No paid placements.

Best overall: Meta Quest 3S

The Quest 3S is the sensible default for most people getting into VR. It's fully standalone (no PC or console required), starts at $689 for 128GB and $879 for 256GB through the Meta Store NZ, and runs the full Quest content library including Beat Saber, fitness apps and mixed-reality experiences that blend VR with your actual room via the passthrough cameras. It's not the sharpest headset on the market (that's the full Quest 3, which uses pancake lenses for a noticeably crisper image), but it's the best value entry point by a wide margin.

Best display quality: Meta Quest 3

The full Quest 3 uses higher-quality pancake lenses versus the 3S's Fresnel lenses, resulting in a sharper, less distorted image, particularly noticeable in text-heavy apps and detailed games. It's more expensive than the 3S but still standalone and still runs the same library — worth the upgrade if visual clarity matters to you (reading text, sim racing, detailed strategy games) more than saving a couple hundred dollars.

Best for PS5 owners: PlayStation VR2

If you own a PS5, PSVR2 is the obvious pick — its price has dropped to around $749 NZD, and it delivers PC-VR-tier visuals (OLED display, eye tracking, proper haptic feedback in the headset and controllers) purely by tethering to your console, no gaming PC required. The catch is a much smaller library than Quest, since it's tied to PS5-published VR titles rather than the broader Quest and SteamVR ecosystems. It is not compatible with Quest headsets or PC VR without workarounds, and it can't be used standalone away from your PS5.

Best for PC VR enthusiasts: Valve Index

The Valve Index remains a niche but genuine option for PC VR purists, priced around $1,799 for the headset-and-controllers bundle or $2,299 for the full kit with base stations. Its finger-tracking "Knuckles" controllers are still considered some of the best in VR for natural hand interaction, and its wide field of view and high refresh rate (up to 144Hz) suit sim racing and flight sim enthusiasts. It requires base stations for room-scale tracking and a genuinely powerful gaming PC, putting total cost well above anything standalone.

Standalone vs PC VR: which do you actually want?

Standalone headsets (Quest 3/3S) are the right call for the vast majority of buyers — no PC needed, works anywhere in the house, and the Quest ecosystem has matured into the largest VR content library by far. PC VR (Valve Index, or Quest tethered via Link cable to a gaming PC) buys you sharper visuals and access to SteamVR's deeper sim and enthusiast titles, but only makes sense if you already own a capable gaming PC — buying one specifically for VR is a lot of extra cost for most people.
