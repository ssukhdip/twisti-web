BUYING GUIDE

Best Noise Cancelling Headphones

ANC quality has basically plateaued at the top end - so the real question is comfort, ecosystem, and whether you actually need flagship noise cancelling at all. Here's what we'd buy.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

What actually matters with ANC headphones

Everyone talks about noise cancelling like it is one single spec, but the thing that separates a great pair from a mediocre one is rarely the ANC strength alone. It is a combination of four things: how well it kills low-frequency drone (engines, aircon, the office aircon unit that never turns off), how natural the sound is with ANC on versus off, how long you can wear them without your ears aching, and whether the app and firmware actually get updated after launch. A pair that nails ANC but clamps your head like a vice by hour three is not a good buy, no matter what the spec sheet says.

Battery life numbers on the box are usually measured with ANC on and at moderate volume, which is a reasonable real-world estimate, but multi-point Bluetooth (pairing to two devices at once, like your laptop and phone) will drain it faster. If you switch between devices a lot, favour a model that handles multipoint gracefully rather than one that just claims to support it.

Our picks for winter 2026

Sony WH-1000XM6 — best all-rounder

NZ price: around $649 to $749 depending on retailer and colourway.

Sony has owned this category for years and the XM6 continues the trend: class-leading ANC, genuinely comfortable clamping force for long-haul flights, and a companion app that is not annoying to use. The multipoint pairing is properly reliable now, which was a weak spot on earlier XM generations. If you want one pair of headphones that does everything well and you are not locked into Apple's ecosystem, this is the safe pick.

Who it suits: commuters, frequent flyers, anyone who wants the best noise cancelling without any brand loyalty baggage.

Bose QuietComfort Ultra (2nd Gen) — best comfort and bass

NZ price: around $599 to $699.

Bose still makes the most physically comfortable over-ear headphones on the market, full stop. Lighter clamping force, plusher pads, and an immersive audio mode that adds a genuinely useful sense of spaciousness for movies. The ANC is a hair behind Sony's in the harshest environments (think jet engine drone) but wins on all-day wearability. If you have a smaller head or wear glasses, try these before you buy the Sony.

Who it suits: glasses-wearers, people who find the Sony's clamp too tight, anyone who prioritises comfort over the last 5 percent of ANC performance.

Apple AirPods Max 2 — best if you are all-in on iPhone

NZ price: around $849 to $899.

The most expensive pick here, and it is genuinely hard to justify the price on sound and ANC quality alone — the Sony and Bose above are close behind for hundreds less. What you are actually paying for is the seamless AirPods experience: instant pairing across Apple devices, spatial audio that tracks head movement for movies, and a build quality that feels properly premium. If you are not deep in the Apple ecosystem, this is the one pick on this list we would tell you to skip.

Who it suits: iPhone and Mac households who already live inside Apple's ecosystem and want the integration more than raw value.

Sony WH-CH720N — the budget-conscious pick

NZ price: around $129 to $169.

Not everyone needs flagship ANC. If you mostly want to mute an open-plan office or a bus commute rather than a transatlantic flight, this cheaper Sony does a genuinely solid job for a fraction of the price, with battery life that actually beats the flagships (Sony rates it around 35 hours with ANC on). The plastic build and thinner padding are the obvious compromises.

Who it suits: anyone buying ANC headphones for the first time who is not sure they will use them enough to justify $600-plus.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Buying more ANC than you need. If your daily noise problem is an open-plan office rather than jet engines, a $150 pair with decent ANC will solve it just as well as a $700 flagship.
  • Ignoring clamping force. This is the single biggest driver of long-wear comfort and it varies a lot between brands. Try before you buy if you can, especially if you wear glasses.
  • Assuming battery claims translate directly. Multipoint connections, high volume, and cold weather all eat into the advertised battery life.
  • Overlooking the case and cable situation. Some flagship headphones still ship with a USB-C to USB-A cable in the box — check what you actually need before you unbox it on Christmas morning.
  • Locking yourself into an ecosystem without meaning to. AirPods Max integrate beautifully with iPhone but noticeably lose features (spatial audio head tracking, seamless switching) on Android. Check this before you buy if you are not on iPhone.
Worth knowing: ANC quality has genuinely plateaued at the top end over the last couple of generations — the gap between a 2024 flagship and a 2026 flagship is much smaller than the gap between a budget pair and any flagship. Don't feel pressure to chase the newest model number if last year's version is discounted.
