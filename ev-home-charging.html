Smart Home EV Charging Guide

Charging an EV at home is usually cheaper and more convenient than public charging — but the setup you choose changes overnight charging times dramatically.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Level 1 vs Level 2 charging

Plugging an EV into a standard household socket (Level 1) works, but delivers power slowly — often only a few kilometres of range per hour. A dedicated Level 2 home charger, installed on its own circuit, charges dramatically faster and is the realistic option for anyone doing meaningful daily driving.

What a smart charger adds

  • Scheduling: set charging to run during off-peak electricity hours automatically, rather than remembering to plug in at a specific time.
  • App monitoring: check charge status, cost, and history remotely instead of walking out to look at the car.
  • Solar integration: some smart chargers can prioritise charging from excess solar generation if you have panels, rather than always drawing from the grid.
Before you install: a proper Level 2 home charger needs a dedicated circuit, sized appropriately by a licensed electrician for both the charger's draw and your home's existing capacity. Check your power retailer for EV-specific or off-peak plans too — the charging schedule only saves money if your plan actually prices power differently by time of day.

Is it worth it?

If you're driving an EV regularly, a proper home charger setup pays for itself in convenience alone — reliably starting each day with a full charge beats relying on public charging. For occasional or short-commute use, Level 1 charging overnight may genuinely be enough, and it's worth confirming your actual driving pattern before spending on an installation.
