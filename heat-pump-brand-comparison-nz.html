Mitsubishi vs Panasonic vs Daikin Heat Pumps in NZ

The big four split-system brands in New Zealand — Mitsubishi Electric, Panasonic, Daikin and Fujitsu — all make genuinely good heat pumps. The real differences are in installed pricing, cold-climate performance and how easy it is to get one serviced locally.

Twisti guides · Updated August 2026

What installed pricing actually looks like

For a single split-system unit sized for a typical NZ living room (around 3.5–5.0kW capacity), fully installed pricing in 2026 generally falls between $2,500 and $4,500+ depending on brand, capacity and installation complexity. Mitsubishi Electric and Daikin tend to sit at the upper half of that range for equivalent capacity, reflecting stronger brand demand and typically higher base unit cost. Panasonic and Fujitsu are frequently the more competitively priced options for a comparable-spec unit, often landing $300–600 cheaper installed for the same kW rating. Larger multi-room or ducted systems scale well beyond this single-unit range into the $8,000–15,000+ territory regardless of brand.

Mitsubishi Electric

Mitsubishi Electric (not to be confused with Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, a separate brand also sold in NZ) is generally considered the benchmark for cold-climate performance, with models like the GE and Kirigamine Zen ranges holding heating capacity well down to around -15°C outdoor temperature. It has the widest authorised installer and servicing network in the country, which matters for warranty claims and parts availability, particularly in the South Island.

Panasonic

Panasonic's Etherea and standard split-system ranges offer strong value — efficiency ratings are competitive with Mitsubishi and Daikin at a noticeably lower installed price point in many cases. Panasonic units are generally quieter at low fan speeds than the market average, which is a genuine point of difference for bedroom installs. Servicing network coverage is solid in main centres but thinner than Mitsubishi's in smaller regional towns.

Daikin

Daikin is a strong all-rounder with excellent build quality and one of the better reputations for long-term reliability in NZ conditions. Its cold-climate models perform close to Mitsubishi's, and Daikin has invested heavily in its NZ dealer network in recent years. It typically prices similarly to or slightly above Mitsubishi for equivalent capacity.

Fujitsu

Fujitsu's Lifestyle and General ranges are usually the most budget-friendly of the four brands for a like-for-like installed system, without a major compromise on core heating/cooling performance for milder climates. Where Fujitsu falls slightly behind is extreme cold-climate heating efficiency and the depth of its NZ servicing network compared to Mitsubishi and Daikin, particularly outside main centres.

Brand snapshot: installed cost & cold-climate fit

Mitsubishi Electric: $3,000–4,500+ installed, best cold-climate performance, widest NZ service network.

Panasonic: $2,500–4,000 installed, strong efficiency-to-price, quieter low-speed operation.

Daikin: $3,000–4,500+ installed, excellent reliability, close second on cold-climate performance.

Fujitsu: $2,500–3,800 installed, most budget-friendly, best suited to milder climates.

Noise levels

Indoor unit noise on modern units from all four brands is generally low (around 19–24dB on the lowest fan setting for a mid-size unit), quiet enough for most bedrooms. The bigger practical difference is outdoor unit noise, which matters for apartment or close-quarter suburban installs — Panasonic and Daikin's newer outdoor compressor units tend to run a touch quieter under load than comparable Fujitsu units, though this varies by specific model and installation.

Which brand suits which NZ climate zone

For colder regions — Otago, Southland, inland Canterbury, and central North Island areas that see regular frosts — Mitsubishi Electric or Daikin's cold-climate-rated models are the safer choice, since their heating output and efficiency hold up better as outdoor temperature drops toward and below freezing. For milder climates — Auckland, Northland, and most of the upper North Island — Panasonic or Fujitsu deliver very similar day-to-day comfort for meaningfully less money, since the extreme cold-climate engineering that justifies Mitsubishi and Daikin's premium isn't being fully used.

Useful tip: Ask your installer specifically for the unit's heating capacity (kW) at -7°C and at -15°C outdoor temperature, not just the headline rated capacity at 7°C — that's the number that actually predicts how the unit performs on a genuinely cold Otago or Canterbury morning, and the gap between brands widens significantly at the colder end.

Energy efficiency ratings

Look at the Energy Rating Label's star rating for both heating and cooling separately, not just an overall score — a unit can be a strong cooling performer with an average heating rating or vice versa, and NZ homes generally use heating far more than cooling. Mitsubishi Electric and Daikin's higher-end inverter models frequently carry 5–6 star heating ratings, while Panasonic and Fujitsu's mid-range models commonly sit around 4–5 stars — still efficient, just slightly behind at the very top end.

Servicing and parts network strength

A heat pump is a 10–15 year purchase, so ongoing servicing matters as much as the install-day price. Mitsubishi Electric has the broadest network of authorised installers and service agents across both islands, including strong coverage in smaller regional South Island towns where some other brands rely on a single traveling technician. Daikin has closed much of that gap over the past few years through dealer network investment, and its parts supply chain in NZ is now considered on par with Mitsubishi's for common components like fan motors and PCBs. Panasonic's servicing network is dependable in main centres — Auckland, Wellington, Christchurch, Hamilton, Dunedin — but thins out noticeably in smaller towns, meaning a call-out from a regional area may involve extra travel charges or a longer wait. Fujitsu's network is the least dense of the four, which is worth weighing against its lower purchase price if you live somewhere rural.

Bottom line

If you live somewhere that gets genuinely cold overnight — Central Otago, Southland, or inland high-country areas — Mitsubishi Electric or Daikin are worth the $300–600 premium for their cold-climate performance and stronger regional servicing network. If you're in Auckland, Northland or another mild coastal climate, Panasonic offers the best efficiency-for-price balance, and Fujitsu is a sound budget pick if servicing network depth isn't a major concern in your area.
