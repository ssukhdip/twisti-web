MacBook Air M4 NZ: price, specs and where to buy

Chip performance, the RAM decision that actually matters, and how to shop the Air sensibly in New Zealand.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Chip and performance

The M4 chip in the current MacBook Air brings a noticeable jump in multi-core performance and GPU speed over M2/M3-generation Airs, plus a genuinely upgraded Neural Engine for on-device AI tasks. For everyday use — browsing, documents, video calls, light photo editing — the difference versus an M2 Air is barely noticeable. Where it matters is multitasking with many apps open, video export, and anything leaning on Apple Intelligence features.

The RAM decision that actually matters

MacBook Air ships with 16GB as the sensible baseline now that Apple has bumped the entry configuration up from 8GB. Unlike storage, RAM cannot be upgraded later — it's soldered to the board — so this is the one spec worth paying up for if you're unsure. 16GB comfortably handles normal multitasking and browser tab hoarding; only go to 24GB if you're doing heavier creative work like large Lightroom catalogues or bigger Xcode builds.

13-inch or 15-inch?

The 13-inch is lighter and cheaper and suits anyone who moves around a lot or primarily uses an external monitor at a desk. The 15-inch adds meaningfully more screen real estate for side-by-side windows and costs more for essentially the same internals — a reasonable upgrade if the Air is your only screen and you value the extra space over portability.

Where to buy it in NZ

MacBook Air is sold at Apple's own store, JB Hi-Fi, Noel Leeming, PB Tech and Harvey Norman, plus education pricing through Apple's student store for eligible buyers. Since Apple controls retail pricing closely, differences between stores usually come down to bundled extras (AppleCare discounts, trade-in offers, gift cards) rather than the sticker price itself — student and education pricing is often the single biggest legitimate discount available if you qualify.

Don't overbuy storage if you use cloud storage anyway. 256GB is tight for a primary machine, but if you already lean on iCloud, Google Drive or an external SSD for large files, 512GB is plenty for most people and meaningfully cheaper than jumping straight to 1TB.

The bottom line

MacBook Air M4 is the sensible default choice for most NZ buyers who don't need a Pro's extra ports or sustained performance — prioritise RAM over storage if you're on a budget, and check student pricing before paying full price anywhere else.
