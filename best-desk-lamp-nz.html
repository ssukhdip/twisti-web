Best Desk Lamp NZ: Which One to Buy

A desk lamp is one of the cheapest upgrades for evening screen work, but most buying decisions come down to guesswork on brightness and colour. Here's what the specs actually mean.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Colour temperature: warm vs cool light

Colour temperature is measured in Kelvin (K), and it's the single most useful spec on any lamp listing. Lower numbers (2700-3000K) give a warm, yellowish light similar to an old incandescent bulb — comfortable for evening reading and relaxing, but can feel dim and yellow-tinted for detailed work. Higher numbers (5000-6500K) give a cooler, bluer light closer to daylight — better for focus and detail work like drawing, sewing, or reading fine print, but can feel harsh in the evening and interfere more with winding down for sleep if used late at night.

The best desk lamps let you adjust colour temperature across a range (usually 2700K-6500K) rather than locking you into one, so you can go warmer in the evening and cooler for daytime focus work — genuinely useful rather than a gimmick.

Brightness adjustability and eye strain

A lamp that's either fully on or fully off is a real problem for eye comfort — too bright at night strains your eyes against a dim room, too dim during the day means you're squinting. Look for a lamp with at least 3-5 brightness steps, or ideally a continuous dimmer/touch-slider rather than fixed presets. Also check the stated brightness in lumens, not just wattage — LED lamps use far less wattage than old incandescent or halogen equivalents for the same or greater brightness, so wattage alone is a poor comparison point on modern lamps.

Honest take: flicker is a real but under-discussed issue with cheap LED lamps, especially at low dimmed brightness — some budget lamps use crude dimming circuits that flicker faster than you consciously notice but that can still contribute to eye fatigue over a long work day. If you're sensitive to this, read reviews specifically mentioning flicker-free dimming (usually called "PWM-free" or "flicker-free") before buying.

Are USB charging ports on the base worth it?

A lot of modern desk lamps build in one or two USB-A or USB-C ports on the base, marketed as a convenience feature. In practice, this is genuinely useful if your desk setup is cable-cluttered and you want to charge a phone or set of earbuds without a separate charger and cable run — one less thing on your power board. It's not a reason on its own to choose one lamp over another if the light quality itself is worse, but as a tie-breaker between two similar lamps, it's a legitimate plus.

When is a premium lamp actually worth it?

  • You work long hours at a screen daily: a quality lamp with adjustable colour temperature and flicker-free dimming genuinely reduces eye fatigue over months of use — worth the extra spend.
  • You do detail work (drawing, craft, reading fine text): higher CRI (colour rendering index, look for 90+) makes colours and detail noticeably truer than a cheap lamp's washed-out light.
  • Occasional bedside or hallway use: a basic $20-30 lamp is genuinely fine — you won't notice the difference for infrequent, short use.
  • Kids' study desks: a mid-range adjustable lamp is worth it for eye comfort during years of homework, without needing the top-tier options aimed at designers and architects.

Where to buy one in NZ

Noel Leeming and Harvey Norman stock the well-known adjustable brands like BenQ and Xiaomi's desk lamp range at the mid-to-premium end. Briscoes and Kmart cover basic budget lamps well for occasional or kids' use. PB Tech carries a solid range of USB-charging-equipped lamps aimed at desk and gaming setups specifically.
