AirPods Pro vs Sony XM6: which earbuds actually suit your phone

Sony makes the better-sounding earbud on paper. Apple makes the one that disappears into your life if you already own an iPhone. Here's the honest trade-off.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

The short version

This one is less about which earbud is objectively better and more about which phone is in your pocket. The Apple AirPods Pro (current generation) sit around $449-$479 NZD, while the Sony WF-1000XM6 come in higher at roughly $549-$599 NZD. Sony is asking more money for a bud that, on paper, out-specs Apple in several categories — but ecosystem lock-in is a real cost too, and it's the thing most buyers underweight.

Fit and comfort

AirPods Pro win here for most people. Their foam-infused silicone tips are genuinely comfortable across long sessions, and in controlled comfort testing they score noticeably higher than Sony's after three-hour wear (around 8.4/10 versus 7.1/10 in independent testing). Sony's stemless design is smaller and more discreet, using a polyurethane-and-silicone-foam hybrid tip that feels secure, but some wearers report it becomes noticeable — a mild pressure — over longer stretches. If you wear earbuds for entire workdays or long runs, try both in-store before deciding; ear shape matters more here than any review can tell you.

Sound quality

Sony's WF-1000XM6 sound noticeably richer and more detailed — warmer bass, more spacious staging, and support for the LDAC hi-res codec if you're pairing with an Android phone. Apple's AirPods Pro have a well-balanced, crowd-pleasing tuning but no built-in EQ to reshape it, and no hi-res codec support at all. If pure audio fidelity is the deciding factor and you're on Android, Sony is the clear winner — LDAC alone makes a genuine difference with hi-res streaming.

Noise cancelling and transparency mode

Sony's ANC is marginally stronger in unpredictable environments because its scene-detection reacts faster. But Apple wins clearly on transparency mode — the hear-through setting that lets outside sound in — with more natural-sounding pass-through of voices and ambient noise. Sony's transparency mode has been described as sounding more robotic, especially with speech. If you walk or cycle through traffic and rely on transparency mode for safety, that's a meaningful practical difference, not just a nice-to-have.

Battery, durability and call quality

  • Battery: Both sit around 8 hours of playback with ANC on — essentially a tie, with the case topping each up to roughly 24-30 hours total.
  • Durability: Apple's AirPods Pro carry an IP57 rating (properly dust and water resistant); Sony's WF-1000XM6 sit at IPX4, fine for sweat and light rain but not built for the same abuse. Runners who train in NZ weather should weigh this one seriously.
  • Call quality: Apple's microphone array and wind-noise handling are the best in the business right now — if you take a lot of calls on the move, AirPods Pro consistently isolate your voice better in street noise.

Ecosystem lock-in: the cost nobody puts on the price tag

AirPods Pro are built for iPhone owners. Automatic switching between your iPhone, Mac and iPad, Find My integration, and now features like Live Translation and hearing-aid-style amplification only make sense — or only work at all — inside Apple's ecosystem. Pair them with an Android phone and you lose most of what makes them worth the money; you're left with decent buds and a worse app.

Sony's WF-1000XM6 are platform-agnostic. They work equally well on iPhone or Android, and Android users specifically unlock LDAC for meaningfully better sound. If you're on Android, Sony isn't just the other option — it's the more complete one.

Mythbusting: spatial audio is not the same experience on both. Apple's spatial audio with head tracking is deeply integrated into iOS and Apple Music and genuinely repositions sound as you turn your head. Sony's 360 Reality Audio needs specific mastered tracks and supporting apps to do anything comparable — most of your existing music library won't trigger it. Don't buy either bud purely for a spatial audio demo you saw once; check it actually works with the music service you use daily.

The verdict

If you're on an iPhone, this isn't really a contest — get the AirPods Pro. The seamless device switching, superior transparency mode, better call quality and tougher build justify the ecosystem tax, and they're actually the cheaper buds anyway.

If you're on Android, or you genuinely prioritise sound quality above all else regardless of phone, get the Sony WF-1000XM6. You're paying more, but you're getting richer sound, LDAC support, and a pair of buds that doesn't quietly nudge you toward buying an iPhone next.
