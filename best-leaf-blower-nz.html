BUYING GUIDE

Best Leaf Blower NZ: Which One to Buy

Leaf blower power ranges from a light battery handheld to a petrol backpack unit that can strip mulch off garden beds. Matching power to your section size actually matters.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Corded, battery or petrol: matching power to your section

Corded electric blowers are the lightest, cheapest and quietest option, but you're tethered to an extension cord — fine for a small courtyard or townhouse section, frustrating for anything larger. Battery-powered blowers have improved significantly and now cover most suburban sections comfortably, especially if you already own batteries in a brand's ecosystem (Ryobi One+, Makita 18V) that you can share across other tools. Runtime is the real limiter — a single battery charge typically covers 15-30 minutes of continuous use depending on power setting, which is plenty for weekly maintenance but tight for a big autumn leaf-fall clean-up. Petrol blowers deliver the most sustained power and unlimited runtime (as long as you have fuel), genuinely justified for large sections, rural properties, or anyone doing leaf clean-up as a regular paid job — but they're heavier, louder, and need fuel mixing and maintenance.

Noise and neighbours: a real consideration, not just courtesy

Leaf blowers are a genuine source of neighbour friction, especially petrol models which can run 90-105 dB at the operator's ear — loud enough to require hearing protection and audible well beyond your section boundary. Battery and corded electric models run considerably quieter, typically 65-75 dB, which matters if you live in a townhouse, unit, or close-set suburban street where early morning or weekend blower noise is a common source of complaints. Some NZ councils and body corporate rules do restrict noisy garden equipment hours — worth checking if you're in an apartment or townhouse complex before buying a petrol unit.

Section-size guidance: don't overbuy

A small courtyard or townhouse section is well served by a light corded or battery blower — a big petrol backpack unit is genuinely overkill and mostly ends up being annoying to store and start for a five-minute job. Standard quarter-acre suburban sections are the sweet spot for mid-power battery blowers, which now handle most leaf and clipping clean-up without the noise or maintenance of petrol. Large rural blocks, driveways, or anyone dealing with wet, heavy leaf litter under big trees genuinely benefits from petrol's sustained higher airflow.

  • Townhouse/small courtyard: corded electric or a light battery handheld is plenty and keeps noise down.
  • Standard suburban section: mid-power battery blower, ideally sharing batteries with tools you already own.
  • Large rural or heavy tree cover: petrol blower for sustained power and no runtime limit.
  • Air volume (m³/min) vs air speed (km/h): volume clears wet, heavy leaves better; speed is better for light debris on hard surfaces.
  • Vacuum/mulch function: useful add-on on some models if you want to collect rather than just relocate leaves.
Honest take: most suburban NZ sections don't need petrol power. A battery blower on the same platform as your drill or trimmer is quieter, cheaper to run, and enough for weekly maintenance.
