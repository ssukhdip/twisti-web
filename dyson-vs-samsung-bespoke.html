Dyson vs Samsung Bespoke

Dyson wins on raw suction and weight, Samsung wins on hands-off maintenance with its auto-empty station. Here is which trade-off actually suits your home.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Two very different philosophies on cordless cleaning

Dyson built its reputation on raw suction and engineering flash - cyclone technology, laser dust detection, digital motors spinning at absurd RPMs. Samsung's Bespoke Jet AI line takes a different angle entirely: it leans hard into the docking station experience, with models that auto-empty, auto-wash their own brush roll, and dry themselves so you are not stuck maintaining a dusty tangle of hair and filters every week. Both are genuinely excellent at picking dirt off your floor. The difference that actually matters to most buyers is what happens after the vacuum is full, and how much of the ongoing maintenance grind each brand is willing to take off your hands.

Suction power and cleaning performance

On raw numbers, Dyson still generally leads: the V15 Detect sits at 240 air watts and the Gen5detect at 280AW, while Samsung's Bespoke Jet AI line uses its HexaJet motor rated around 280W of suction power at the top end too, so the two brands are now genuinely competitive on paper rather than Dyson simply out-gunning everyone. In real-world testing, both brands perform strongly on carpet and hard floors, with Dyson generally edging ahead on stubborn embedded grit thanks to its cyclone separation design, and Samsung's AI Cleaning Mode doing a genuinely useful job of auto-detecting floor type (hard floor versus rug) and adjusting power on the fly without you touching a button.

Dyson (V15 Detect / Gen5detect) - best raw cleaning power

NZ price: roughly $850 (V15) to $1,500 (Gen5detect).

Stronger suction on the toughest jobs, laser dust detection that visibly shows what you are missing on hard floors, a smaller and lighter overall package, and no recurring costs beyond the occasional filter wash. Dyson's bin capacity (0.76L) is smaller than most Samsung stations, and you are emptying it by hand into your household bin every session or two.

Samsung Bespoke Jet AI - best for hands-off maintenance

NZ price: roughly $1,098 (Jet AI Lite Pet) to $2,099 (Jet AI Ultra Elite).

The All-in-One Clean Station empties the bin into a sealed bag automatically after every run, and higher-tier models charge two batteries simultaneously so you always have a fresh one ready to go - genuinely useful if you vacuum a large house in one sitting. Up to 100 minutes runtime on the flagship model beats both Dyson options. The trade-off is the clean station itself takes up real floor space, and the proprietary dust bags are an ongoing cost Dyson does not have.

Battery and runtime

Samsung's flagship Bespoke Jet AI models claim up to 100 minutes of runtime, genuinely ahead of the Gen5detect's 70 minutes and well ahead of the V15's 60. Some of that gap closes once you factor in that both brands' real-world runtime drops sharply on max power - nobody vacuums an entire house on the highest suction setting for 100 minutes straight. Where Samsung has a genuine practical edge is the dual-battery charging on higher-end models: you are never caught with a dead vacuum and a multi-hour recharge wait, because a second battery is always topped up in the dock.

Weight and ergonomics

Dyson vacuums are noticeably lighter in the hand - both the V15 and Gen5detect sit around 2.1 to 2.2kg, which matters when you are holding the wand up to clean ceilings, curtains, or car interiors. Samsung's Bespoke Jet models are competitive but generally a touch heavier, and the weight balance differs - some reviewers find Samsung's handle position a bit more front-heavy during overhead use. If you do a lot of above-floor cleaning (blinds, stairs edges, car boots), test the weight in person before you decide.

Filtration and allergies

Both brands offer genuinely strong sealed filtration on their higher-tier models. Dyson's Gen5detect edges ahead with its 0.1-micron seal versus Samsung's typical HEPA rating, but Samsung's auto-emptying station has its own allergy-relevant advantage: because you rarely open the bin by hand, you are not getting a face-full of dust every time you empty it, which matters more than lab-spec filtration numbers for a lot of allergy sufferers in practice.

Ongoing cost: the number nobody puts on the spec sheet

This is the biggest practical difference between the two brands. Dyson's cordless vacuums have essentially no recurring cost beyond an occasional filter rinse and, eventually, a replacement battery some years down the line. Samsung's clean-station models require replacement dust bags for the auto-empty function, which is a small but real ongoing cost that adds up over a few years of ownership - factor this into the higher upfront price of the clean-station bundles.

Debunking the myth: AI cleaning mode does not mean it thinks for itself

Samsung markets its AI Cleaning Mode heavily, and it is a genuinely useful feature - sensors detect floor type and adjust suction automatically so you are not fumbling with a dial. But it is worth being clear-eyed: this is smart automation, not a robot vacuum's spatial mapping or object avoidance. You are still pushing the vacuum around the room yourself; the AI is optimising power delivery, not doing the cleaning for you.

Verdict: who should buy which

  • Buy Dyson if: you want the lightest, most maneuverable stick vacuum with zero ongoing costs, and you do not mind manually emptying the bin every session or two.
  • Buy Samsung Bespoke Jet AI if: you have a larger home, hate the ritual of emptying a vacuum bin by hand, and are happy to pay a bit more upfront (and a small ongoing bag cost) for a genuinely hands-off experience.
  • Budget-conscious buyers: the Dyson V15 Detect at around $850 remains the strongest value pick of everything covered here.
  • Pet owners: Samsung's Jet AI Lite Pet and Dyson's Detect models both handle pet hair well; Samsung's auto-empty station is the bigger quality-of-life win if you are dealing with daily shedding.
Worth knowing: the clean-station bundles from Samsung look expensive next to a bare Dyson, but factor in that Dyson's higher-tier bundles with extra tools and a wall dock close a lot of that price gap - compare like-for-like bundles, not just the cheapest SKU from each brand.
