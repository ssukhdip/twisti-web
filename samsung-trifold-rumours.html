Samsung Tri-Fold Phone Rumours: Everything We Know

Samsung has led the foldable phone market for years, and a tri-fold device — a phone that folds twice into a tablet-sized screen — has been the subject of persistent speculation. Here's the current state of fact versus rumour.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

What's confirmed

Samsung has publicly acknowledged working on foldable form factors beyond its existing Z Fold and Z Flip lines, and has shown early tri-fold concept hardware at trade events in the past, though not as a shipping consumer product with confirmed specs or pricing. Samsung has not confirmed a specific launch date, model name or full spec sheet for a consumer tri-fold phone as of this writing.

What's being reported

Industry reports, largely sourced from supply chain analysts, have suggested Samsung is preparing a tri-fold device for a limited initial regional launch before any broader rollout, following the pattern Samsung has used for genuinely new form factors in the past. Reported details floating around include a folded size close to a standard smartphone that unfolds into a larger tablet-style display, and a price positioned well above Samsung's existing Z Fold line given the added hinge complexity. None of these specifics have been confirmed by Samsung.

Tri-fold vs the existing Z Fold line

Samsung's current Z Fold phones already command a premium over standard flagships because of their folding hinge and dual-screen design. A tri-fold device, with two hinges instead of one, would add further engineering complexity — reported concerns in early coverage include hinge durability, overall thickness when folded, and battery capacity given the larger internal footprint needed for the display. If those trade-offs play out as speculated, a tri-fold would likely appeal to a narrower audience than the existing Fold line, at least in a first generation.

What it could mean for NZ

  • Regional availability — Samsung has occasionally launched niche form factors in limited markets first; whether NZ would get a tri-fold at launch, later, or not at all is unconfirmed.
  • Price positioning — if reports of a premium price above the existing Z Fold hold true, an NZ tri-fold would likely land as the most expensive phone Samsung sells locally.
  • Case and accessory support — a genuinely new form factor typically means a slower ramp-up of third-party accessories compared to established phone shapes.
Our take: A Samsung tri-fold phone appears to be real hardware in development, but the launch timing, exact specs and NZ availability are all still speculative rather than confirmed. Given Samsung's history of limited first-market launches for new hinge designs, it would be reasonable to expect NZ availability to lag behind a first release elsewhere, if it arrives at all in an early wave.
