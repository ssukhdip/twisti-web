Eufy NZ Buying Guide

Eufy has built a loyal NZ following on one simple pitch: local storage, no mandatory subscription, and prices well under Ring or Nest.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
How we picked these: We reviewed current NZ retail listings at Noel Leeming, JB Hi-Fi, and The Warehouse, and compared spec sheets against user reviews to flag which models are genuinely worth the extra spend.

Eufy's pitch: no subscription required

Eufy's whole brand identity in NZ rests on one feature: most of its cameras and doorbells store footage locally on a HomeBase or onboard microSD card, rather than forcing a monthly cloud fee like Ring. That single decision has made it the default recommendation for budget-conscious Kiwi households wanting home security without an ongoing bill. Eufy gear is widely stocked at Noel Leeming, JB Hi-Fi, and The Warehouse, with PB Tech carrying the broader catalogue online.

Security cameras: SoloCam vs eufyCam

For a single spot — a front door or driveway — the SoloCam S340 (around $249 NZD at Noel Leeming) is the easiest starting point: fully wireless, solar-assisted battery, no hub required. If you want multiple cameras working together with longer battery life and higher resolution, the eufyCam 3 two-pack with HomeBase (around $799 NZD at JB Hi-Fi) is the better long-term buy, since additional cameras can be added to the same hub later.

Video doorbells

The Eufy Video Doorbell E340 (dual-camera, around $329 NZD) is the standout for Kiwi villas and townhouses with a package-delivery problem, since the second lower-angle lens actually captures parcels left on the step rather than just a visitor's knees. The battery-only Doorbell C30 (around $179 NZD) suits renters who can't run wiring, though it needs recharging every few months depending on usage.

Robot vacuums: where Eufy has grown fastest

Eufy's robot vacuum range has expanded quickly, and it's now genuinely competitive with Roborock at the budget end. The Eufy X10 Pro Omni (around $999 NZD at Harvey Norman) includes a self-washing, self-emptying dock — a feature that used to cost twice as much a few years ago. For smaller apartments or first-time robot vacuum buyers, the Eufy Clean 11 (around $399 NZD) covers hard floors and light carpet without the auto-empty dock, keeping the price down.

Which vacuum for which home

  • Small apartment, mostly hard floor: Eufy Clean 11 or similar entry model.
  • Medium home with pets: X10 Pro Omni for the self-cleaning dock and stronger suction.
  • Large multi-level home: pair a vacuum with Eufy's separately sold boundary strips, since Eufy's LiDAR mapping still handles single-level layouts best.

Indoor cameras and baby monitoring

The Eufy Indoor Cam S350 (around $139 NZD) pairs pan-tilt tracking with local storage, making it a sensible pick for keeping an eye on pets or as a basic nursery monitor, though dedicated baby monitor brands still offer better night-vision detail for that specific job.

Where the HomeBase actually matters

A common mistake is buying multiple standalone cameras without a HomeBase, then discovering local storage is capped and cloud backup requires a subscription after all. If you're planning to run three or more cameras, budget for the HomeBase 3 (around $249 NZD standalone) up front rather than retrofitting it later.

Where to buy in NZ

Noel Leeming and JB Hi-Fi run the most frequent Eufy bundle deals, especially around Black Friday and Boxing Day. The Warehouse stocks a smaller range but is worth checking for clearance pricing on previous-generation SoloCam models.
