Amazon Prime Day NZ: Is It Actually Worth Shopping?

Prime Day headlines look tempting, but by the time shipping, customs and currency conversion hit an NZ order, the actual saving can shrink a lot. Here's how to judge it properly.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Why the US price isn't the NZ price

Amazon Prime Day deals are typically priced and promoted in USD for the US site, and while Amazon does ship many items to New Zealand, the landed cost is a different equation entirely. Once you add international shipping, currency conversion at the day's exchange rate, and potentially GST or customs charges on higher-value orders, a headline discount can be significantly smaller — or gone entirely — by the time it reaches your cart total in NZD.

What typically ships well to NZ vs what doesn't

Small, light electronics — cables, accessories, some headphones — tend to ship more affordably relative to their price than bulky items like monitors, appliances or anything with lithium battery restrictions, which can face higher freight costs or shipping limitations. Always check the shipping estimate and delivery timeframe at checkout before assuming an item is a straightforward buy.

The real comparison to make

Add shipping, expected customs/GST charges, and currency conversion to the Prime Day price, then compare that total landed cost in NZD against the same or equivalent product from a NZ retailer's own sale. Only then is it a fair comparison.

GST and customs on imported orders

Goods imported into New Zealand above certain value thresholds can attract GST and, in some cases, customs processing costs, which are not always clearly shown at the point of purchase on overseas sites. This is one of the most common reasons a Prime Day "bargain" ends up costing more than expected once it clears customs. Check current thresholds and how they apply before ordering anything of meaningful value.

  • Confirm whether the seller collects NZ GST at checkout or whether it's charged separately on arrival.
  • Factor in potential delivery delays for international orders, especially around a high-volume sale event.
  • Check the return process for overseas orders — it's often slower and costlier than returning to a local store.

When NZ retailers are the smarter choice

Many NZ electronics retailers run their own competing promotions around the same period as Prime Day, partly to capture shoppers who'd otherwise be tempted overseas. These often come with local warranty support, faster delivery, and no customs uncertainty — all worth factoring in even if the headline price looks a touch higher.

Practical approach: Check local retailer pricing on the same or equivalent model before ordering from Prime Day. If the difference after shipping and customs is small, the convenience and warranty support of buying locally is often worth it.

Bottom line

Prime Day can offer genuine savings on smaller, easily shipped items, but it's rarely a straightforward win for bulkier electronics once real landed costs are counted. Always compare full cost, not headline price, against what's available in NZ.
