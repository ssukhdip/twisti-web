When to buy tech in NZ: a month-by-month price calendar

Prices on laptops, phones, TVs and appliances don't drop randomly — they follow release cycles, clearance windows and sales calendars you can actually plan around. Here's what tends to move when.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

If you can wait a few weeks, timing a tech purchase around the right window in New Zealand's retail calendar can save you a genuine chunk of money — often more than hunting for a one-off coupon. Below is a rough month-by-month shape of the year, based on how pricing typically behaves across JB Hi-Fi, Noel Leeming and PB Tech. Treat the price movements as directional rather than exact — always check the live price on the day, since exact numbers shift retailer to retailer.

January – February: post-Christmas and back-to-school

The first few weeks of January still carry Boxing Day–era clearance pricing on last year's TVs and appliances. By late January, back-to-school promotions kick in — this is one of the more reliable windows for laptop deals, particularly mid-range models retailers want to move before new stock arrives.

March – May: a quiet stretch, with one exception

This is generally the slowest period for genuine discounts — most retailers are running at full price between major sales events. The exception is outgoing laptop and monitor models: if a manufacturer has a new range landing later in the year, older stock can quietly get cheaper here as retailers thin it out ahead of the changeover.

June – July: End of Financial Year and Winter sales

EOFY (end of June) and Winter sales in July are genuinely useful windows, especially for appliances — vacuums, coffee machines and air fryers tend to see real cuts as retailers push to hit annual targets. It's also a reasonable time to check TV pricing, though it's usually not as deep as Black Friday or Boxing Day.

August – September: new phone season

New flagship phones typically launch in this window, which means the previous generation phone — still a perfectly good device — tends to drop in price as retailers clear space. If you don't need the newest model, this is often the best time all year to buy last year's flagship at a meaningfully lower price.

October – November: Black Friday build-up

Retailers usually start easing into Black Friday–adjacent pricing from early-to-mid November, sometimes under a "Black Week" banner. TVs, headphones and small appliances tend to see their deepest cuts of the year here. Current-generation flagship phones and new console hardware are usually the exception — see our Black Friday NZ guide for what genuinely goes on sale versus what's inflated hype.

December: Boxing Day

Boxing Day often rivals or beats Black Friday for appliance and TV clearance pricing specifically, as retailers push to clear end-of-year stock before the new calendar year's ranges arrive. Worth comparing both events rather than assuming Black Friday is automatically cheaper across every category.

The two-week rule. Whatever the season, check a product's price from two to four weeks before any "sale" starts. Some retailers quietly raise prices right before a sale so the discount looks bigger than it is — a genuine deal should beat the price from a month earlier, not just the price from last week.

Category quick-reference

  • Laptops — back-to-school (Jan–Feb) and Black Friday/Boxing Day (Nov–Dec) are the two strongest windows.
  • Phones — buy the previous generation just after a new launch (Aug–Sep), or wait for Black Friday if you want the newest model at a discount.
  • TVs — Black Friday and Boxing Day are consistently the strongest; outgoing models also get cheaper just before a new range launches.
  • Small appliances — EOFY (June), Winter sales (July), and Black Friday/Boxing Day all see genuine, comparison-worthy cuts.

The bottom line

There's no single "best" month to buy tech in New Zealand — it depends on the category. But if you can be patient, aligning your purchase with the right window above will usually beat buying on impulse at full price.
