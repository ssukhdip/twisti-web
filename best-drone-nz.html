Best Drone NZ

New Zealand's landscape is basically drone-photography bait, and the CAA rules are simpler than people assume. Here's what to buy and what you actually need to know before you fly.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
How we picked these: Based on current listings from NZ-authorised DJI retailers (DJI Ferntech, Drone Depot) and Autel dealers, plus aviation.govt.nz guidance. No paid placements.

Best overall: DJI Mini 4 Pro

The Mini 4 Pro is the drone most NZ buyers should actually get. It weighs under 249g, which matters hugely for CAA compliance (more on that below), yet shoots 4K/60fps HDR video with omnidirectional obstacle sensing that genuinely stops it running into trees. NZ pricing sits around $1,199 for the standard combo and roughly $2,399 for the Fly More Combo with the DJI RC 2 controller, extra batteries and a case — the Fly More Combo is worth it since battery life is the real limiting factor on any drone day trip.

Best step-up: DJI Air 3 / Air 3S

Once you're past 249g the CAA treats you differently (see below), but you get real gains: the Air 3S adds a dual-camera setup (wide plus telephoto) and a larger sensor that noticeably improves low-light and dynamic range over the Mini series. It's the pick for anyone doing paid or semi-serious landscape and real estate photography around NZ. Check current pricing at DJI-authorised NZ retailers like DJI Ferntech and Drone Depot, as Air-series pricing shifts with each hardware refresh.

Best alternative: Autel EVO Lite

Autel is the main non-DJI option stocked in NZ through dealers like Techoutlet and DronetechNZ, appealing mostly to buyers who want to avoid DJI specifically. The EVO Lite uses a four-axis gimbal and a 50MP 1/1.28-inch sensor, putting its image quality roughly in Air-series territory. It's a smaller ecosystem in NZ so parts, firmware support and resale are less straightforward than DJI — worth it mainly if you have a specific reason to avoid DJI hardware.

NZ CAA rules: the basics

  • Weight matters. Drones under 25kg fall under Part 101 rules for most recreational flying — this covers virtually every consumer drone.
  • Altitude limit: stay below 120 metres (400 feet) above ground level.
  • Line of sight: you must be able to see your drone with your own eyes at all times, not just through the app.
  • Daylight only for standard recreational flying.
  • Permission required from the property owner or occupier of any land you fly over, including many public parks and all DOC land unless specifically permitted.
  • Stay clear of aerodromes and controlled airspace without authorisation — check the Airshare app before flying anywhere near an airport.

Sub-249g drones like the DJI Mini series don't need to be registered in most casual-use cases, which is a big part of why they're the sensible default for most buyers. If you're flying commercially or regularly near controlled airspace, check aviation.govt.nz directly — rules around certification tighten quickly once you're outside pure recreational use.

Camera quality: what the tiers actually buy you

Entry Mini-tier drones (Mini 4 Pro and similar) shoot excellent 4K video that's genuinely good enough for YouTube, real estate walkthroughs and holiday footage. Air-tier drones add a second lens (telephoto or wide) and a larger sensor that shows up mainly in low light and when you crop or grade footage heavily. Unless you're doing paid cinematography work, the jump from Mini to Air is nice-to-have rather than essential.
