Best Electric Blanket NZ: Which to Buy

An electric blanket costs a fraction of running a heater all night, but underblanket, overblanket and dual-zone models all suit different people and different beds. Here's how to pick correctly.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Underblanket vs overblanket: different jobs entirely

An underblanket sits on the mattress beneath your fitted sheet and pre-warms the bed before you get in, then typically switches off or drops to a low maintenance heat once you're asleep — this is the traditional Kiwi winter staple and works well for most people. An overblanket/throw sits on top like a regular blanket and stays warm the whole time you're using it, which suits people who want ongoing warmth while reading or watching TV on the couch, or those with mobility issues who find it easier to have heat on top rather than reaching under sheets. They're not really interchangeable — buy based on whether you want pre-warming or continuous warmth.

Dual-zone control: worth it if you share a bed

If two people sleep in the same bed with different temperature preferences, a dual-zone electric blanket with independent left/right controllers is genuinely worth the extra cost over a single-zone one. Cheaper single-control blankets force a compromise temperature that's rarely right for both people, and one partner constantly turning it up or down disturbs the other's sleep. Most mid-range and above underblankets from Noel Leeming, Harvey Norman and The Warehouse now offer dual-zone as standard on queen and king sizes.

Underblanket vs overblanket vs heated throw

Underblanket: best for pre-warming a cold bed before sleep, most energy-efficient option for overnight use.

Overblanket: best for continuous overnight warmth or for people who find underblankets uncomfortable against the mattress.

Heated throw: best for couch use, reading, or as a supplementary layer rather than a full bed solution.

Safety features that actually matter

An auto shut-off timer (typically after 3, 8 or 10-12 hours depending on the model) is a genuine safety feature, not a marketing add-on — it prevents the blanket running unattended all night at full heat, which is both a fire risk over many years of wear and unnecessary since most people don't need sustained high heat once asleep. Overheat protection sensors are standard on any blanket sold through a reputable NZ retailer, but check the age of any secondhand or older blanket you're still using — internal wiring degrades over years of washing and folding, and insurers increasingly ask about the age of electric blankets after house fires.

Running cost reality: A standard electric blanket typically draws somewhere in the range of 50-150W depending on setting and size, run for a few hours before bed. That's a fraction of what a portable heater or heat pump draws to warm an entire bedroom overnight, which is exactly why electric blankets remain one of the cheapest ways to stay warm through a NZ winter without turning up the whole-house heating.

What to check before buying

  • Zone control — dual-zone for shared beds, single-zone is fine and cheaper for single sleepers.
  • Auto shut-off timer — confirm it has one and understand the default cutoff time.
  • Machine washability — check the controller detaches properly; not all models are fully washable.
  • Size matching — buy to your actual mattress size, not a generic assumption that one size fits all, as fit affects both warmth and safety.
  • Age and replacement — electric blankets have a practical lifespan of around 5-10 years; don't keep using one well past that out of habit.

Bottom line

Buy an underblanket for efficient overnight bed warming, an overblanket for continuous heat or couch use, and always check for dual-zone control if you share a bed with someone who runs a different temperature to you. Compared to heating a whole bedroom overnight, a good electric blanket is one of the cheapest genuine comfort upgrades available this winter.
