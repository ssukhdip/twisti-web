Cyber Monday NZ: is it actually worth waiting for?

Cyber Monday lands three days after Black Friday, and for a lot of NZ shoppers it blurs into one long weekend of sales. Is there any reason to hold out specifically for the Monday?

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Where Cyber Monday actually came from

Cyber Monday started in the US as an online-focused counterpart to the in-store rush of Black Friday — the idea being shoppers went back to work on Monday and shopped online during downtime. In New Zealand, that origin story doesn't really apply the same way, since most Black Friday shopping here is already online. The result is that Cyber Monday in NZ tends to function less as a genuinely distinct event and more as the tail end of an extended Black Friday sale period that many retailers now run across the entire weekend.

Do NZ retailers treat it as separate?

Some do run Cyber Monday-specific promotions — often on electronics and tech accessories specifically, playing into the "cyber" branding — while others simply extend their Black Friday pricing through to Monday without meaningfully changing anything. It varies retailer by retailer and year by year, which makes it hard to generalise. The practical reality is that by the time Cyber Monday arrives, most of the best individual deals from the earlier Friday-to-Sunday period have often already been snapped up or restocked at regular price.

Black Friday vs. Cyber Monday, in practice

Black Friday tends to have the broadest range of deals and the deepest initial discounts, particularly on physical/in-store-adjacent categories. Cyber Monday in NZ is more likely to be a narrower, tech-and-electronics-focused continuation, occasionally with a handful of new deals added, rather than a full second wave of discounting.

Is there any reason to specifically wait for Monday?

If you're after a particular tech accessory or electronics item and missed it over the weekend, it's worth checking whether Cyber Monday brings a fresh discount on that specific product — some retailers do hold back Monday-only offers. But treating Cyber Monday as guaranteed to be better than what came before it is a mistake; often the opposite is true, with some of the weekend's best stock already gone.

  • Don't assume Cyber Monday automatically beats Black Friday pricing
  • Check specific products you're after rather than waiting on principle
  • Compare against the price history from earlier in the weekend before assuming Monday is better
Worth knowing: Retailer strategy on Cyber Monday changes year to year — some genuinely add fresh deals, others just relabel Black Friday pricing. There's no reliable rule that holds every year, so check actual pricing rather than assuming based on past experience.

The bottom line

In New Zealand, Cyber Monday is best treated as a possible bonus on top of Black Friday, not a reason to deliberately hold off buying. If you see a good price during the Black Friday weekend on something you actually want, there's rarely a strong reason to gamble on Monday being better.
