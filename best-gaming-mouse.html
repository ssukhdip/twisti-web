Best Gaming Mouse (NZ, 2026)

DPI numbers matter less than grip style and weight - here's what actually decides comfort and accuracy, compared with real NZ pricing.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

What actually matters

Sensor DPI is the spec everyone quotes and the one that matters least past a certain point - every mouse on this list has a sensor accurate enough for competitive play at 800-1600 DPI, which is what most pros actually use. What matters more day to day: weight (sub-60g mice reduce fatigue in long sessions but feel skittish to some), wireless latency (genuinely a non-issue on any mouse using a proper 2.4GHz dongle from a reputable brand - Bluetooth alone can lag slightly more), and grip style, because a mouse shaped for palm grip will feel wrong in a fingertip grip and no amount of DPI fixes that.

Figure out your grip style before you shop: palm (whole hand resting on the mouse), claw (fingertips down, palm arched off the back), or fingertip (barely touching, mostly fingers). It changes which shape actually suits you more than any spec on the box.

Our picks

Logitech G Pro X Superlight 2 - roughly $299-$329 NZD

The mouse most competitive FPS players default to, and the reputation is earned - under 60g, Logitech's HERO sensor with genuinely zero perceptible wireless lag, and a shape that suits palm and claw grips equally well. Who it suits: FPS and competitive players who want a proven shape without gimmicks. The trap: no side buttons beyond the basics, and no RGB - if you want customisation for MMOs or want lighting, this isn't built for that.

Razer Viper V3 Pro - roughly $279-$309 NZD

Razer's answer to the Superlight, and arguably edges it on sensor tracking speed for very fast flick-heavy games, with a slightly flatter shape that fingertip grippers tend to prefer. Who it suits: fast-paced shooter players, especially those with a fingertip or hybrid grip. The trap: Razer Synapse software is heavier than it needs to be for a mouse this simple - budget a few minutes for setup and be aware it wants to run in the background.

Razer DeathAdder V3 - roughly $139-$159 NZD

The ergonomic palm-grip shape that's barely changed in over a decade because it doesn't need to - comfortable for long sessions, reliable sensor, and a much friendlier price than the flagship wireless mice. Who it suits: palm grippers who want a proven all-rounder for both gaming and general use, without flagship pricing. The trap: it's a right-hand-only ergonomic shape, so left-handed players are out of luck here.

SteelSeries Aerox 5 - roughly $179-$199 NZD

A lightweight honeycomb-style shell with more side buttons than most lightweight mice bother with, aimed at MMO and MOBA players who need extra binds without going to a full MMO mouse. Who it suits: players who want low weight but also want more than two side buttons. The trap: the honeycomb shell design (on older Aerox models) can let dust in over time - wipe it out occasionally, especially if you eat at your desk.

Logitech G502 X Plus - roughly $219-$249 NZD

Heavier and more button-laden than anything else here, with adjustable weights and a shape built for palm grip comfort over marathon sessions rather than competitive flick speed. Who it suits: single-player and strategy game fans who want a mouse that feels substantial in hand, plus general desktop use. The trap: at 100g+ it's genuinely heavier than the current competitive meta - not the pick if fast flicks in an FPS are your priority.

Common mistake: chasing max DPI numbers past 3200. Almost nobody plays at 16,000 DPI - it's a marketing number, not a practical setting. Match sensor quality and shape to your grip, not the biggest number on the box.

If in doubt: figure out your grip style first, buy accordingly, and don't overspend on DPI you'll never use.
