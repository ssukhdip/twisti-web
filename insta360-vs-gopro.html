Insta360 vs GoPro

GoPro built the action camera category; Insta360 rebuilt it around 360-degree capture and "reframe it later" editing. Here's how the X5 and Hero 13 actually compare.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
How we picked these: We compared the current flagship models — Insta360 X5 (and the pocket-style Ace Pro) against GoPro Hero 13 Black — using published specs and international pricing, since neither brand publishes an official NZD price list; NZ figures below are converted estimates, flagged as such.

Two different ideas of what an action camera is

GoPro Hero 13 Black is a traditional single-lens action camera: point it, shoot, and what's in frame is what you get. Insta360's X5 uses dual 1/1.28-inch sensors to capture a full 360-degree sphere, which you then "reframe" afterward in the app — cropping, panning, and stabilising the shot after the fact rather than needing to aim perfectly in the moment. Insta360 also makes the Ace Pro, a more GoPro-style single-lens camera (co-developed with Leica) for people who want Insta360's software and stabilisation without the 360-degree capture.

Specs that actually matter

GoPro Hero 13 Black shoots up to 5.3K resolution at 60fps with a 1/1.9-inch sensor, and is waterproof to 10m without a housing. The Insta360 X5 shoots up to 8K 360-degree video using two 1/1.28-inch sensors — noticeably larger than the Hero 13's single sensor — and is waterproof to 15m. Bigger sensors generally mean better low-light performance, which is a real advantage for the X5 in dim conditions like bush trails at dusk.

Pricing

GoPro Hero 13 Black starts around US$329 (roughly NZ$550-580 before local retailer markup), while the Insta360 X5 starts around US$549-559 (roughly NZ$900-950). Both brands sell through NZ retailers like JB Hi-Fi, Ted's Cameras, and Camera House, where actual NZD pricing typically runs a bit above a straight currency conversion — check current listings rather than assuming the conversion holds exactly.

Editing workflow

This is the real differentiator. With the Hero 13, you get the shot in-camera — good framing depends on aiming it correctly while you're moving. With the X5, you shoot the whole sphere and decide the framing afterward on your phone or desktop: pan, zoom, switch angles, even create hyperlapse and "invisible selfie stick" effects, all from one 360 capture. That flexibility is powerful for creators who don't want to think about framing mid-activity (mountain biking, diving, skiing) but it does mean more time in post-production, and files are larger.

Who each one suits

  • GoPro Hero 13: best if you want a camera you point and forget, tight integration with existing GoPro mounts/accessories, and slightly more compact everyday carry.
  • Insta360 X5: best if you want to fix framing mistakes after the fact, want genuinely better low-light footage, or are creating content where "invisible selfie stick" style shots and 360 reframing are the appeal (e.g. mountain biking, boating, group activities).
  • Insta360 Ace Pro: worth a look if you want Insta360's stabilisation and Leica-tuned colour science in a familiar single-lens form factor, without going full 360.

Durability and mounting

GoPro's mount ecosystem is enormous and well-established in NZ outdoor and watersports retailers — a real practical advantage if you already own GoPro mounts or are buying accessories locally. Insta360 mounts are compatible with standard GoPro-style mounts too, so this is less of a lock-in issue than it used to be.
