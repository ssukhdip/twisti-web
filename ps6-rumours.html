RUMOUR ROUNDUP

PS6 rumours: what's actually confirmed so far

Sony hasn't officially announced PlayStation 6 — here's what's leaked, what's plausible, and what's still pure speculation.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

What Sony has actually confirmed

As of writing, Sony has not officially announced PlayStation 6, its name, specs, or a release date. Everything below comes from industry leaks, supply-chain reporting and analyst estimates — treat it as informed speculation, not confirmed fact.

Expected release window

The most consistent estimate across reporting points to a late 2027 launch, though some analysts suggest it could slip into 2028 given rising component costs and slower-than-expected PS5 sales momentum potentially affecting Sony's timeline decisions. Nothing here is locked in — console launch windows have shifted before based on chip supply and market conditions.

Leaked specs (codename "Orion")

  • CPU — a custom AMD chip using Zen 6 cores, reportedly around 8 cores.
  • GPU — RDNA 5 architecture with roughly 52-54 compute units, targeting 2.5-3x the raw rasterization performance of PS5.
  • Memory — up to 40GB of GDDR7, a significant jump from PS5's setup.
  • Manufacturing — built on TSMC's 3nm process node, Sony's most advanced console chip to date if accurate.

Ray-tracing performance is rumoured to see the single biggest generational leap, with some estimates suggesting 6-12x PS5's ray-tracing capability — though these figures should be treated cautiously given how early and unofficial the leaks are.

A handheld companion device is also rumoured. Reports describe a portable codenamed "Canis" built to run PS5-family games natively rather than by streaming, positioned as part of the PS6 hardware family. Like everything else here, this is unconfirmed by Sony.

The bottom line

Every spec, name and date attached to PS6 right now is an unverified leak — nothing is confirmed by Sony. If you're deciding whether to buy a PS5 or PS5 Pro today, a 2027-2028 PS6 launch window means there's no need to hold off for a next-gen console arriving imminently.
