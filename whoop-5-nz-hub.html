Whoop 5.0 NZ: Price, Specs & Where to Buy

Whoop 5.0 drops the screen-free band price and folds hardware into a membership, which trips up a lot of first-time buyers. Here's how the NZ pricing actually breaks down.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
How we picked these: Pricing pulled from Whoop's official NZ storefront (shop.whoop.com/nz) and JB Hi-Fi NZ, Whoop's exclusive NZ retail partner. Whoop's model is membership-first, so we've broken out device cost versus ongoing membership cost separately, checked July 2026.

How Whoop's pricing model actually works

Unlike a Garmin or Apple Watch, you don't really "buy" a Whoop outright — you join a membership, and the band itself is bundled in (sometimes free, sometimes discounted, depending on the plan and current promotion). In NZ, the entry point is the Whoop Your Way 5.0 package, which starts from around NZ$79 for the band with a membership commitment, while individual replacement bands and premium materials (like the SuperKnit Luxe band) range from roughly NZ$69 to NZ$229.

The real cost is the membership, which renews annually. After the first year, Whoop's tiers renew at approximately:

  • Whoop One: ~NZ$359/year
  • Whoop Peak: ~NZ$489/year
  • Whoop Life: ~NZ$739/year

Higher tiers unlock more health metrics, coaching features, and in some cases better hardware inclusions. If you're comparing total cost of ownership against a one-off purchase like a Garmin or Apple Watch, budget for the membership as an ongoing subscription, not a one-time cost — it's the single biggest thing people underestimate about Whoop.

What's new in 5.0

Whoop 5.0 is smaller and lighter than the 4.0 band it replaces, with Whoop claiming meaningfully longer battery life (Whoop has publicly cited up to roughly double the battery life of 4.0 in some configurations) and refined heart rate and skin temperature sensors. There's no screen — Whoop's whole design philosophy is "wear it and forget it," with all the data living in the phone app. If you want an at-a-glance display on your wrist, this isn't the device for you; that's what Garmin or Apple Watch are for.

Who Whoop actually suits

Whoop is built for people obsessive about recovery, sleep, and training load — athletes, gym-goers doing structured programmes, and people managing burnout or sleep issues who want granular daily data. It's screen-free and genuinely comfortable to sleep in, which is a real advantage over most smartwatches. It's a poor fit if you want notifications on your wrist, GPS mapping for runs, or a device you buy once and own outright — Whoop is a subscription product first and a piece of hardware second.

Where to buy in NZ

JB Hi-Fi is Whoop's exclusive NZ retail partner for in-store and online browsing, though the membership itself is set up and billed through Whoop's own NZ storefront (shop.whoop.com/nz). Because the hardware is tied to the membership, there's less scope for retailer price shopping than with a typical gadget — the main lever is which membership tier and promotional package you sign up under.

Before you commit

  • Check the renewal price, not just the intro offer — Whoop membership renews at full price after year one.
  • Decide if you need Peak or Life tier features (like advanced coaching or stress management tools) before paying for them — One tier covers the core sleep/recovery/strain metrics most people actually use.
  • Factor in that cancelling means losing app access to your data and the device becomes non-functional — this is different to owning a smartwatch outright.
