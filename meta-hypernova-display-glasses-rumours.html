Meta Ray-Ban Display Gen 2 / Hypernova Rumours: What's Next for Meta's AR Glasses

Meta's $799 Ray-Ban Display glasses — the ones with an actual screen in the lens, built under the codename Hypernova (also called Celeste) — only just started shipping. Even so, early chatter about a successor is already circulating. Here's what's real and what's guesswork, and how this line differs from the cheaper, screenless Gen 3 Ray-Bans.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
Why we're labelling this so carefully: Twisti's whole value is showing you real, current prices — not guesses. None of the pricing below is confirmed. We're publishing it because people are genuinely searching for this information already, but we'd rather be upfront that it's industry speculation than let a rumour read like a fact.

What's confirmed

The current Meta Ray-Ban Display launched at $799 with a monocular waveguide display.

Meta officially shipped this device — built under the internal codename Hypernova and reportedly marketed at times as "Celeste" during development — with a single in-lens display offering roughly a 600x600 pixel resolution and a 20-degree field of view, paired with the EMG Neural Band wristband for gesture control. This is a separate, pricier product line from Meta's screenless Ray-Ban glasses (Gen 2, and the rumoured Gen 3).

Because this product only recently rolled out, Meta has said nothing official yet about a follow-up. Everything below is early-stage leak territory, and we're being extra cautious about how confidently we state any of it.

What's reported (analyst / supply-chain sourced)

A binocular successor — display in both lenses — is reportedly being considered for around 2027.

UploadVR has reported Meta is developing a follow-up, sometimes referred to informally as "Hypernova 2," that would add a second in-lens display rather than the single-eye setup on the current model. Some coverage suggests this could potentially move up from a 2027 target, but nothing is locked in.

Meta's own CTO has publicly floated the size-versus-display tradeoff.

Andrew Bosworth reportedly polled followers on Instagram asking whether they'd prefer a smaller, lighter frame or a second display (with the added cost and weight that implies) — a sign the binocular decision genuinely hasn't been finalised internally, rather than being a done deal being teased.

Meta reportedly built the current Display glasses as a limited, expectations-managed run.

Road to VR has reported that a "respected analyst" expects the current $799 Display glasses to sell in relatively modest volumes, framing them as an early-adopter product rather than a mass-market push — which is part of why a more ambitious, higher-volume successor is plausible down the line.

Twisti's opinion

Treat "Hypernova 2" as a direction, not a spec sheet.

Unlike the screenless Gen 3 Ray-Bans, where leaked renders and named battery figures are already circulating, the display-glasses successor is still at the "internal poll about tradeoffs" stage. That's normal for a product this new — the current Display glasses have barely reached buyers — but it also means anyone quoting firm 2027 specs right now is getting ahead of what's actually been reported.

What we'll update

We'll revise this post once Meta confirms any roadmap details for a Display successor, or if credible supply-chain reporting attaches real specs, pricing or a launch window rather than the current binocular-versus-size framing.
