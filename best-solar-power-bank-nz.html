Best Solar Power Bank NZ: Which One to Buy

Solar power banks are genuinely useful for camping and emergency prep, but only if you understand what the solar panel actually does — and what it doesn't.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Set your expectations on solar charging speed first

This is the single most misunderstood thing about these products. The small solar panel built into or folded onto a power bank is not a replacement for wall charging — it's a slow trickle-charge backup for when you have no other option. A typical panel on a portable power bank might take somewhere from a full day to several days of direct, unobstructed sunlight to fully charge a mid-capacity battery, and that's assuming clear skies. Clouds, shade, an imperfect angle to the sun, or NZ's shorter winter daylight hours all slow it down further. If you're buying one expecting to top up a power bank at lunch and use it that evening, you'll be disappointed. Buy one expecting the solar panel to be a slow, weather-dependent bonus feature, and a plan B for multi-day trips where you can prop it in full sun for hours at a time, and you'll be using it correctly.

Capacity vs weight for hiking

Bigger batteries mean more charges, but also more weight and bulk in a pack where every gram matters. For weekend tramping where you mainly need to top up a phone or GPS device, a smaller, lighter power bank with a modest solar panel is usually the better trade-off than a huge capacity brick that never gets fully solar-charged anyway because you don't have days of continuous sun exposure. For multi-day trips in remote areas with no power access, a higher-capacity bank makes more sense, and it's worth accepting the weight penalty as the cost of genuine redundancy. A rough way to think about it: match the capacity to how many phone charges you actually need between opportunities to use mains power, not to the biggest number on the shelf.

What to actually check before buying

  • Wall-charging speed: A solar power bank should still charge quickly and fully from a USB-C wall charger — that's how you'll charge it 90% of the time.
  • IP rating: For camping and hiking, look for genuine water and dust resistance ratings, not just "splash resistant" marketing language.
  • Panel type and folding design: Foldable multi-panel designs generally charge faster than a single small panel embedded in the case, because they capture more surface area of sunlight.
  • Pass-through charging: Useful if you want to charge the bank via solar while simultaneously charging a device, though it slows both processes down.
  • Battery chemistry and cycle life: LiFePO4 batteries tolerate heat and repeated charge cycles better than standard lithium-ion, which matters if it's sitting in direct sun regularly.

The genuine emergency-preparedness case

Solar power banks earn their keep in genuine emergency scenarios — power cuts after storms, which NZ sees a fair amount of, especially in rural and coastal areas prone to outages. Here the value isn't fast charging; it's having a way to keep a phone alive for calls and updates over several days without mains power, especially paired with a car charger or a larger portable solar panel if you have one. For that use case, prioritise capacity and reliability over charging speed, and don't rely on the solar panel alone — pair it with charging the bank fully from the wall before storm season and treating solar as a slow top-up once it's depleted.

Worth noting: some marketing figures for solar charging times are based on lab conditions with a solar simulator, not real NZ outdoor sunlight — treat manufacturer charging-time claims as best-case, not typical.

Skip it if

If your main use case is daily commuting or topping up a phone at work, a standard high-capacity power bank without a solar panel will charge faster, cost less, and weigh less — the solar panel is dead weight you're carrying for a scenario that won't come up. Solar genuinely earns its place only for multi-day outdoor trips or emergency preparedness where mains power isn't an option for a while.
