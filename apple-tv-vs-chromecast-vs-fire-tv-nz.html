Apple TV vs Chromecast vs Fire TV: Which Streaming Box Suits NZ Households

All three play Netflix and Disney+ fine. The real differences show up with NZ-specific apps, remote quality, and how much you're already locked into Apple or Google's ecosystem. Here's what actually matters for a NZ lounge.

Twisti guides · Updated August 2026

The three contenders, realistically priced for NZ

As of 2026, the current lineup and typical NZ retail pricing looks like this: the Apple TV 4K (3rd gen, Wi-Fi model) sits around $219–249 from JB Hi-Fi, Noel Leeming or the Apple Store NZ. The Chromecast with Google TV (4K) runs roughly $99–119. The Amazon Fire TV Stick 4K is the budget option at around $75–95, with the entry Fire TV Stick HD occasionally dropping under $60 on sale. That price spread alone tells you a lot about the intended audience for each.

NZ streaming app support — where they actually differ

App availability by device (NZ store fronts, 2026)

Apple TV 4K: Full native apps for Netflix, Disney+, Prime Video, Neon, TVNZ+ and ThreeNow all confirmed working well via the App Store. Apple's tvOS app review process is strict, so NZ broadcaster apps tend to be polished here, though TVNZ+ and ThreeNow occasionally lag slightly behind their Android TV releases on new features.

Chromecast with Google TV: Full native support for all of the above — TVNZ+, ThreeNow, Neon, Netflix, Disney+ and Prime Video all have dedicated Android TV apps that install directly, and because most NZ broadcaster apps are built Android-first, TVNZ+ and ThreeNow often get feature updates here before Apple TV.

Fire TV Stick 4K: Netflix, Disney+ and Prime Video (unsurprisingly, being Amazon's own storefront) all work natively. TVNZ+ and ThreeNow are available via the Amazon Appstore but have historically been slower to receive updates and occasionally lag in picture quality options compared to their Android TV or tvOS counterparts — worth checking current reviews before committing if TVNZ+ is a daily-use app for your household.

Remote and interface quality

The Apple TV's Siri Remote (redesigned in 2021, still current) is genuinely the best hardware remote of the three — aluminium, a proper clickable trackpad ring, and a dedicated mute button most competitors lack. The interface is fast and consistent because Apple controls both hardware and software tightly. Chromecast's remote is a serviceable plastic remote with a dedicated Google TV button and reasonably intuitive voice search via the Google Assistant button. Fire TV's remote is functional but the interface is the most ad-heavy of the three — Amazon surfaces Prime Video content and sponsored tiles prominently on the home screen, which some buyers find intrusive for a device they paid for outright.

Performance and 4K/HDR handling

All three handle 4K HDR (Dolby Vision or HDR10) content from Netflix and Disney+ without issue on compatible TVs. The Apple TV 4K's A15-class chip gives it a noticeable edge in app load speed and smoothness of the interface, which matters more than most buyers expect once you're several months in — cheaper streaming boxes on Chromecast and base-tier Fire TV Stick can feel sluggish navigating menus after a system update or two.

Real-world NZ tip: if your household already pays for Neon, note that Neon is a Sky-owned service and its app is consistently well-supported across all three platforms as of 2026 — it isn't a differentiator. Where you'll actually notice a gap is TVNZ+ during live events (like rugby test coverage), where the Android TV version on Chromecast has tended to get stability fixes first.

Voice search and smart home integration

All three devices support voice search for finding shows, but the ecosystem tie-in differs meaningfully. Apple TV's Siri integrates cleanly with HomeKit, so if you've got HomePods, HomeKit-compatible lights or a smart lock, the Apple TV can double as a local smart home hub. Chromecast with Google TV leans on Google Assistant, which is genuinely useful if you already run Nest speakers, Nest cameras or Google Home routines — you can ask it to show your front door camera feed on the TV, for instance. Fire TV's Alexa integration is the most mature of the three for pure voice shopping and Alexa routines, which matters if you already have Echo speakers scattered around the house.

Storage, ports and extras

The Apple TV 4K comes with either 64GB or 128GB storage and includes a Thread border router built into some models, useful for Matter smart home devices. Chromecast with Google TV has modest onboard storage (roughly 8GB usable) which is fine for a streaming-only device but tight if you sideload a lot of apps. Fire TV Stick 4K is similarly modest on storage. None of the three include an Ethernet port out of the box — Apple TV 4K needs the pricier model or a USB-C to Ethernet adapter for wired connections, which is worth knowing if your NZ broadband setup has a flaky Wi-Fi signal near the TV.

Which suits which household

  • Already own iPhones, use AirPlay, or have an Apple Music/iCloud household: Apple TV 4K is worth the premium — AirPlay mirroring from iPhone, HomeKit integration and Apple ecosystem features (shared photo albums, Find My) work seamlessly in a way the other two can't replicate.
  • Android phone users or households already using Google Home/Nest devices: Chromecast with Google TV is the natural fit — Google Assistant voice control ties into existing smart home setups, and at under $120 it's the best value-for-features option of the three.
  • Budget-focused buyers, spare bedroom or bach TV: Fire TV Stick 4K is hard to beat under $95, especially when Amazon runs its regular NZ sale pricing (frequently under $70 during Black Friday or Boxing Day sales). The ad-heavy interface is the tradeoff for the lower price.

Bottom line

For most NZ households not deeply tied to one ecosystem, the Chromecast with Google TV is the best all-rounder — full NZ app support, often the first to get TVNZ+ and ThreeNow updates, and the best price-to-feature ratio. Go Apple TV 4K if you're already an iPhone household and value the noticeably smoother interface and AirPlay integration enough to pay roughly double. Choose the Fire TV Stick 4K only when price is the deciding factor, such as kitting out a second TV, and you're willing to tolerate a more ad-heavy home screen.
