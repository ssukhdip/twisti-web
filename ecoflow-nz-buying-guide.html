EcoFlow NZ Buying Guide

EcoFlow has grown from camping-focused batteries into a full home backup ecosystem. Here's how its NZ range breaks down and which model actually fits.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
How we picked these: This guide is based on EcoFlow's current NZ product range as listed at Noel Leeming, PB Tech and EcoFlow's own NZ store, checked against published capacity and recharge specs.

EcoFlow's lineup at a glance

EcoFlow's range splits into the compact River series, the mid-to-large Delta series, and an expanding whole-home backup ecosystem that ties batteries into your switchboard. The key differentiator against rivals is recharge speed — EcoFlow's X-Stream fast charging tech gets most units from flat to 80% in under an hour on mains power, which matters if you're relying on it during back-to-back outages.

River 3 — best light and portable option

The River 3 costs around $429 at PB Tech and is EcoFlow's answer to weekend camping needs — small, light, and enough for phones, cameras and small lighting across a multi-day trip. Its fast recharge means you can top it up quickly from a car outlet between stops.

River 3 Plus — the versatile middle ground

At around $549 from Noel Leeming, the River 3 Plus adds meaningful extra capacity over the base River 3, enough to run a CPAP machine overnight or keep a family's devices charged through a full weekend away. This is the model most casual campers and light blackout-prep buyers should look at first.

Delta 3 Plus — serious home backup

The Delta 3 Plus (around $1,399 at PB Tech) steps into genuine home backup territory, with enough capacity and output to run a fridge, lighting and multiple devices through an extended outage. It supports expansion batteries and solar input, making it a sensible base to build a larger home backup setup around over time.

Delta Pro 3 — whole-home ambitions

For buyers wanting to back up several circuits in a power cut, the Delta Pro 3 at around $3,299 from EcoFlow's NZ store scales up to genuinely substantial capacity, and can be combined with EcoFlow's home backup panel to automatically switch key circuits over during an outage — closer to a mini home battery system than a portable unit at this point.

Solar panels — do you need one?

EcoFlow's 110W portable solar panel (around $399) makes sense for off-grid camping trips where you can't recharge from the mains for days at a time. For home blackout backup where you'll simply recharge from the wall socket before and after outages, it's an optional extra rather than essential.

Which EcoFlow model fits your situation

  • Solo or couple weekend camping: River 3.
  • Family camping or CPAP use overnight: River 3 Plus.
  • Home blackout backup for key appliances: Delta 3 Plus.
  • Multi-circuit whole-home backup ambitions: Delta Pro 3 with the home backup panel.
