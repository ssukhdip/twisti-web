Best Portable Monitor (NZ, 2026)

Single-cable power draw matters more than resolution for most people - here's what to check before buying, with real NZ pricing.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

What actually matters

Resolution and size trade off against portability the same way they do on any screen, but two things matter more for a portable monitor than a desktop one: brightness for outdoor/bright-room use (250 nits is fine indoors, you want 300-400+ if you plan to use it near a window or outside) and power draw from your laptop's USB-C port, because most portable monitors are powered entirely by the single cable running to your laptop - no separate power brick. That's the whole appeal, but it means an underpowered laptop port, or a laptop already running low on battery, can leave you with a monitor that flickers or won't power on at all.

Check whether your laptop's USB-C ports support both video (DisplayPort Alt Mode) and enough power delivery for the monitor - not all ports do both, especially on older or cheaper laptops.

Our picks

ASUS ZenScreen MB16AHP - roughly $329-$379 NZD

The reliable default - 15.6" Full HD, built-in kickstand and a smart cover that doubles as a stand, plus ASUS's typically solid build quality. Single USB-C cable for power, video and data on a compatible laptop. Who it suits: anyone wanting a dependable second screen for travel or a compact desk setup without chasing extra features. The trap: Full HD at 15.6" isn't razor sharp up close - fine for a second screen at arm's length, less ideal if you plan to do detailed photo work on it.

ASUS ZenScreen Go MB16AWP (battery model) - roughly $449-$499 NZD

Same core panel as the MB16AHP but with a built-in battery, so it can run untethered from a laptop's power for a few hours - genuinely useful on a plane or anywhere power isn't guaranteed. Who it suits: frequent travellers who want a second screen that doesn't instantly drain the laptop battery. The trap: it's noticeably heavier and thicker than the non-battery model - weigh up whether you'll actually use the battery feature often enough to justify carrying the extra weight.

UPERFECT UStation Delta - roughly $229-$279 NZD

A budget-friendly option that punches above its price with a built-in stand and touch-capable versions on some models. Who it suits: people testing whether a portable monitor fits their workflow before committing to a pricier brand-name option. The trap: build quality and screen uniformity vary more between units than with ASUS - check the return policy given occasional reports of panel inconsistency.

InnoView 15.6" Portable Monitor - roughly $199-$249 NZD

The cheapest sensible option here, with Full HD resolution and basic single-cable USB-C operation covering the essentials without extras. Who it suits: occasional use - a spare display for a work trip a few times a year rather than a daily-driver second screen. The trap: brightness tops out lower than the ASUS options, so outdoor or bright-office use will feel dim by comparison - fine at a desk indoors, less fine in a sunny cafe.

Common mistake: assuming any USB-C cable will power a portable monitor off a laptop. Cheap or charge-only USB-C cables often lack the data lines needed for video - use the cable that came with the monitor, or a certified USB-C cable rated for both data and power delivery.

If you're using this for travel, prioritise the single-cable power draw and check it against your specific laptop's ports before buying - that detail matters more than resolution for most people's actual use case.
