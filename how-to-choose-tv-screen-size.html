How to Choose the Right TV Screen Size

Screen size is one of the easiest specs to get wrong when buying a TV, because it is tempting to just go as big as the budget allows. Viewing distance and room layout usually matter more than the number on the box.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Start with viewing distance, not the screen

The general rule that home theatre enthusiasts use is to work backward from how far you actually sit from the TV, rather than picking a size first and figuring out the room later. As a rough guide, many people find a comfortable viewing distance is somewhere around 1.5 to 2.5 times the screen's diagonal size, though personal preference and eyesight vary quite a bit. If you regularly sit close — in a small apartment lounge, for example — a very large screen can feel overwhelming or make fast motion harder to watch comfortably.

Resolution changes the maths a little

Higher resolution screens, such as 4K, let you sit closer to a larger screen without individual pixels becoming visible, compared to an older 1080p panel of the same size. This is part of why 55 and 65 inch TVs have become common living room sizes in New Zealand homes even in fairly modest-sized rooms — the resolution supports a shorter viewing distance than older technology allowed.

Common room sizes and typical TV sizes

As a general pattern rather than a strict rule: smaller bedrooms or secondary rooms often suit screens in the 32 to 43 inch range, typical living rooms with moderate viewing distances commonly work well with 50 to 65 inch screens, and larger, more open living areas or dedicated media rooms can often accommodate 65 inches and above. These are loose guides — always sanity-check against your actual room dimensions rather than following them rigidly.

Do not forget where the TV will physically sit

Measure the cabinet or wall space you intend to use before settling on a size. It is easy to fall in love with a screen size in-store, where showroom rooms are large, only to find it dominates a smaller lounge once it is home. If wall-mounting, also check stud spacing and cable routing, since larger panels are heavier and need secure mounting.

Tip: Many retailers list a TV's exact width, height and depth on the product page — measuring your own space against those figures before buying is more reliable than judging size by the diagonal inch measurement alone.

Balancing size against budget

Once you know a sensible size range for your room, it is usually more effective to compare panel quality — brightness, contrast and processing — within that size range than to stretch the budget for a larger screen with weaker picture quality. A well-specified 55 inch TV often looks better in daily use than an oversized budget screen crammed into too small a room.
