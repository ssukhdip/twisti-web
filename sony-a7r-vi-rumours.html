Sony A7R VI Rumours: Sensor, AF & Price Predictions

The Sony A7R VI has already launched, so most of what follows is confirmed rather than rumoured. We're keeping the honest breakdown here for anyone comparing what leaked beforehand against what Sony actually shipped.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
Why we're labelling this so carefully: Twisti's whole value is showing you real, current prices — not guesses. The A7R VI is now an official product, so most of this page is confirmed, but NZ retail pricing and local stock timing can still shift, so we've kept our usual labelling.

What's confirmed

Confirmed

Sony officially launched the A7R VI on May 13, 2026, at a US launch price of $4,499.99, undercutting earlier leaks that had suggested a price closer to $4,999.

Confirmed

The camera ships with an approximately 66.8-effective-megapixel back-illuminated, fully-stacked Exmor RS CMOS sensor paired with Sony's new BIONZ XR2 processing engine, the same processor used in the flagship A1 II.

Confirmed

Confirmed capabilities include blackout-free continuous shooting at up to 30fps, up to 60 AF/AE calculations per second, 8K30p oversampled video, 6.4K and 4K oversampled recording, and 4K120p for high frame rate capture.

What's reported (analyst / supply-chain sourced)

Reported

CineD and DPReview both frame the A7R VI as effectively merging Sony's resolution flagship and speed flagship into one body, since the stacked sensor architecture gives it roughly 5.6x faster readout than the outgoing A7R V.

Reported

NZ retail pricing has not yet been independently confirmed by Twisti at the time of writing. Given typical currency conversion and import patterns for Sony's Alpha line, expect local pricing to sit meaningfully above a straight USD-to-NZD conversion once GST and distributor margins are factored in.

Twisti's opinion

Opinion

At $4,499.99 USD before any NZ markup, the A7R VI sits firmly in professional territory, and the merged resolution-plus-speed pitch is genuinely compelling if you shoot both landscapes and fast-moving subjects. If that price is out of reach, the outgoing A7R V is likely to see solid discounting on Twisti now that its successor has shipped, and it remains an excellent 61-megapixel body in its own right.

What we'll update

We'll add confirmed NZ retail pricing the moment local Sony dealers list the A7R VI, and we'll track any early hands-on reviews that test the claimed autofocus improvements in real shooting conditions.
