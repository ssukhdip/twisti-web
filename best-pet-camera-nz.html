Best Pet Camera NZ: Which One to Buy

Pet cameras range from a basic Wi-Fi camera to a treat-flinging, two-way-talking hub with a monthly subscription. Here's what's genuinely useful and what's paying for features you'll use twice.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Two-way audio and treat dispensing: useful, but check the details

Two-way audio lets you talk to your pet through the camera's speaker, which can genuinely help with separation anxiety in dogs — hearing your voice sometimes calms a stressed dog during the first hour after you leave. It's less consistently useful for cats, who often ignore it entirely. Treat-dispensing cameras add a mechanism that flings a treat on command from the app; this works well for training reinforcement but the treat capacity is usually small (enough for a day or two), meaning you're refilling it regularly if you use it often.

Before paying extra for these features, think about whether you'd actually use them daily. A camera that just lets you check in visually, without audio or treats, covers most people's actual need: confirming the dog hasn't destroyed the couch, or checking a cat got home through the cat door.

Cloud storage subscriptions — read the fine print

Most pet cameras with motion-triggered recording only store footage in the cloud with a paid subscription; without it, you typically only get a live view and maybe a short local clip buffer. If you want to look back at footage from earlier in the day (did the dog get into the rubbish at 2pm or 4pm?), check what's included free versus what needs a monthly plan before you buy — some brands are generous with free storage, others gate almost everything behind a subscription.

Genuine separation anxiety monitoring vs just watching

If your actual goal is understanding whether your dog has separation anxiety — pacing, barking, destructive behaviour when alone — a camera with motion and sound alerts sent to your phone is more useful than one you only check occasionally out of curiosity. Set alerts up properly and actually review a full session at least once; a lot of owners buy a pet camera, get notified constantly by minor movement, and turn alerts off within a week, defeating the purpose.

What to check before buying

  • Field of view and mounting — a wide-angle lens covering the main room matters more than a narrow high-zoom one for most households.
  • Night vision quality — useful if you want to check on a pet overnight or in a dim room.
  • Local vs cloud storage — some models support a local SD card as a subscription-free alternative to cloud recording.
  • App notification tuning — check you can adjust motion sensitivity, or you'll be flooded with alerts for every shadow.
  • NZ Wi-Fi reliability — cameras that rely entirely on cloud processing can lag or drop out on slower home connections; check reviews for connection stability.
