Should You Buy Last Year's Flagship? The NZ Money-Saving Case

When a new iPhone, Galaxy or MacBook lands, the outgoing model usually gets quietly discounted at JB Hi-Fi, Noel Leeming and PB Tech - and for most people the difference in daily use is smaller than the price gap.

Twisti guides · Updated August 2026

Why last year's model gets cheaper

NZ retailers need shelf and warehouse space for the new model, so the previous generation typically gets marked down the moment a successor is announced - often well before the new one is even in stock. Manufacturers do the same thing on their own sites, quietly dropping the RRP on the outgoing model rather than letting it sit at full price next to something newer.

The size of the discount depends on category. Phones and laptops usually see the biggest cuts, since the yearly refresh cycle is aggressive and resale value drops fast. TVs and appliances move more slowly, so last year's model might only be a little cheaper - but it's also less likely to have been meaningfully improved on anyway.

Where the savings are real

Phones

The jump between consecutive flagship generations is frequently a modest camera or chip upgrade rather than anything most people will notice day to day. Buying the previous-gen flagship instead of the newest one is one of the most reliable ways to save meaningfully on a phone in NZ, particularly once the new model has been out for a month or two and stock of the old one needs clearing.

Laptops

Chip generations matter more here than with phones, but a previous-gen Intel or AMD chip is still plenty capable for browsing, office work and most everyday tasks. If you're not doing video editing, heavy gaming or serious multitasking, the older chip won't be the bottleneck - the RAM and SSD size will matter far more than which generation the processor is from.

Headphones, earbuds and smart watches

These categories often get yearly refreshes that are mostly software or minor hardware tweaks. Last year's noise-cancelling earbuds or smart watch usually do 90% of what the new one does, for noticeably less.

When it's not worth it

  • Software support windows are shrinking. If you plan to keep the device five-plus years, check how many more years of OS and security updates the older model has left before buying it purely to save money now.
  • Battery health starts on day one. A "new" older-generation phone that's been sitting in a warehouse for a year still has a battery that's aged from manufacture, not from purchase - it's a minor factor, but worth knowing.
  • Genuine feature jumps happen occasionally. Some years bring a real step change - a much better camera sensor, a significant battery life improvement, or a new port standard. When that happens, the newest model can be worth the extra spend even if it costs more.
  • Warranty timing. Manufacturer warranties in NZ typically run from the original sale date, not the manufacture date, so this usually isn't a real downside - but it's worth confirming with the retailer if you're buying old stock at a steep discount.

How to actually do this without overpaying

Watch for the announcement of the new model in your category, then track the price of the outgoing one over the following few weeks - that's when NZ retailers tend to move on price to clear stock. Don't assume the first "clearance" tag is the best price; discounts on outgoing models often step down further as launch day for the replacement gets closer.

Quick check before buying: compare the previous-gen price against the newest model's price side by side, and ask what the extra cost of the new one is actually buying you. If you can't name a specific feature you'll use, the older model is probably the better deal.

Quick FAQ

Is last year's flagship still "new"?

Usually yes - most stock sold as last year's model in NZ retail is unopened, unused stock, just discounted to move it before or after a successor launches. It isn't the same as buying refurbished or used.

How much can you realistically save?

It varies by category and timing, but discounts of 15-30% off the original launch price are common for previous-gen phones and laptops once a successor has launched, and can be steeper during clearance events like Boxing Day or Black Friday.

Does this work for every category?

It's most reliable for phones, laptops, tablets and wearables where there's a predictable yearly or near-yearly refresh. It's less useful for categories like TVs or major appliances, where model years matter less and genuine year-on-year improvements can be bigger.
