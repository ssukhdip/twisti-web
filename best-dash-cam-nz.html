Best Dash Cam NZ

Insurance claims after a crash go a lot smoother with footage. Here's what's actually worth fitting to your car in New Zealand, from budget 1080p units to 4K dual-channel setups with parking mode.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
How we picked these: Based on current NZ retailer listings (PB Tech, Hyper Drive, PriceSpy NZ) and specs from manufacturers, favouring brands with actual NZ stock and support rather than obscure AliExpress-only units.

Best overall: Viofo A229 Pro

Viofo has quietly become the enthusiast pick for dash cams sold into NZ, and the A229 Pro (front, or front-and-rear dual-channel) shoots proper 4K at the front with a Sony sensor that holds up reasonably well at night — the weak point of most budget cams. NZ pricing through specialists like Hyper Drive typically lands around $450-$550 depending on single or dual-channel configuration. It supports parking mode with a hardwire kit (sold separately, budget another $40-60), which keeps recording motion-triggered footage while the car is parked and off — the feature you actually want if your car gets hit in a car park.

Best all-round: Nextbase 622GW

Nextbase is the most recognisable name in dash cams in NZ, sold through PB Tech and listed on PriceSpy NZ around the $450-$500 mark. The 622GW shoots 4K at 30fps with image stabilisation that noticeably smooths footage over rough NZ roads, and it has an emergency SOS feature that can automatically alert emergency contacts after detecting a serious impact — genuinely useful on rural highways with patchy signal. Nextbase's companion app and cloud backup are also more polished than most competitors.

Best value: 70mai A500S

70mai's A500S is the budget pick worth actually buying, at roughly $169 in NZ. It shoots 1944p (a step up from standard 1080p) and includes built-in GPS for speed and location logging, which matters if you ever need to prove where and how fast you were going. It lacks the low-light polish of the Viofo or Nextbase units, but for a daily commuter car where you mainly want basic accident evidence, it covers the essentials without the premium price.

Parking mode: is it worth paying for?

If you street-park in central Auckland, Wellington or any dense NZ suburb, parking mode is close to essential — it's the feature that catches the driver who backs into your bumper and leaves. It requires a hardwire kit that taps into your car's fuse box for constant low-draw power, which is a separate purchase and roughly a 30-minute install (or a job for an auto electrician if you'd rather not touch your fuse box). Budget cams without parking mode are fine if your car lives in a garage or driveway.

NZ legal considerations

Dash cams are legal in NZ and footage is commonly accepted by insurers and, in some cases, Police as evidence after a crash. The main rule to know: your dash cam mount must not obstruct your view of the road, which in practice means mounting it low behind the rearview mirror, not stuck in the middle of the windscreen. If your cam has audio recording and you're using it to record passengers or rideshare trips, be aware NZ's Privacy Act principles around covert recording can apply — telling passengers it's recording is the safe approach.

Resolution: what actually matters

1080p is the minimum to reliably read number plates in daylight. 1440p/1944p adds meaningful clarity, especially useful for reading plates at a distance or in poor weather. True 4K (like the Viofo A229 Pro or Nextbase 622GW) is the most future-proof but eats through microSD storage fast — budget for a high-endurance 128GB-256GB card rated for continuous dash cam recording, not a standard consumer card, which will wear out quickly under constant overwrite cycles.
