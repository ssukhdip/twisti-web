How to time a big purchase around NZ sale cycles

Buying a TV or laptop the week before Black Friday is a classic mistake. Here's how NZ retail sale cycles generally run, so you can plan around them.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

NZ retail runs on a fairly predictable calendar

Electronics pricing in New Zealand isn't random — it clusters around a handful of recurring sale periods. Knowing roughly when these fall lets you avoid buying at full price right before a discount window opens, or conversely, avoid waiting indefinitely for a sale that won't beat today's price.

The main sale windows to know

  • Boxing Day through January: Post-Christmas clearance typically runs into the new year, often with strong deals on TVs, whiteware and last year's phone models.
  • Late November (Black Friday/Cyber Monday): Usually the single biggest discount period of the year across nearly every category, with many retailers extending it into a week or two of "Black Friday" promotions.
  • Mid-year sales: Many NZ retailers run winter or mid-year clearance events, often tied to stock clearing ahead of new model releases.
  • Back-to-school (late January to February): Laptops, tablets and headphones often see targeted deals aimed at students.
  • End of financial quarter: Retailers sometimes discount to hit sales targets — this is less predictable but worth watching if you're price-tracking closely.

Plan around release cycles too

Sale cycles aren't the only pattern that matters. Many product categories follow annual or near-annual refresh cycles — a new phone or tablet generation, for instance, often pushes the previous model's price down. If you don't need the newest version, buying just after a new model launches can sometimes get you a better price on the outgoing one than waiting for a generic sale event.

How to avoid buying at the wrong moment

The simplest approach is to track the price of the specific item you want over a few weeks rather than acting on the first price you see. If a major sale period (like Black Friday or Boxing Day) is within a month, it's often worth waiting unless the item is urgently needed or already unusually discounted. Outside of those windows, prices tend to move more slowly, so there's less to be gained from delaying a purchase indefinitely.

When timing doesn't matter as much

Not every category benefits equally from waiting. Fast-moving accessories, small items, and products with thin margins to begin with may not see meaningful discounts even during major sale events. For these, comparing across retailers right now is usually more useful than waiting for a calendar date.

Watch for fake urgency: Some "limited time" or countdown-timer promotions reset or repeat regularly. A price that's been "ending soon" for several weeks running is a sign the discount is closer to the everyday price than a genuine one-off event.
