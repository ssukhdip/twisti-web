How AI Is Changing Cybersecurity

AI is quietly running both sides of the fight — defending accounts and email, and making scams more convincing than ever. Here's what actually changed.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Where AI is genuinely helping

  • Spam and phishing filtering: email providers use AI models to catch phishing attempts based on patterns, not just keyword blocklists, catching more convincing fakes than older filters could.
  • Anomaly detection: banks and platforms use AI to flag unusual account activity — logins from new locations, atypical spending patterns — faster than manual review ever could.
  • Malware detection: modern antivirus and endpoint protection increasingly use behavioural AI models to catch new threats that don't match a known signature yet.

Where it's making things harder

The same AI tools that help defend also help attackers. Generative AI makes it cheap to produce highly convincing phishing emails without the spelling mistakes that used to be a giveaway, and voice/video deepfake tools have made impersonation scams (a "family member" or "colleague" asking for money) more believable than before.

What this means practically: the old advice to "watch for bad grammar" is far less reliable now. Verifying identity through a second channel — calling back on a known number, confirming with someone in person — matters more than ever for anything involving money or sensitive information.

The fundamentals still matter most

AI-powered filtering is a helpful extra layer, not a replacement for basic security hygiene: unique passwords or a password manager, two-factor authentication on anything important, and healthy scepticism toward unexpected requests — especially urgent ones involving money.
