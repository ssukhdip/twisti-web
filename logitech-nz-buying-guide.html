Logitech in NZ: The Complete Buying Guide

From office mice to gaming keyboards under the Logitech G badge, Logitech covers nearly every desk setup sold in New Zealand. Here's how to pick the right series instead of guessing.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Two brands under one roof

Logitech splits its range into two identities that rarely overlap on shelf placement: mainline Logitech, which covers productivity mice, keyboards, webcams and video conferencing gear, and Logitech G, the gaming sub-brand covering gaming mice, mechanical keyboards, headsets and simulation gear like racing wheels. Knowing which badge you're shopping under avoids paying gaming-grade prices for features an office setup doesn't need, or vice versa.

Picking a mouse for how you actually work

  • MX series — Logitech's premium productivity mice and keyboards, aimed at professionals who want multi-device switching, precise scrolling and long battery life.
  • M-series budget mice — reliable, no-frills wireless mice for everyday use, a common pick for home offices and students.
  • G-series gaming mice — lighter builds, higher-precision sensors and faster polling rates, priced for buyers who care about competitive gaming performance.

MX Master vs a budget wireless mouse

The MX Master line costs several times more than Logitech's basic wireless mice, and for good reason if you're on it eight hours a day — better ergonomics, faster scrolling, and seamless switching between multiple computers. If you just need reliable point-and-click for occasional use, a budget M-series mouse does the job without the premium, and the difference in day-to-day comfort will be far less noticeable at low usage.

Webcams and video conferencing gear

Logitech's webcam range (the C-series and Brio line) is a common recommendation for remote work in NZ, sitting above most laptop-integrated cameras on image quality. Higher-tier models add features like auto-framing and better low-light performance, which matter more if video calls are a daily part of your job rather than an occasional Zoom catch-up.

Gaming peripherals: what the G-series adds

Logitech G mice and keyboards are built around faster response times and more durable switches rated for a higher number of keystrokes or clicks than standard office gear. Wireless gaming mice in this range use low-latency connections designed to feel indistinguishable from wired for competitive play, though casual gamers may not notice the difference enough to justify the price gap over Logitech's standard wireless mice.

Software isn't always required. Logi Options+ (or the older G Hub for gaming products) unlocks custom button mapping and DPI settings, but most Logitech mice and keyboards work fine out of the box over Bluetooth or the included USB receiver without installing anything.

Where to buy for the best price

Logitech's core mice and keyboards are stocked almost everywhere in NZ, which means pricing is genuinely competitive between retailers — worth a quick comparison before checkout, especially on higher-priced MX and G-series gear where the dollar difference between retailers can be significant.
