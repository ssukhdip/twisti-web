Best Stick Vacuum NZ

The runtime on the box is almost never the runtime you get on max power. Here's what actually matters in a stick vacuum and which models are worth it in 2026.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

What actually matters when you're buying a stick vacuum

Cordless stick vacuums have basically replaced the barrel vacuum as the default choice for most Kiwi households, but the good ones and the disappointing ones look identical in a catalogue photo. Here's what separates them.

  • Runtime on max power: the headline runtime figure (often 60+ minutes) is almost always measured on the lowest suction setting. On max/boost mode, expect closer to 8-15 minutes - enough for a quick clean, not a whole house in one go unless you swap battery packs or work in eco mode.
  • Bin capacity and emptying: a small bin means more trips to the outside bin, and cheap ones can be genuinely unpleasant to empty without getting dust on your hands. Look for a clean push-button ejector.
  • Weight and balance: a stick vacuum you're holding overhead to do curtains or stairs needs to be light and well-balanced, not just light on paper. Handle-heavy designs feel worse in the hand than the spec sheet suggests.
  • Filtration and hair tangle: HEPA filtration matters if anyone in the house has allergies. Anti-tangle brush heads are worth paying for if you have long hair or pets - the difference in weekly maintenance is significant.
  • Attachments: a decent stick vacuum kit includes a mini motorised head for upholstery and stairs, a crevice tool, and ideally a flexible hose attachment for skirting boards and car interiors.

Dyson V15 Detect

The laser dust-detection gimmick is more useful than it sounds - it genuinely reveals how much fine dust is on hard floors that you'd otherwise miss. Sitting around $898 at Noel Leeming, the V15 remains the benchmark for suction power and build quality in the premium bracket. It's the safe, expensive choice if you want the best all-round performance and don't mind paying Dyson prices for it.

Dyson Gen5detect / Gen5outsize

Dyson's newer Gen5 range pushes suction and filtration further again, with the Outsize variant aimed squarely at bigger homes and bigger bins so you're not emptying every five minutes. This sits at the top of the price ladder - expect four figures - and is really only worth the jump over the V15 if bin capacity or filtration (fine particle allergy sufferers) is a genuine priority for you.

Shark stick vacuums (Anti Hair Wrap range)

Shark has carved out a strong mid-range position in NZ, usually landing somewhere between $400-$700 depending on the model, with anti-hair-wrap brush tech that's a genuine point of difference for households with pets or long hair. Suction isn't quite Dyson-level but for most hard-floor-and-rug homes it's more than adequate, and the flexible "hose mode" some models offer is genuinely handy for stairs and car interiors.

Ecovacs and other budget cordless sticks

Ecovacs and a handful of other brands have moved into the sub-$400 stick vacuum space, trading some runtime and suction for a genuinely low price. These suit renters, small apartments, or a second vacuum kept upstairs - not the primary tool for a large family home with carpet throughout.

Electrolux / AEG cordless range

A solid middle ground that's often overlooked next to Dyson and Shark - decent suction, quieter operation than most competitors, and typically priced in the $500-$800 band at Noel Leeming and Harvey Norman. Worth cross-shopping if you want less brand premium for similar real-world performance.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Judging by advertised runtime: always check the max-power runtime figure specifically, not the headline "up to X minutes" number which is almost always eco mode.
  • Ignoring battery replacement cost: after 2-3 years most stick vacuum batteries degrade noticeably. Check whether the battery is user-replaceable and what a new one costs before you buy.
  • Skipping the wall mount: most stick vacuums come with a wall-mounted charging dock. If you don't have a sensible spot to install one, charging becomes a hassle and the vacuum ends up living on the floor.
  • Overbuying for a small space: if you live in an apartment with mostly hard floors, a $900 flagship is solving a problem you don't have. Save the money.
Got a robot vacuum already? A stick vacuum still earns its keep for stairs, car interiors, spills, and the spots robots can't reach - the two aren't really competing for the same job. See our Dyson vs robot vacuum breakdown if you're trying to choose just one.
