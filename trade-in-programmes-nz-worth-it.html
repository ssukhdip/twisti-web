Trade-in programmes in NZ: are they actually worth it?

Trading in your old phone or laptop towards a new one is fast and easy — but easy isn't always the same as good value. Here's how to work out which is which.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

How retailer trade-in schemes work

Most major NZ electronics retailers, along with Apple and some phone carriers, run trade-in programmes where you get an instant valuation for an eligible device and the value is deducted from your new purchase, or occasionally paid out as store credit or a gift card. The appeal is obvious: no listing photos, no dealing with strangers, no waiting for a buyer, and the transaction happens in the same visit as your new purchase.

Where trade-in value tends to fall short

The convenience comes at a cost. Trade-in valuations are typically set to leave the retailer margin for refurbishing and reselling the device, which generally means the amount offered sits below what you could get selling privately through a marketplace, especially for well-kept, popular models in high demand. The gap tends to be widest for higher-value devices like recent-generation phones and laptops, where private buyers are willing to pay closer to market rate.

Trade-in vs private sale

Trade-in wins on speed, certainty and zero effort — you get a quoted price on the spot and it's immediately applied to your purchase, with no risk of a buyer messing you around. Private sale (via marketplace platforms) usually nets more money for devices in good condition, particularly newer or higher-demand models, but takes longer, involves more effort (photos, listing, meeting buyers or shipping), and carries some risk of a difficult buyer or payment issue. If your device is older, has cosmetic damage, or you value your time highly, trade-in often makes more sense despite the lower payout.

When trade-in makes more sense

  • Your device is old enough, or in poor enough condition, that private sale demand and price would be low anyway.
  • You want to walk out of the store with your new purchase today and don't want the hassle of managing a private sale alongside it.
  • The retailer is running a promotional trade-in bonus (temporarily boosted valuations) that closes or narrows the usual gap to private sale value.

Getting the most from a trade-in

Always get quotes from more than one retailer or trade-in service before accepting the first offer — valuations for the same device can vary meaningfully between providers. Also factor in condition honestly: valuations are usually provisional until the device is physically inspected, and a lower final offer after inspection is common if the device has damage not disclosed upfront.

Wipe your data properly first: Whichever route you choose, back up and factory reset your device, and remove it from any account lock (like Find My iPhone or a Google account) before handing it over. Trade-in providers and private buyers alike will reject or heavily discount a device still locked to a previous owner's account.
