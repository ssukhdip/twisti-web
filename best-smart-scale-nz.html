Best Smart Scale NZ: Which One to Buy

Smart scales are genuinely accurate at one thing — weight — and much less accurate at the body composition numbers that make up most of the marketing. Here's how to read the results properly.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Weight is accurate. Body fat percentage is an estimate.

Every smart scale on the market measures weight the same way a normal bathroom scale does, with a load cell, and that number is reliable. Body fat percentage, muscle mass, water percentage and bone mass, on the other hand, are calculated using bioelectrical impedance analysis (BIA) — a tiny electrical current passed through your feet, with the resistance used to estimate body composition based on population averages for your height, weight, age and sex.

BIA readings can shift several percentage points based on how hydrated you are, whether you've just eaten, exercised, or showered, and even the time of day. They're reasonably useful for spotting a trend over weeks or months if you measure under consistent conditions (same time of day, similar hydration), but a single reading shouldn't be treated as a precise medical measurement — it isn't one.

What smart scales are genuinely good for

Tracking trend direction over time is where smart scales earn their keep. If your weight and body fat estimate are both trending down over eight weeks of consistent measurement, that's meaningful even if any single day's body fat number is off by a few percent. Most scale apps plot this trend automatically, which is more useful than the daily number on its own.

App ecosystem matters more than the scale itself

Because the value of a smart scale is mostly in tracking trends over time, the app you're locked into matters a lot. Some scales sync cleanly with Apple Health or Google Fit, letting you see weight alongside other health data in one place. Others push you into a proprietary app with its own graphs and, in some cases, a subscription for extra features. Check what the app requires and whether it exports data before buying — losing months of tracked data because you switch brands later is a genuine annoyance.

What to check before buying

  • Multi-user support — for household use, check the scale can distinguish between different people, usually via the app or by recognising different weight ranges.
  • Health app integration — Apple Health, Google Fit or Fitbit sync saves you from being locked into one app long-term.
  • Battery type — some use replaceable coin batteries, others rechargeable; replaceable is generally more convenient long-term.
  • Surface compatibility — BIA readings need bare feet and a hard floor; carpeted bathrooms can cause connection issues with some models.
  • Pregnancy and medical device warnings — BIA scales are generally not recommended for pregnant users or those with pacemakers; check the manual.
Bottom line: if you only care about weight, any smart scale will do that part accurately. If you want body composition tracking, treat the numbers as a trend indicator, not a lab-grade measurement.
