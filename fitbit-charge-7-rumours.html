Fitbit Charge 7 Rumours: Everything We Know

Unlike most of the devices in this rumour series, this one starts with a device that's actually shipped. Google launched the Fitbit Air in May 2026, quieting years of speculation about whether Fitbit was being wound down. What hasn't shipped — or been named, or confirmed in any official capacity — is a Fitbit Charge 7. Here's where the line between the two sits.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
Why we're labelling this so carefully: Twisti's whole value is showing you real, current prices — not guesses. None of the pricing below is confirmed. We're publishing it because people are genuinely searching for this information already, but we'd rather be upfront that it's industry speculation than let a rumour read like a fact.

What's confirmed

Confirmed

Google officially unveiled the Fitbit Air on May 7, 2026, opening pre-orders in the UK at £84.99, with stock arriving in stores from May 26, 2026. It's a screenless, lightweight fitness tracker aimed at people who want everyday health tracking without the bulk, notifications, or price tag of a full smartwatch. This is the first major new piece of Fitbit hardware to actually reach customers in 2026.

Confirmed

Google has publicly stated that more new Fitbit hardware is coming during 2026, as reported by both Android Central and Tom's Guide. That statement came amid genuine public doubt about Fitbit's future within Google, so it's worth treating as a real commitment rather than routine marketing filler — Google made a point of saying it. It also means Fitbit Air is very likely the opening move of the year, not the whole story.

What's reported (analyst / supply-chain sourced)

Reported

No device called "Fitbit Charge 7" has been named or confirmed by Google or Fitbit. According to Smartwatch Insight, everything beyond the specs of the current Charge 6 — the last confirmed tracker in that line — is speculation pieced together from leaks, patent filings, and community analysis. Anyone showing you a "Charge 7 spec sheet" right now is showing you a guess dressed up as a leak.

Reported

The most consistent theory, referenced by both Smartwatch Insight and Android Central, is that Fitbit's lineup is splitting into two distinct products rather than one replacing the other. Fitbit Air would stay as the screenless, everyday tracker; a Charge 7 would sit above it as a more active-fitness-focused band with built-in GPS, aimed at runners and cyclists who currently have to step up to a full smartwatch just to get location tracking.

Reported

Community discussion cited by Android Central draws the comparison to how Apple runs the Watch SE alongside the Watch Series line — different price points and feature sets for different users, sold in parallel rather than one making the other obsolete. If Fitbit is heading the same direction, Charge 7 would be Fitbit's answer to the GPS-and-training-metrics crowd, while Air stays the low-fuss option.

Reported

Because nothing beyond that positioning theory has surfaced — no leaked renders, no battery figures, no confirmed release window — we're not going to invent pricing or a launch date here. Any number you see elsewhere claiming to be a "Charge 7 price" this early is not sourced to anything Google or Fitbit has said.

Twisti's opinion

Opinion

We think the Air/Charge split theory is the most sensible reading of what Google's actually done so far, not just what people want to believe. Shipping a screenless, no-GPS tracker first and reserving a GPS-equipped band for later is a logical way to cover two different customer bases without cannibalising either one. That said, "logical" isn't the same as "confirmed" — Google could just as easily fold GPS into a future Air variant instead of launching a whole new Charge generation. Until there's an actual product name from Google, we'd treat Charge 7 as a placeholder for "the next active-fitness tracker," not a real device with real specs.

What we'll update

We'll revise this page the moment Google names an actual successor to the Charge 6, or confirms specs, pricing, or a release date for anything positioned as a GPS-focused tracker. Until then, the only Fitbit hardware you can actually buy from this generation is the Fitbit Air.
