Best Standing Desk NZ

A decent electric standing desk is one of the few home office purchases with real evidence behind it. Here's what's worth buying in NZ, and what separates a $200 desk from a $1,200 one.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
How we picked these: Based on current NZ retail listings from Dick Smith, Kogan NZ, Mighty Ape and Hercules Store, cross-checked against manufacturer specs. No paid placements.

Best budget: Ergolux Electric Standing Desk

Ergolux is the volume budget brand stocked through Dick Smith and Kogan NZ, with basic electric sit-stand desks regularly priced between $109 and $319 depending on size, finish and current promotions — genuine "don't pay" discounts of 20-30% are common outside full price periods. These are single-motor desks, meaning lift speed and weight capacity are more modest than dual-motor units, but for a home office monitor-and-laptop setup they're a perfectly serviceable way into a standing desk without spending big.

Best all-round: Dual-motor electric desks (Ergolux L-Shape / Hercules Pro Dual)

Dual-motor desks lift more evenly (less side-to-side wobble at height) and handle heavier setups — think dual monitors, a monitor arm, and a desktop tower. Hercules Store's Pro Dual electric standing desk frames pair with a range of desktop sizes and finishes, and Ergolux also sells a dual-motor L-shaped option for corner setups, which is worth it if you want a proper wraparound desk that still lifts smoothly. Expect to pay meaningfully more than the single-motor budget desks above, generally sitting in the mid-hundreds once a desktop is included.

What actually matters when buying

  • Weight capacity — single-motor desks typically handle around 70-80kg; dual-motor desks often push higher. Add up your monitor(s), arm, PC and accessories before assuming a budget desk will cope.
  • Memory presets — the ability to save 2-4 height positions and jump straight to them with one button. Genuinely useful if multiple people share a desk, or you switch between sitting and standing height often through the day.
  • Height range — check the minimum height suits you if you're on the shorter side; some budget desks don't go as low as taller desks do, which matters more than the maximum height for most buyers.
  • Anti-collision sensors — the desk stops or reverses if it hits an obstacle while rising. Worth having if kids or pets are around, or the desk sits near shelving.

Do you need a premium desk?

Premium desks (like US-market UPLIFT desks, which offer 15-year warranties and up to 355lb dual-motor lift capacity) aren't broadly available new through mainstream NZ retail, and importing one adds shipping and warranty complications that usually aren't worth it for a home office. For most NZ buyers, a mid-range dual-motor desk from a locally warrantied brand is the better bet — you get 90% of the stability and features without the import hassle.
