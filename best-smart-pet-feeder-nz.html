Best Smart Pet Feeder NZ: Which to Buy

Automatic pet feeders promise precise portions on a fixed schedule, but accuracy and reliability vary a lot between models — and multi-pet households have a specific problem most feeders don't solve.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Portion control accuracy is usually good, but not perfect

Most smart feeders dispense dry food by rotating a measured compartment or using an auger, and for consistent kibble size, they're generally accurate to within a reasonable margin of the set portion. Accuracy drops with irregularly shaped kibble, very small portions, or food with a lot of dust and crumb that can jam the mechanism over time. If your pet needs precise portion control for a medical reason, it's worth weighing a few dispensed portions on a kitchen scale during the first week to confirm the feeder is actually delivering what you set.

Scheduling reliability — the thing that actually matters day to day

The core promise of a smart feeder is that it fires on schedule even when you're not home, and most do this reliably as long as they have power and, for Wi-Fi models, a stable connection. Battery backup is worth checking for — if your power goes out, a feeder without battery backup will simply miss the next scheduled feed until power returns, which matters if you're relying on it while away for a few days. Wi-Fi dependency is also worth considering: a feeder that needs an internet connection to trigger a schedule is less resilient than one that stores the schedule locally and only uses Wi-Fi for remote manual triggers and notifications.

Multi-pet households: the real limitation

Standard smart feeders serve one bowl, which is fine for a single pet but doesn't stop a food-motivated dog from eating a cat's portion, or one cat bullying another away from the bowl. Some feeders use microchip or RFID-collar recognition to only open for a specific pet, which solves this properly but costs more and requires your pets to wear a compatible tag. If you have multiple pets with different dietary needs, this is worth the extra cost — a standard single-bowl feeder won't actually solve the portion-stealing problem.

What to check before buying

  • Battery backup — essential if you're relying on the feeder during a power cut or while travelling.
  • Hopper capacity — larger capacity means fewer refills, useful if you're away for a few days at a time.
  • Wet food compatibility — most smart feeders are built for dry kibble only; wet food needs a different, usually simpler, timed feeder.
  • Cleaning ease — check how easily the hopper and dispensing mechanism come apart for cleaning, especially for oily kibble.
  • App notification reliability — notifications for low food or jams are only useful if they consistently reach your phone.
Before you buy: if you're leaving pets home alone for more than a day or two, don't rely solely on a smart feeder without a backup plan — a pet sitter or neighbour checking in still matters in case of a mechanical fault or power issue.
