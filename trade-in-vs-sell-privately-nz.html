Trade-In vs Selling Privately NZ

Retailer trade-in schemes promise convenience; private selling promises a better price. Here's what each actually involves before you box up your old phone or laptop.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
No single answer: Which route wins depends on the device, its condition, and how much your time is worth. This isn't a case where one option is objectively better — it's a genuine trade-off.

How retailer trade-in works in NZ

Noel Leeming runs a trade-in scheme (delivered in partnership with refurbishment company Moorup) where you get an instant, upfront gift card valuation online or in-store for your old device, which you then post in or drop off; a credit card pre-authorisation hold is taken in case the device doesn't match the described condition on arrival. JB Hi-Fi NZ runs a comparable trade-in programme through its own JB Trade-in page, also issuing store credit rather than cash.

The core trade of retailer schemes: you get a known, guaranteed value upfront (assuming your description of the device's condition is accurate), it's fast, and there's no listing, no messaging back and forth with strangers, and no risk of a no-show or scam buyer. The trade-off is that the payout is store credit, not cash, and retailer trade-in valuations are typically set to leave margin for refurbishment and resale — so they tend to sit below what a motivated private buyer would pay for the same item in good condition.

How Trade Me and Facebook Marketplace compare

Selling privately on Trade Me or Facebook Marketplace generally returns a higher raw dollar figure for popular, in-demand items (recent-model phones, gaming consoles, drones) because you're selling at closer to genuine second-hand market value rather than a wholesale trade-in rate. Trade Me also charges success fees on completed sales, which eat into the final return, and photographing, listing, answering buyer questions, and arranging safe handover (or courier) all take real time.

Private selling also carries the most risk: no-show buyers, lowball negotiation, payment disputes, and — particularly on Facebook Marketplace — scam attempts. Meeting in a safe public location (many NZ police stations have designated "safe exchange" zones) is standard advice for higher-value electronics sales.

A practical way to decide

  • High-value, in-demand item (recent iPhone, DJI drone, PS5) — private selling usually nets meaningfully more, and demand means it should sell quickly, so the extra effort is worth it.
  • Older, lower-value, or awkward-to-ship item — trade-in convenience often outweighs the modest cash difference, especially if you're using the credit toward a new purchase at the same retailer anyway.
  • You need cash, not store credit — private selling is the only option, since retailer trade-in schemes pay in gift cards/credit, not bank transfer.
  • You want zero risk and zero hassle — trade-in wins outright; the guaranteed quote removes negotiation and no-show risk entirely.

Before you trade in or list anything

Factory reset the device and sign out of iCloud/Google/Samsung accounts (a device still linked to an account is close to worthless to a buyer or trade-in scheme), keep original accessories and box if you have them since it measurably affects value on both routes, and get an independent trade-in quote from at least two retailers before accepting the first one, as valuations can differ.
