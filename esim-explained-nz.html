eSIM Explained for NZ Shoppers

No plastic card, no SIM tray fiddling — eSIM is now standard on most new phones. Here's how it actually works and which NZ carriers support it.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
How we picked these: Carrier support details are drawn from Spark, One NZ, and 2degrees' published eSIM help pages, cross-checked against device spec sheets from Apple and Samsung.

What eSIM actually is

An eSIM (embedded SIM) is a small chip built into your phone that stores your mobile plan digitally instead of on a removable plastic card. Rather than inserting a physical SIM, you scan a QR code or use a carrier app to activate a plan, and the phone downloads the profile over the air. You can typically store multiple eSIM profiles at once and switch between them in settings, or run one alongside a physical SIM for true dual-SIM use.

The benefit isn't just convenience — eSIM makes it far easier to switch carriers, add a second number for work, or activate a temporary plan while travelling, all without hunting down a SIM pin or waiting for a card to arrive in the post.

Which phones support it

eSIM has been standard on iPhone since the XS in 2018, and Apple has sold eSIM-only models (no physical tray at all) in some markets since the iPhone 14. Most current iPhones sold in NZ, including the iPhone 16 and iPhone 17 ranges, support eSIM alongside a physical SIM slot. On Android, Samsung's Galaxy S and Z Fold/Flip series, Google Pixel phones from the Pixel 3 onward, and most flagship-tier phones from Oppo and Xiaomi support eSIM — though support is patchier on budget Android models, so it's worth checking the spec sheet before assuming.

NZ carrier support

Spark, One NZ, and 2degrees all support eSIM activation for both prepay and postpay plans, usually via a QR code you scan in-store or generate through their app. Porting an existing number onto an eSIM works the same way as porting to a physical SIM — you'll need your account number or a copy of a recent bill.

One practical note: if you're buying a new phone outright from Noel Leeming, JB Hi-Fi, or a carrier store, ask staff to set up the eSIM at the counter — it takes a few minutes and avoids fumbling with QR codes at home, especially useful if you're moving from an older phone with a physical SIM.

Travel eSIMs — the biggest everyday win

The most useful eSIM trick for NZ travellers is buying a short-term data eSIM for your destination before you fly, through apps like Airalo, Holafly, or your home carrier's roaming eSIM option. You land, your data connects automatically, and you keep your NZ number active on its own SIM or eSIM slot for calls and texts. A typical 10-day, 5GB regional data eSIM runs somewhere around NZ$25–45 depending on destination, which is usually cheaper than carrier roaming add-ons for the same period.

Because you can install a travel eSIM ahead of time and only activate it on arrival, there's no need to touch your phone's settings the moment you land, and no risk of losing a physical SIM card while swapping it out.

Things to check before you rely on eSIM

  • Carrier locking — some phones bought on contract may need unlocking before an eSIM from a different carrier will activate.
  • Device transfer limits — eSIM profiles are usually tied to one device; moving to a new phone means re-issuing the eSIM, not just copying a card across.
  • Dual-SIM battery impact — running two active SIMs (physical + eSIM) simultaneously can use slightly more battery than a single SIM.
