10 Smart Home Upgrades That Improve Security

Most of the meaningful security gains cost little or nothing — the hardware matters less than the habits around it. Here's what actually moves the needle, roughly in order of effort.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

"Smart home security" often gets marketed as a camera and a doorbell. Those help, but the biggest real-world improvements are usually the boring, free ones — passwords, updates, and network settings. Here's a practical list covering both.

  1. Replace default router passwords

    Every router ships with a default admin password, and plenty are never changed. This is the single highest-impact, zero-cost fix on this list — do it first.

  2. Enable two-factor authentication

    On your router admin panel, smart home app accounts, and camera/doorbell apps specifically — 2FA turns a leaked password into a dead end instead of full access.

  3. Use WPA3 Wi-Fi where available

    If your router supports it, WPA3 is a meaningful step up from WPA2 in resisting password-guessing attacks. Most routers from the last few years support it — worth checking your settings.

  4. Create a guest Wi-Fi network

    Put smart home devices (plugs, bulbs, cheaper cameras) on a separate guest or IoT network rather than your main one. If a lower-quality device is ever compromised, it can't see your laptops or phones.

  5. Keep device firmware updated

    Security patches for routers, cameras and locks only help if they're actually installed. Turn on auto-update where the option exists, and check manually every few months where it doesn't.

  6. Install a smart video doorbell

    The most visible deterrent on this list — visitors and would-be intruders both know they're on camera. Look for local storage or a subscription plan you're actually willing to keep paying for; free tiers often only keep clips for a day or two.

  7. Install indoor and outdoor cameras

    Cover entry points and blind spots the doorbell can't see. Outdoor units need a genuine weatherproof rating, and placement matters more than resolution — an obstructed 4K camera sees less than a well-placed 1080p one.

  8. Use smart locks with unique access codes

    Unique codes per person (rather than one shared code) mean you can see who unlocked the door and when, and revoke a single person's access without rekeying anything.

  9. Add motion lighting

    Cheap, low-effort, and effective — motion-activated lighting around entry points removes the cover of darkness, which is one of the most consistent deterrents in home security research.

  10. Back up important recordings

    If footage only lives on the camera or a single cloud tier, it can be the first thing lost or overwritten. Export anything that actually matters rather than assuming it'll still be there later.

Priority order if you're starting from zero: router password, 2FA, guest network, then hardware. The free steps meaningfully reduce risk before you spend a cent on cameras or locks.
