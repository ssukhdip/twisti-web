Best Security Camera NZ: Ring, Nest, Eufy or Tapo

What local versus cloud storage really costs over time, and five named cameras that suit different budgets and habits.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

The decision that matters more than the brand

Before comparing models, settle one question: do you want local storage or cloud storage. This decides almost everything else about cost and privacy. Cloud-based systems (Ring, Google Nest) store your footage on the company's servers, which usually means a monthly subscription (typically $8 to $20 a month per camera or as a whole-home plan) if you want more than a few hours of event history -- but you get remote viewing, smart alerts and no hardware to manage beyond the camera itself. Local-storage systems (Eufy being the best known) keep footage on a home base station or microSD card with no ongoing subscription required, which is cheaper long-term and keeps your footage off someone else's server, at the cost of a bit more setup and no easy off-site backup if your house is broken into and the base station is taken too.

The second big decision is battery versus wired. Battery cameras go up almost anywhere in minutes and suit renters, but you're recharging or swapping batteries every few months depending on activity. Wired cameras need a power point or some cabling, but they run forever and usually have better night vision and no risk of missing footage because the battery died at the wrong moment.

The picks

TP-Link Tapo C210 (indoor pan & tilt) -- around $40 to $50

The cheapest genuinely good camera on this list. 2K resolution, pan and tilt so one camera covers more of a room, and works with a microSD card for free local storage with no subscription. No battery to manage since it's wired. Suits: watching the living room, keeping an eye on pets, a baby monitor stand-in, or a first camera for someone testing the waters.

TP-Link Tapo C225 or similar outdoor Tapo model -- around $85 to $110

Weatherproof, colour night vision, and again works entirely on local microSD storage if you want to skip subscriptions altogether. The app isn't quite as polished as Ring's or Nest's, and smart alerts (person vs vehicle vs animal detection) are a bit less refined, but for the price it's an easy way to cover a front door or driveway without ongoing costs. Suits: budget-conscious households, holiday homes, anyone who wants outdoor coverage without committing to a subscription.

Ring Stick Up Cam (battery), single -- around $140 to $180

The most polished ecosystem for alerts, neighbourhood integration and easy remote viewing, plus optional professional monitoring if you want an alarm response service. The catch is Ring's useful features (video history beyond live view, person detection) sit behind a Ring Protect subscription, roughly $8 to $12 a month per camera or a bit more for a whole-home plan. Buy the camera cheaply, then do the maths on whether you'll actually pay the subscription for years. Suits: households wanting the most seamless app experience and are happy paying an ongoing fee for it.

Google Nest Cam (battery, outdoor/indoor) -- around $250 to $300

Excellent image quality and genuinely smart alerts (it can tell you a delivery driver left a parcel, not just "motion detected"), and integrates cleanly with Google Home and Nest doorbells if you're already in that ecosystem. Like Ring, the good stuff -- 30-day event history, familiar face alerts -- needs a Nest Aware subscription on top of the camera price. Suits: Google Home households who want the smartest alerts and don't mind the subscription cost.

Eufy 4-camera kit with HomeBase -- around $900 to $1,000

The best value if you're covering a whole house rather than one spot, because the per-camera cost drops a long way once you're buying a kit, and there's no subscription at all -- footage lives on the HomeBase's local storage. Image quality and app polish are a notch below Ring and Nest, and you're relying on the HomeBase staying safe (and powered) since that's where everything is stored. Suits: households wanting several cameras covering entry points without a recurring bill, and who are comfortable with local storage's slightly rougher edges.

Common mistake: buying a cheap camera and then discovering the useful features are locked behind a subscription you didn't budget for. Always check what's included free (usually just a live view and short motion clips) versus what needs a monthly plan (extended history, person/package detection, downloadable footage) before you commit to a brand. Privacy basics worth thinking about

  • Where's the footage actually stored -- cloud systems mean a company has a copy of what your camera sees; ask yourself if you're comfortable with that for the specific spots you're filming.
  • Two-factor authentication -- turn it on for any cloud camera account; compromised camera logins have been used to spy on households before.
  • Point cameras at your own property -- filming a neighbour's yard or windows, even accidentally, can create issues under NZ privacy law.

The bottom line

If you want one camera and don't want a subscription, the TP-Link Tapo C210 or its outdoor sibling is the easy, honest choice. If you want the smartest alerts and don't mind paying monthly, Ring or Nest are worth it. And if you're covering a whole house and want to avoid subscriptions for good, the Eufy multi-camera kit is the best value per camera on this list.
