Best Open-Ear Earbuds NZ: Which to Buy

Open-ear earbuds trade audio quality for awareness and comfort — that's the whole trade-off. Whether that's worth it depends entirely on how and where you actually use them.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Why anyone chooses open-ear over regular earbuds

Open-ear designs — whether true bone-conduction models that sit in front of your ear canal, or the newer clip-style open-ear buds that hook over your ear without sealing the canal — exist for one reason: they let ambient sound through. That matters if you run or cycle on roads and need to hear traffic, if you work in an office and want to stay aware of colleagues, or if standard in-ear buds are physically uncomfortable or cause ear canal irritation after long wear. It's a genuine, sensible trade-off for the right use case, not just a gimmick.

Sound quality: be honest with yourself about this trade-off

Neither bone-conduction nor open-ear clip designs will match a good pair of sealed in-ear buds for bass response or noise isolation, full stop. Bone-conduction in particular has a thin, mid-forward sound because it physically can't reproduce low bass the way an eardrum-facing driver can. Open-ear clip designs (like Shokz OpenFit-style buds) do noticeably better on bass than true bone-conduction models because they still use a small air-conduction driver, just without a seal. If music quality is your top priority, open-ear isn't the category for you — buy proper in-ears instead.

Open-ear vs sealed in-ear: who should buy which

Open-ear (bone-conduction or clip-style): runners/cyclists on roads, office workers who need situational awareness, anyone with ear canal sensitivity, people who wear glasses/sunnies and hate stacking bud + earhook.

Sealed in-ear: commuters wanting noise cancellation, anyone prioritising bass and detail, gym use where sweat sealing and isolation from gym noise matters more than street awareness.

Sound leakage is real — don't wear these in quiet shared spaces

Because open-ear designs don't seal the ear, sound leaks outward at higher volumes, and people sitting near you on a bus or in a quiet office will hear tinny audio bleeding out. It's not as bad as an old-school over-ear headphone leak, but it's noticeable past about 60% volume. If you're regularly in shared quiet spaces, keep volume low or accept that open-ear isn't the private-listening option sealed buds are.

Battery life note: Most open-ear clip designs run a similar 6-8 hour single-charge battery life to standard true wireless earbuds, with a charging case adding another two or three full charges — check this per model rather than assuming, as bone-conduction specifically tends to run shorter.

What to check before buying

  • Fit style — clip-style sits over the ear cartilage, bone-conduction sits against the temple; try both if you can, comfort is very individual.
  • IPX rating — look for at least IPX4 if you're running or cycling in NZ weather.
  • Multipoint Bluetooth — useful if you switch between phone and laptop through the day.
  • Stockist support — JB Hi-Fi and PB Tech both carry Shokz and other open-ear brands with proper NZ warranty backing, worth it over grey-import options.

Bottom line

Buy open-ear if situational awareness or comfort is genuinely important to how you use earbuds — running near traffic, long office days, sensitive ears. Buy sealed in-ears if sound quality and noise isolation matter more than anything else. Don't buy open-ear expecting it to sound like a premium sealed earbud, because it won't.
