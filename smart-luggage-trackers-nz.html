Smart Luggage & Bag Trackers: Worth Buying?

A $50 AirTag or a $300 suitcase with a tracker built in — which one actually gets your bag back after Air New Zealand loses it in Auckland? We looked at what really matters.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Why this matters more than it used to

Lost and delayed luggage hasn't gone away just because airlines got better software. Anyone who's stood at the Wellington or Christchurch baggage carousel watching it stop turning knows the sinking feeling. A tracker doesn't stop an airline losing your bag, but it tells you where it actually is instead of relying on a staff member reading a barcode scan from three airports ago.

Clip-on trackers: Apple AirTag, Samsung SmartTag, Tile

These are the cheap, flexible option. Drop one in a side pocket or zip it into a packing cube and you're using the same Find My or SmartThings network your phone already talks to. The advantage is scale — Apple's Find My network has an enormous number of iPhones passively relaying location, so even in a random regional airport overseas, there's a decent chance another iPhone nearby pings your AirTag's position.

The catch is precision. These trackers report "last seen near this location," not live GPS tracking through the whole journey. Once your bag is inside an airport's baggage system, in a container, or in a plane's hold, it usually goes dark until it's unloaded and someone with a phone walks past it.

Battery rules matter. AirTags and SmartTags use tiny coin-cell batteries (well under 0.3g of lithium content) and airlines including Air New Zealand, Qantas and Jetstar explicitly permit them in checked luggage. This is different from power banks and spare device batteries, which must go in carry-on only.

Built-in luggage trackers (GPS suitcases)

A handful of suitcase brands build a GPS/cellular tracker into the shell, giving you closer to live tracking rather than crowd-sourced pings. Sounds great until you read the fine print: many of these use a SIM-based cellular chip, which means a battery large enough to raise real airline concerns, and some models have been pulled from checked baggage lists entirely after airline pushback over lithium battery size. A few require you to remove the battery module before checking the bag in — at which point you've lost the entire point of the feature.

Clip-on tracker vs built-in GPS suitcase

  • Cost: AirTag/SmartTag ~$45–$60 one-off vs $250–$400+ for a tracker-equipped suitcase.
  • Airline compliance: coin-cell trackers are universally accepted; some built-in GPS units are restricted or need battery removal.
  • Accuracy: built-in GPS is more precise when it has signal; clip-on trackers rely on other people's phones being nearby.
  • Flexibility: a clip-on tracker moves between any bag you own; a built-in tracker is locked to one suitcase.

What we'd actually buy

For most NZ travellers, a clip-on AirTag or SmartTag in an existing bag beats a dedicated GPS suitcase. It's cheaper, it's unambiguous under airline lithium battery rules, and you're not stuck if your travel suitcase breaks or you switch to a backpack. iPhone users get the biggest practical benefit from AirTag's network size; Android users are better served by a Samsung SmartTag if they're on a Galaxy phone, or a Chipolo/Tile if not.

If you're comparing trackers or shopping for a new suitcase, check current pricing across PB Tech, JB Hi-Fi, Noel Leeming and Harvey Norman before you commit — the same AirTag four-pack can vary by a surprising amount between retailers, especially around sale periods.
