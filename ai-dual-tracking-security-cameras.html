AI Dual-Tracking Security Cameras Explained: The 2026 Shift

Single-lens security cameras have always had the same blind spot problem: once someone walks past the edge of the frame, you lose them. A new wave of two-lens cameras launched over the past year fixes that by having one camera hand a moving subject off to the other, mid-track.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
A note on this guide: Unlike our rumour roundups, this is settled, currently shipping technology — not speculation. Prices below are US retail figures we've verified via search and vary by retailer, promotion and region, so treat them as a guide rather than an exact NZD quote.

What's actually shipping

Baseus Security X1 Pro — the camera that kicked off the "dual-tracking" category.

Originally funded on Kickstarter in late 2025 and now sold on Amazon and Baseus's own site, the X1 Pro uses two independent 3K lenses in one housing, each capable of a 300-degree pan, that pass tracking of a moving person between them as they cross from one lens's field of view into the other's. It requires no subscription, stores locally (up to 512GB), and has been discounted from an MSRP around US$270 down to sale prices in the US$130–$160 range through mid-2026.

aosu T2 Pro — a dual-camera-in-one-body design built around a wider hub ecosystem.

Starting at US$199.99 on aosu's own site, the T2 Pro pairs two lenses in a single unit and is designed to work with aosu's Base and Cortex hub hardware for managing multiple cameras across a property, rather than being sold purely as a standalone unit.

Reolink TrackFlex Floodlight WiFi — a dual-lens floodlight camera that shows both feeds at once.

Launched 26 January 2026 at US$259.99, the TrackFlex combines a 4K wide-angle lens with a 6x hybrid zoom lens that locks onto and follows movement, displaying both the full-yard view and the zoomed tracking view simultaneously in the Reolink app. It also does local AI video search — typing a description like "man in a blue shirt" to find matching clips without uploading footage to the cloud. It picked up 19 "Best of IFA 2025" awards from outlets including TechRadar and BGR ahead of its January 2026 launch.

How dual-tracking actually works

The core problem these cameras solve is the "hand-off" — the moment a person walks out of one camera's field of view. On a traditional single-lens PTZ (pan-tilt-zoom) camera, the lens has to physically swivel to keep following someone, which is slow and can lose the subject entirely if they move quickly or duck behind an obstacle.

Dual-lens designs solve this differently. Instead of one lens trying to cover everything, two lenses with overlapping or complementary fields of view work together: as AI-based person detection tracks a subject moving across the scene, the system hands tracking duties to whichever lens currently has the best view, rather than relying on a single lens to mechanically catch up. In Baseus's X1 Pro, that's two similar 3K lenses covering different angles of a wide outdoor space. In Reolink's TrackFlex, it's a more specialised pairing — one wide fixed lens for context, one zoom lens that closes in on the moving subject — with both feeds shown together so you never lose the wider picture while watching the close-up.

The AI component matters as much as the extra lens. On-device person/vehicle/animal detection is what decides who's worth tracking in the first place, and increasingly it's also what powers local search — being able to type a plain-language description of a person or event and have the camera search its own locally stored footage, without a cloud subscription, is a feature Reolink and others are pushing specifically as a no-monthly-fee alternative to Ring- or Nest-style subscription models.

Twisti's opinion

Dual-tracking is a genuinely useful upgrade for larger sections of yard or driveway, but it's solving a real problem for a specific setup, not a must-have for every household.

If you've got a single wide open space — a driveway, a long backyard, a farm gate — the hand-off between lenses is a real improvement over a single PTZ camera losing someone at the edge of frame. If your property is smaller or already covered by two or three separate fixed cameras, a standard multi-camera setup will do largely the same job for less money. The no-subscription local storage model on most of these is the more universally appealing part — that's the bit we'd weigh heavily regardless of whether you need the dual-lens tracking specifically.
