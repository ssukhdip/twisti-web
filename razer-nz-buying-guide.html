Razer in NZ: The Complete Buying Guide

Razer sells the most expensive gaming laptops of any mainstream brand in NZ, but its peripherals compete on far more even footing with Logitech and SteelSeries. Here's how to shop the range sensibly.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
How we picked these: We compared Razer's official NZ-facing retail presence at PB Tech, along with Mighty Ape, JB Hi-Fi and Computer Lounge, the main NZ retailers carrying the brand's laptop and peripheral lines.

Razer Blade -- premium pricing, no real NZ competition at the top

Razer Blade gaming laptops are stocked at PB Tech and are priced well above Acer, ASUS and even most Legion configurations at equivalent specs -- PB Tech's current Blade listings run from roughly $4,520 NZD up to $5,199 NZD excluding GST for configurations pairing a Ryzen AI 9 processor with an RTX 5070 and a 16-inch 2560x1600 display. That premium buys a genuinely excellent CNC-milled aluminium chassis and one of the best trackpads and keyboards of any gaming laptop, but it's a real premium -- an ASUS ROG Zephyrus or Acer Predator Helios with comparable internals will typically cost 20-30% less in NZ. Blade is the right buy specifically if chassis build quality and portability matter as much to you as raw gaming performance; if you just want the fastest GPU for the money, look elsewhere first.

Razer mice and keyboards -- competitive, not overpriced

Unlike the laptop line, Razer's gaming mice and keyboards are priced competitively against Logitech and SteelSeries equivalents in NZ. PB Tech's Razer mouse range spans budget-friendly wireless options through to premium esports-grade mice, and Mighty Ape and JB Hi-Fi NZ both carry a solid chunk of the DeathAdder and Viper ranges, which remain two of the most widely recommended gaming mouse families among NZ PC gaming communities. Razer keyboards (BlackWidow mechanical range, plus smaller low-profile options) are similarly positioned in the mid-to-premium bracket, comparable in price to equivalent Corsair and SteelSeries mechanical keyboards.

Headsets

Razer's headset range (Kraken, BlackShark) is stocked at PB Tech, though specific current NZ pricing is worth checking directly since availability shifts more than mice and keyboards. Razer headsets compete directly with SteelSeries Arctis and Corsair HS/Virtuoso ranges at similar price points, and the deciding factor for most buyers tends to be comfort preference (Razer's ear cushion and headband design differs noticeably from SteelSeries) rather than a clear performance gap.

Where to buy

PB Tech has the broadest official Razer range in NZ across laptops, mice, keyboards and headsets. Mighty Ape NZ and JB Hi-Fi NZ both carry a solid peripheral selection and are worth price-checking against PB Tech, particularly for mice and keyboards where the retail margin differences show up more in day-to-day pricing than they do on laptops. Computer Lounge NZ is a smaller specialist retailer that also stocks Razer gear and occasionally has better availability on specific SKUs when the bigger retailers are out of stock.

Is Razer worth the premium?

For peripherals -- mice, keyboards, headsets -- yes, Razer is priced in line with equivalent competitors and the premium is mostly about aesthetics (RGB, branding) rather than a meaningful price gap. For laptops, the Blade line carries a genuine, substantial premium over Acer, ASUS and Lenovo at matching internals, and it's only worth paying if chassis quality and portability are priorities alongside gaming performance.
