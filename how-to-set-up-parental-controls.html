How to Set Up Parental Controls on Devices

Most phones, tablets, laptops and consoles now include built-in parental controls, so you often do not need extra software. Here is a practical rundown of where to find them and what they can do.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Phones and tablets

Both major mobile platforms have mature parental control systems built in. On iPhone and iPad, Screen Time (found in Settings) lets you set app limits, downtime schedules, content restrictions and purchase approvals, and it can be managed remotely for a child's device through Family Sharing. On Android, Google's Family Link app performs a similar role, letting a parent set daily screen time limits, approve app downloads and see rough location information from a connected parent account.

Laptops and computers

Windows includes Microsoft Family Safety, which covers screen time schedules, content filtering and activity reporting once a child's account is linked to a family group. On Mac, Screen Time carries over from iOS and works similarly across Apple devices signed into the same family group. Chromebooks generally rely on Family Link as well, keeping the setup broadly consistent with an Android phone.

Game consoles

PlayStation, Xbox and Nintendo Switch all offer their own family management tools, typically accessed through a companion app or web portal rather than the console itself. These usually cover playtime limits, spending limits tied to a linked payment method, and age-based content restrictions on games and online communication. Settings are generally similar in spirit across the three platforms, though the exact menus and app names differ.

What parental controls typically cannot do

It is worth being realistic about limitations. Content filters rely on age ratings and keyword matching, so they can miss content that is not clearly categorised, and a determined older child may find workarounds, particularly on a device they have had time to explore. Controls work best as one part of a broader approach rather than a complete solution on their own.

Note: Settings and menu names change fairly often as platforms update their software, so if a setting mentioned here does not match what you see on your device, check the platform's current support pages — the general approach (screen time, content filters, purchase approval) tends to stay consistent even when the interface moves around.

Getting started without overcomplicating it

For most families, setting up one platform-level tool (Family Link, Screen Time or Family Safety) covering the devices actually in use is enough to start. You can always add more granular restrictions later once you know what limits are actually needed day to day.
