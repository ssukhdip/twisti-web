Next-Gen RTX GPU Rumours: What We Know So Far

NVIDIA graphics cards are a big part of NZ's PC gaming and workstation market, and every generation launch brings a wave of speculation months ahead of anything official. Here's how to read the current rumour cycle.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

What NVIDIA has actually confirmed

NVIDIA rarely confirms specific next-generation GPU details before its own keynote announcements, typically at trade events. Ahead of any official reveal, the company generally limits public comment to broad statements about continued investment in AI and ray-tracing performance, without confirming architecture names, model numbers or release windows. Treat anything more specific than that as unofficial until NVIDIA says so directly.

What's being reported

Leaks in the GPU space typically come from board partner leaks, supply chain sources and data-mined driver files, and they tend to focus on a handful of recurring themes: a new manufacturing process node promising better performance-per-watt, increased VRAM on mid-tier and high-tier cards, and continued expansion of AI-upscaling features. Power draw and cooling requirements are also common speculation points, since recent high-end generations have pushed connector and case-cooling standards further than before.

Flagship vs mid-range: different rumour reliability

Leaks about flagship, halo-tier cards tend to firm up earliest, since board partners need lead time to design coolers for the biggest dies. Mid-range and budget card details usually stay vaguer for longer and are more prone to changing right up until launch, partly because NVIDIA adjusts specs based on competitive pressure from AMD closer to release. If you're budget-conscious, treat mid-range rumours as considerably less reliable than flagship ones this early.

What it could mean for NZ pricing

  • Launch pricing in NZD — NVIDIA's US pricing typically converts to NZ retail with local tax and import costs added, meaning NZD prices are consistently higher than a straight currency conversion would suggest.
  • Previous-generation discounts — a new GPU generation launch usually triggers price cuts on the outgoing generation at NZ retailers, which can be the better value play if you don't need the newest features.
  • Stock availability — high-demand flagship cards have historically sold out fast at NZ retailers around launch, with pricing sometimes climbing above RRP before settling.
Our take: Specific model names, VRAM configurations and prices circulating in rumour roundups are not confirmed by NVIDIA and should be treated as informed speculation at best. If you're planning a GPU purchase, the safest approach is to wait for NVIDIA's official announcement rather than holding out for a rumoured spec that may not eventuate as described.
