Best Soundbar NZ

TV speakers are thin and tinny almost by design. A soundbar is the single cheapest upgrade you can make to how your lounge sounds — here's what's actually worth buying in New Zealand right now, at every budget.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
How we picked these: We compared current NZ retail listings (Noel Leeming, Harvey Norman, JB Hi-Fi) against independent testing from RTINGS and Tom's Guide, and stuck to models actually stocked in NZ. No paid placements — if a bar isn't good value, we say so.

Best overall: Samsung HW-Q990F

Samsung's flagship 11.1.4-channel system (soundbar plus wireless subwoofer and rear speakers) is the one to beat if budget genuinely isn't a constraint. It's around $1,499 at Noel Leeming and delivers real Dolby Atmos height channels rather than the "virtual" upmixed version cheaper bars fake. Q-Symphony lets it sync with compatible Samsung TVs to use the TV's own speakers as an extra channel, which sounds better than it has any right to. If you've got a Samsung TV already, this is the obvious pairing.

Best all-round: Sonos Arc (or Arc Ultra)

The Sonos Arc remains the benchmark for people who want one really good bar without a subwoofer and satellite speakers cluttering the room, and it integrates cleanly with the rest of the Sonos ecosystem if you already have Sonos speakers elsewhere in the house. Sonos has since released the Arc Ultra, its new flagship with Sonos's "Sound Motion" bass tech built in so you may not need a separate sub at all — it's listed at Noel Leeming as a premium-tier bar, generally sitting above $1,500. The original Arc and the smaller Sonos Beam (Gen 2) are still sold and are worth checking for discounts now the Ultra is out; the Beam Gen 2 is the pick if your room is small and you don't need surround width.

Best value: LG SC9S / JBL Bar 500MK2

You don't need to spend four figures to get real Dolby Atmos. LG's SC9S (3.1.3 channel with genuine up-firing Atmos drivers) is listed around $588 at Noel Leeming, and it pairs particularly well with LG OLED TVs via WOW Orchestra syncing. JBL's Bar 500MK2, a 5.1-channel system with a wireless sub, is around $547 and throws a wider, more room-filling sound for movie nights thanks to the dedicated surrounds. Either is a genuinely big step up from a TV's internal speakers without spending Sonos money.

Best budget pick

If you just want dialogue to stop disappearing during quiet scenes, entry soundbars from JBL, LG and Samsung start well under $350 in NZ. These are usually stereo or basic 2.1 setups without real Atmos — fine for a bedroom or small apartment TV, not what you want for a dedicated home theatre. Watch for bundle deals around Black Friday and Boxing Day; NZ retailers regularly discount last year's models by 30-40% once the new range lands.

Do you need a subwoofer?

If your TV lounge is small to medium and you mostly watch normal TV and streaming, a bar with a built-in bass driver (like the Beam or Bose Lifestyle Ultra) is enough. If you watch a lot of action movies or play games with a lot of low-end rumble, a separate wireless subwoofer — bundled with most of the mid-range and premium options above — makes a genuinely noticeable difference and is worth the extra cost and floor space.

Dolby Atmos: real vs virtual

Cheap bars claim "Atmos support" by taking a Dolby Atmos audio stream and processing it through forward-firing speakers only — you get the encoding but not the height effect. Bars with actual up-firing drivers (Samsung Q990F, LG SC9S, Sonos Arc/Arc Ultra) bounce sound off your ceiling to create real overhead effects. If Atmos height is the reason you're buying a new bar, check the spec sheet says "up-firing" or "height channel," not just "Dolby Atmos decoding."

Don't buy at the first price you see

Soundbar pricing swings hard around sales periods in NZ. Compare current listings before you commit.
