Best MacBook for Students

The base MacBook Air covers almost every student's real needs - here's when it's worth upgrading, and when the Pro is genuinely justified instead.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Why students keep choosing MacBooks

The honest reason MacBooks are popular with students isn't brand snobbery - it's that the base MacBook Air genuinely nails the things students care about most: it's silent (no fan on the Air), the battery comfortably lasts a full day of lectures and study, it's light enough to not notice in a backpack, and it holds resale value dramatically better than Windows laptops when you go to sell it in a few years.

The trade-off is real too: Apple's chips are excellent but you're locked into macOS, some Windows-only engineering/CAD software simply won't run natively, and Apple's own storage/RAM upgrades at checkout are expensive compared to just buying a higher base configuration or a Windows alternative.

RAM: 16GB is the sensible minimum for a MacBook you intend to keep for your whole degree - Apple doesn't let you upgrade RAM after purchase, ever, so buy for the next 3-4 years, not just this semester. Storage: 256GB is workable if you lean on cloud storage; 512GB is the safer buy if you'll be storing lecture recordings, design files, or a large photo/video library locally.

Our picks

MacBook Air 13" M4 (16GB/256GB) - around $1699-$1899

The sensible entry point for most students. The M4 chip has more than enough headroom for note-taking, research, coding, and light photo/video editing, and the 16GB RAM means it'll still feel snappy in your final year, not just your first.

MacBook Air 13" M4 (16GB/512GB) - around $1899-$2099

Worth the extra spend if you're studying something that involves keeping large files locally - design, video, photography, or research with big datasets. The extra storage heads off having to juggle an external drive constantly.

MacBook Air 15" M4 - around $2199-$2499

Same internals as the 13-inch, in a bigger, sharper display for a real premium. Worth it if you'll be reading dense PDFs, doing design work, or simply prefer more screen real estate - not worth it just for the extra size if portability is your priority.

MacBook Pro 14" M4 - around $2999+

Overkill for most degrees, but genuinely justified for computer science, engineering, film/video, or music production students who need the extra CPU/GPU grunt, better screen (mini-LED on higher configs), and active cooling for sustained workloads. Not a casual "just in case" buy given the price jump.

Refurbished/previous-gen MacBook Air (M2/M3) - around $1299-$1599

A genuinely smart budget move if bought from Apple's certified refurbished store or a reputable NZ retailer with warranty. The performance difference for typical student use (browsing, writing, Zoom, basic editing) is barely noticeable next to the current M4 model, at a real discount.

Check for education pricing and back-to-school bundles before paying full price - Apple's NZ education store offers a discount for eligible students plus sometimes an AirPods or gift card bundle during back-to-school periods (typically January-March), and it stacks with retailer promotions at Noel Leeming or JB Hi-Fi in some cases.

How to actually decide between them

For most students, the decision really comes down to storage need, not chip performance - the base M4 chip is more than capable for the vast majority of coursework, so the 256GB versus 512GB question, and whether the 13 or 15-inch size suits how you work, matters more than which exact configuration to chase.

If money is tight, a well-cared-for previous-generation MacBook Air (M2 or M3) from Apple's certified refurbished store is a genuinely smart way to get the same day-to-day experience for meaningfully less, and still comes with a warranty.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Buying 8GB RAM to save money. Since RAM can't be upgraded later, an 8GB Mac is the one spec compromise that will genuinely age badly over a 3-4 year degree - spend the extra for 16GB if at all possible.
  • Assuming you need a MacBook Pro "to be safe." Unless your specific course requires sustained heavy compute (video rendering, ML training, CAD), the Air's fanless design and better battery life make it the better everyday student machine.
  • Forgetting about software compatibility. If your course requires specific Windows-only software (some engineering, architecture or finance tools), a MacBook alone might not cover it - check before you commit, or budget for a Windows virtual machine/alternative if needed (Apple Silicon Macs can't run Boot Camp).
  • Overpaying for the storage upgrade at checkout. Apple's SSD upgrades are expensive per GB - an external SSD or cloud storage is almost always cheaper for the same headroom.

Quick FAQ

Air or Pro for a computer science student?

The Air handles coursework coding fine for most first and second-year papers. Consider the Pro only if you're doing heavy compilation, local ML model training, or virtualisation regularly - most CS students genuinely don't need it.

Is buying secondhand a good idea?

Apple-certified refurbished units are a safe bet with warranty included. Private secondhand sales can be good value too, but always check Apple's Battery Health figure and confirm the machine isn't still linked to someone else's Apple ID (Activation Lock) before paying.
