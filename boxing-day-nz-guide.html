Boxing Day NZ: what actually goes on sale

How Boxing Day pricing differs from Black Friday, and which categories genuinely see their best clearance pricing of the year.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Boxing Day vs Black Friday: not the same sale twice

Black Friday is largely a US-imported retail event NZ stores have adopted for electronics and audio specifically. Boxing Day is NZ's own long-standing post-Christmas clearance tradition, and it tends to lean harder into general stock clearance — appliances, TVs, homewares and whatever retailers are trying to move before the new year — rather than being audio- and electronics-focused the way Black Friday is.

What genuinely gets its best pricing on Boxing Day

TVs are consistently strong on Boxing Day, often edging out Black Friday pricing on outgoing model-year stock as retailers clear floor space ahead of new ranges arriving. Large appliances (fridges, washing machines) see some of their only meaningful discounts of the year around Boxing Day specifically, since they rarely go on sale otherwise. Christmas-adjacent gift categories — small kitchen appliances, headphones, toys and games — also see genuine clearance pricing as unsold Christmas stock gets moved.

What to watch out for

Some Boxing Day "sales" are simply a continuation of pre-Christmas pricing relabelled as a clearance event, rather than a genuinely new discount. Check price history from a few weeks before Christmas to confirm the Boxing Day price is actually lower, not just repackaged as a sale.

Stock can be limited on the best Boxing Day deals. The deepest clearance discounts are often on limited floor stock or ex-display models — worth checking availability and condition before committing, particularly for larger appliances bought in-store.

The bottom line

Boxing Day is the stronger window specifically for appliances and TVs; Black Friday tends to lead on audio and general electronics — check both rather than assuming one event covers everything.
