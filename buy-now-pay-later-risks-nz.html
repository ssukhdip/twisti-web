Buy Now Pay Later for Electronics: What to Watch For

Buy now pay later services have become a common checkout option for electronics purchases in New Zealand. They can be a genuinely useful budgeting tool, but they carry real risks that are worth understanding before you use one. This is general information, not financial advice.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

How buy now pay later generally works

Buy now pay later (BNPL) services let you split the cost of a purchase into a series of instalments, often paid weekly or fortnightly, usually with no interest charged if payments are made on time. Several providers operate in the New Zealand market, and many are integrated directly into electronics retailers' checkout pages, appearing as one of several payment options alongside card and account payment.

The main risks to understand

  • Late fees — most BNPL providers charge a fee for a missed or late instalment, and these can add up quickly if multiple payments are missed, or if you have several BNPL purchases running at once.
  • Credit impact — some BNPL providers now report payment history to credit reporting agencies, and missed payments can affect your credit score in the same way a missed loan or credit card payment might. Policies vary by provider, so it is worth checking the specific terms.
  • Overcommitment — because each individual instalment feels small, it is easy to take on more BNPL commitments across multiple purchases than you would with a single upfront payment, and the combined weekly total can become difficult to manage.
  • Impact on future borrowing — active BNPL commitments are increasingly taken into account by lenders assessing affordability for things like home loans, even when payments are up to date.

When it can be a reasonable choice

Used carefully, BNPL can help spread the cost of a necessary purchase, such as a laptop needed for study or work, without paying interest, provided the instalments are comfortably affordable and paid on time. It works best as a short-term budgeting tool for a planned purchase, rather than a way to buy something that would not otherwise fit the budget at all.

Questions worth asking before you use it

Before checking out with a BNPL option, it helps to check the exact fee structure for missed payments, whether the provider reports to credit agencies, and whether you already have other BNPL instalments running that this purchase would add to. It is also worth comparing the total commitment against your regular income and other expenses, the same way you would for any other form of credit.

Note: This article provides general information only and is not personalised financial advice. If you are unsure whether BNPL is right for your situation, consider speaking with a budgeting advisor or financial mentor — free services are available through organisations like MoneyTalks in New Zealand.

Comparing BNPL against other payment options

Before committing to instalments, it is worth checking whether paying upfront, using a standard credit card, or waiting until you have saved the full amount might work out better overall once fees and interest are accounted for. There is no single right answer — the best option depends on your personal financial situation and how confident you are in meeting the payment schedule.
