Robot Vacuum Brand Support in NZ: Who Actually Backs You Up

A robot vacuum is only as good as the brushes, filters and battery you can still buy for it in two years. Here's which brands genuinely support their gear in New Zealand and which ones leave you importing parts from Amazon.

Twisti guides · Updated August 2026

Why this matters more than the spec sheet

Every robot vacuum brand looks similar on launch day — LiDAR mapping, self-empty bases, mop attachments, app control. The difference shows up 14 months later when a side brush snaps, the mop pad wears through, or the battery starts dying after 25 minutes instead of 90. If the brand doesn't stock parts locally, you're either paying courier fees from an overseas warehouse or replacing a $900 machine because a $15 part isn't available.

Roborock

Roborock has built out a genuine NZ presence through PB Tech and its own local distributor, and it's currently the strongest of the "value flagship" brands for parts availability here. Replacement side brushes, HEPA filters and mop cloths for models like the S8 Pro Ultra and Qrevo series are stocked locally, usually $15–40 per part. Batteries are the exception — they're less commonly held in NZ stock and often take 1–2 weeks to arrive when ordered in.

Ecovacs

Ecovacs (Deebot) parts availability in NZ has improved but is inconsistent between retailer and model. Mainstream Deebot X-series and T-series consumables (filters, brushes) are usually available through Noel Leeming and Ecovacs' own NZ storefront, but older or lower-volume models can require a direct order from Ecovacs Australia, adding a week or two and shipping cost.

Eufy (Anker)

Eufy doesn't run a dedicated NZ service centre — support and parts largely route through Anker's regional (Australia/APAC) channels. For popular models like the Eufy X10 Pro Omni, filters and brushes can usually be sourced via Anker's website with NZ shipping, but turnaround is slower than a local stockist and there's no walk-in repair option.

Dyson

Dyson's robot vacuum range is smaller, but where it's sold, Dyson's NZ-based support line and dyson.co.nz parts store make this the most consistent local service experience of any brand on this list — the same infrastructure that backs its stick vacuums covers the robot range.

Roomba (iRobot)

iRobot's NZ distribution has been inconsistent since its ownership changes, and this is the brand most likely to leave you stuck. Some Roomba models (j7+, Combo j9+) are stocked through Noel Leeming and Harvey Norman, but genuine replacement parts and batteries are frequently only available by importing from iRobot's US or Australian store, with shipping often taking 3+ weeks.

NZ parts & support comparison

Roborock: Local parts (brushes/filters) via PB Tech, 1–2yr warranty, batteries slower to source.

Ecovacs: Mainstream models well stocked locally, older models need AU order-in, 1–2yr warranty.

Eufy: No dedicated NZ service centre, parts via Anker AU/APAC shipping, 1–2yr warranty.

Dyson: NZ-based support line and parts store, 2yr warranty, most consistent local service.

Roomba (iRobot): Patchy NZ stock, parts often US/AU import only, 1yr warranty on many models.

What actually breaks first

Across most brands, the parts you'll need soonest are the same: side brushes (typically wear out at 6–9 months of daily use), the main brush roll (12–18 months), HEPA filters (should be replaced every 2–3 months for genuine suction retention), and mop pads if you run wet mopping. Batteries are the expensive one — expect a genuine replacement to run $80–150 regardless of brand, and expect it to be the hardest single part to find in NZ stock on short notice.

Useful tip: Before buying, search "[model name] side brush NZ" and "[model name] battery NZ" directly rather than trusting a listing's generic "spare parts available" claim — if nothing comes up on PB Tech, Noel Leeming or the brand's own NZ store, budget for a 2–3 week wait and international shipping the first time something wears out.

App support and firmware updates

Parts aren't the only thing that lapses when a brand's NZ presence is thin. App support matters too — a robot vacuum that stops receiving firmware updates loses map refinement, new no-go zone features, and sometimes basic connectivity as phone operating systems update around it. Roborock and Dyson have been consistent about pushing updates to older models through their apps. Ecovacs has generally kept pace with mainstream models but has been known to deprioritise older Deebot lines faster than Roborock does. Eufy's app update cadence has been inconsistent across its wider product range, and iRobot's app support for older Roomba generations has visibly slowed as the company has gone through ownership changes.

Warranty length compared

Most robot vacuums sold in NZ carry a 1-year warranty as standard, with Roborock, Ecovacs and Dyson sometimes extending to 2 years on flagship models when registered directly with the manufacturer. Under the Consumer Guarantees Act, NZ buyers also have rights beyond the stated warranty period if a fault appears that a reasonable person wouldn't expect for the price paid — but that's a claim against the retailer, not an automatic parts guarantee from the brand.

Bottom line

If local support matters as much as day-one performance, Dyson and Roborock are currently the safest robot vacuum choices for NZ buyers — both have workable local parts channels and predictable warranty service. Ecovacs is fine if you stick to current mainstream models. Eufy and Roomba are the two to go in with eyes open: budget extra time and shipping cost for parts once the warranty period ends.
