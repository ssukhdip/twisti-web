Best Baby Monitor NZ 2026: Which One to Buy

Baby monitors range from simple audio-only units to full smart-home connected cameras with app streaming. The right one depends on your house layout and how much tech you actually want between you and the nursery.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Audio, video or app-connected?

Audio-only monitors are the simplest and cheapest option, and for a lot of families they're genuinely enough — you hear your baby, you go check. Video monitors add a screen (usually a dedicated handheld unit) so you can see without walking in, which is particularly useful for older babies and toddlers who might be standing up or getting into things. App-connected Wi-Fi monitors stream to your phone, meaning you can check in from anywhere in the house, or even away from home, but they rely on your home Wi-Fi and the manufacturer's cloud service being reliable.

A dedicated handheld video monitor generally has lower latency and doesn't depend on your internet connection, which matters if your Wi-Fi is patchy or you want one less thing relying on the cloud at 2am.

Wi-Fi app monitors vs dedicated handheld units

App-based monitors are convenient if you want to check on your baby from another part of the house on your phone, or share access with a partner or caregiver easily. The trade-off is a dependency on your internet connection and the vendor's servers — if the Wi-Fi drops or the company's cloud service has an outage, you may lose the feed. Dedicated handheld monitors avoid this by transmitting directly, though the display and range are limited to the one unit.

Range and interference

If you live in a larger home, or the nursery is on a different floor to where you'll usually be, check the monitor's stated range and note that real-world range is usually lower than the box states due to walls and interference. Digital monitors using frequency-hopping generally handle interference from other household devices (routers, microwaves, cordless phones) better than older analogue models, which are increasingly rare anyway.

Features worth prioritising

  • Night vision: essential on any video monitor — check it uses infrared rather than just a low-light colour sensor, since infrared works in true darkness.
  • Temperature and humidity sensors: a genuinely useful nursery safety feature on many mid-range and premium monitors.
  • Two-way audio: lets you speak to soothe your baby remotely without walking in, handy on video and app models.
  • Pan/tilt/zoom camera: useful once your baby starts moving around the cot or room more, so you're not stuck with a fixed frame.
Safety note: Whatever monitor you choose, follow the cord and camera placement guidance in the manual carefully — cots should be positioned so cords and the camera unit itself are well out of reach, regardless of brand.

Price range in NZ

Basic audio-only monitors are the most affordable option, dedicated video handheld monitors sit in the middle, and app-connected smart monitors with extras like sensors and pan-tilt cameras command the highest prices. It's worth deciding which features you'll actually use daily rather than paying for a feature-packed model you won't touch.
