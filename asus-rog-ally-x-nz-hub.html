Asus ROG Ally X: NZ Price Guide

Asus's flagship handheld has settled into place as the gaming PC to beat in your pocket. Here's what it costs in New Zealand right now, and where it's cheapest.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
How we picked these: Prices pulled from listed retailer pages for the standard 1TB Ally X configuration, cross-checked against manufacturer RRP and recent NZ tech press coverage. Figures are indicative and move with stock.

Where the ROG Ally X sits today

The Asus ROG Ally X is no longer the shiny new thing — it's the handheld Asus has quietly kept as its flagship, with the larger 80Wh battery, 24GB of RAM and a roomier chassis that fixed most of the original Ally's complaints. In New Zealand it's sold through the big electronics chains rather than direct from Asus, so pricing varies more than you'd expect for a single SKU.

Current NZ pricing

Expect to pay around $1,399 at JB Hi-Fi for the 1TB model, with Noel Leeming usually within $20-30 of that depending on active promotions. PB Tech tends to run the sharpest everyday pricing, sometimes dipping to $1,329, particularly around bundle deals with carry cases or extended warranties. Mighty Ape is worth checking for bundled game credit, which can offset the sticker price by $50-100 in effective value.

Specs that actually matter

  • AMD Ryzen Z1 Extreme processor, unchanged from launch but still competitive for 2026 handheld gaming
  • 24GB LPDDR5X RAM, up from 16GB on the original Ally — this is the upgrade most reviewers cared about
  • 1TB NVMe SSD with a full-size M.2 2280 slot, so upgrading storage yourself is straightforward
  • 7-inch 1080p 120Hz VRR display, bright enough for most indoor and shaded outdoor use
  • 80Wh battery, roughly double the original Ally's capacity

Windows vs the SteamOS question

The Ally X still ships with Windows 11, which remains the most-cited frustration in NZ user reviews — background processes and update prompts eat into battery life that the bigger cell was supposed to fix. Enthusiasts have had reasonable success installing SteamOS-based distributions, but that's a DIY project rather than something retailers will support if it goes wrong. If you want console-like simplicity out of the box, that's still the trade-off to weigh against Valve's Steam Deck.

How it compares on value

Against the Steam Deck OLED, which typically lands around $949-$1,199 in NZ depending on storage, the Ally X asks a premium for its stronger CPU, brighter screen and Windows compatibility with your existing game library, including titles that don't run on Linux. Whether that's worth an extra few hundred dollars comes down to whether you already own a big non-Steam library on Epic, Battle.net or Xbox PC Game Pass.

Where to buy

All four major retailers — Noel Leeming, JB Hi-Fi, PB Tech and Mighty Ape — carry the Ally X in NZ, usually with 1-3 day shipping to main centres. Harvey Norman stocks it in select stores rather than online-wide, so ringing ahead saves a wasted trip. Warranty is the standard 12-month manufacturer cover through Asus NZ, with several retailers offering paid extensions to 24 or 36 months.

Should you buy now or wait

Handheld PC pricing in NZ tends to soften around major sales periods rather than dropping steadily over time, so if you're not in a rush, keeping an eye on Black Friday and Boxing Day listings is the more reliable way to save than waiting out a general price decline. For everyone else, current pricing is close to the best it's likely to get outside a sale window.
