Best EV Home Charger NZ

A dedicated home charger is far faster than a standard wall socket and pays for itself quickly if you drive an EV daily. Here's what to buy and budget for.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
How we picked these: Picks reflect current NZ retail and installer pricing, manufacturer specifications, and typical electrician quotes reported across NZ EV owner communities and installer sites.

Do you need a dedicated charger at all?

Plugging into a standard household socket ("trickle charging") works but is slow — roughly 8-15km of range per hour. A dedicated home charger steps that up to 20-40km of range per hour, which matters if you drive long daily distances or want a full charge overnight without planning around it. For low-mileage drivers, a standard socket with a proper EV-rated extension may genuinely be enough.

Best overall: Wallbox Pulsar Plus

The Wallbox Pulsar Plus costs around $1,199 for the unit, with typical installation adding $400-$800 depending on your switchboard's distance from the parking spot. It supports up to 7.4kW single-phase charging, has a companion app for scheduling off-peak charging, and is one of the most commonly installed units by NZ electricians, so parts and support are easy to find.

Best budget option: Zappi mini or EVSE basic units

Simpler fixed-cable chargers without smart app features run from around $650-$900 for the hardware, with similar installation costs to the smarter units. These suit anyone who just wants reliable overnight charging and doesn't care about app scheduling or solar integration.

Best for solar households: Zappi EV Charger

The myenergi Zappi (around $1,650 plus installation) is built specifically for solar households — it can prioritise charging your car from excess solar generation rather than the grid, which meaningfully reduces running costs if you already have solar panels installed.

What installation actually costs

Installation cost depends heavily on how far your switchboard is from where you park, whether your switchboard has spare capacity, and regional electrician labour rates. Budget $500-$1,200 as a realistic all-in installation range on top of the charger hardware price, and always get at least two quotes since prices vary a lot between electricians.

Single-phase vs three-phase power

  • Most NZ homes are single-phase, which caps home charger speed around 7.4kW regardless of the charger's rated maximum.
  • Three-phase power (more common in newer builds or after an upgrade) allows faster charging up to 22kW, but few EVs sold in NZ can actually use more than 11kW on AC anyway.
  • Check your EV's onboard charger spec before paying extra for a charger rated faster than your car can use.

Off-peak charging plans

Several NZ electricity retailers offer EV-specific off-peak plans or free overnight charging windows. Pairing a smart charger that can schedule charging for those windows with the right power plan often saves more over a year than the difference between charger brands.
