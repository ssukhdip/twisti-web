Dyson vs Shark Vacuums NZ: Which Cordless Vacuum Wins?

Dyson owns the premium end of the NZ cordless vacuum market and Shark has spent the last few years undercutting it on price while closing the performance gap. Here's what actually separates them once you look past the marketing.

Twisti guides · Updated August 2026

What you'll actually pay in NZ

Dyson's NZ range starts around $549 for an older V8-generation stick vacuum and climbs steeply from there. The V11 sits in the $700–$900 bracket depending on the bundle, while the flagship V15 Detect and newer Gen5detect models regularly retail between $800 and $1,200 at Noel Leeming, Harvey Norman and Dyson's own NZ store, with the laser dust-detection and HEPA-sealed Gen5detect sitting at the top of that range. Dyson rarely discounts hard outside of Black Friday and Boxing Day.

Shark's NZ lineup is priced to undercut that comfortably. The Shark Anti Hair Wrap range (Powered Lift-Away and Cordless Pet Pro models) typically sits between $400 and $600, and the Shark Stratos — its current flagship with Clean Sense IQ technology and a built-in odour neutraliser — lands around $650 to $750 depending on whether it includes the self-empty base. Shark also runs more frequent sitewide sales through The Warehouse and PB Tech than Dyson does.

Suction and battery run-time

On paper Dyson wins the suction spec sheet — the V15 Detect is rated around 230 airwatts versus roughly 150 airwatts for the Shark Stratos. In real carpet-and-hardwood testing that gap narrows: Shark's anti-hair-wrap brush roll keeps suction more consistent over a cleaning session because it doesn't clog with hair the way conventional brush bars do, while Dyson's raw power advantage shows up mainly on deep-pile carpet and pet hair embedded in rugs.

Battery life is where the brands genuinely diverge. Dyson's V15 and Gen5detect give around 60 minutes on Eco mode but drop to 8–12 minutes on Boost/Max, which is the mode most people actually need for a proper carpet clean. Shark's Stratos and Anti Hair Wrap models are rated for up to 60 minutes on standard suction, so for a full three-bedroom NZ home in one charge, Shark often finishes the job without a mid-clean recharge that a Dyson on max power would need.

Self-emptying docks: who actually offers one

This used to be a robot-vacuum-only feature, but both brands now sell stick vacuums with self-emptying docks in NZ. The Shark Stratos with Clean Sense IQ auto-empty base is available here and typically adds $100–150 over the standard Stratos. Dyson's self-empty dock option is more limited in the NZ range and mostly bundled with Gen5detect models at the top end, pushing the total closer to $1,200–1,400. If a clean-hands-free empty is a priority, Shark currently offers it at a noticeably lower entry price in New Zealand.

Anti-hair-wrap technology: does it actually work

Shark's Anti Hair Wrap brush roll uses interlocking bristle combs that actively strip hair off the roller as it spins, and it's genuinely one of the better hair-wrap solutions on the market — long hair and pet fur that would jam a normal brush bar within a week gets ejected into the bin instead. Dyson's fluffy and Motorbar cleaner heads have improved with conical brush designs on the V15 and Gen5detect, but they still need occasional manual hair removal, especially in households with long-haired pets or people. If hair wrap is your specific pain point, Shark has the edge.

Dyson V15 Detect / Gen5detect vs Shark Stratos

Dyson: $800–$1,200 NZD, ~230AW suction, 60min eco / ~10min max, laser dust detection, 2-year warranty, better raw pick-up power on carpet.

Shark Stratos: $650–$750 NZD (self-empty base +$100–150), ~150AW suction, up to 60min standard mode, Anti Hair Wrap brush roll, odour neutraliser, 2-year warranty, better hair handling and value.

Warranty and NZ service reality

Both brands offer a standard 2-year warranty in New Zealand, but the service experience differs. Dyson runs its own NZ-based customer service line and repair centre network, and genuine spare parts (batteries, filters, cleaner heads) are stocked directly through dyson.co.nz — batteries specifically run around $150–180 to replace out of warranty. Shark's NZ warranty support goes through its local distributor rather than a dedicated Shark-branded service centre, so turnaround on claims can be slower and spare parts (particularly replacement batteries) are less consistently stocked locally, sometimes requiring an order-in wait of a couple of weeks.

Where to buy in NZ

Dyson stock is widest at Noel Leeming, Harvey Norman and the Dyson NZ online store, with occasional appearances at The Warehouse for older models. Shark is carried by The Warehouse, Noel Leeming, Harvey Norman and PB Tech, and PB Tech in particular tends to run the sharpest pricing on Shark's Anti Hair Wrap range during sale periods. Always check whether the listed price includes the self-empty dock — retailers frequently list the base unit and the dock as separate line items.

Useful tip: If you're comparing exact models, search the retailer's site by model code, not marketing name — "Dyson V15 Detect Absolute" and "Dyson V15 Detect Total Clean" are different bundles with different accessories priced $100–200 apart, and the same applies to Shark's "Stratos" vs "Stratos with Clean Sense IQ" listings.

Bottom line

Buy Dyson if you have deep-pile carpet, embedded pet hair, or want the strongest raw suction and don't mind paying $800–1,200 plus for it. Buy Shark if you want genuinely competitive cleaning performance, better long-hair handling, and a self-emptying dock option for $300–500 less — for most NZ households doing a standard weekly clean, the Shark Stratos is the better value pick, while Dyson remains the choice for households that need maximum suction on demand.
