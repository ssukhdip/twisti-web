MONEY-SAVING GUIDE

Best Months to Buy Gaming Consoles

Consoles don't follow the same yearly refresh clock as TVs or laptops - they follow a much longer hardware cycle, which changes the whole strategy for timing a purchase in NZ.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Consoles run on a multi-year cycle, not an annual one

PlayStation, Xbox and Nintendo hardware generations last several years rather than resetting every January like TVs or laptops. That means there is no reliable "new stock just arrived, old stock is being cleared" window every few months. Instead, the big price movements on consoles cluster around a handful of predictable events: mid-cycle hardware revisions (like a slim or Pro model), the run-up to a new generation, and the retail calendar's two biggest sale periods.

Because the hardware itself barely changes in price for long stretches, most of the genuine savings on consoles in NZ come from bundles, storage tier discounts, and timing around Black Friday and Boxing Day rather than waiting for the base console to get cheaper on its own.

The console calendar month by month

  • January-March: A quiet stretch. Post-Christmas demand has been absorbed, and retailers are mostly running down whatever bundle stock is left from the holiday period at modest discounts.
  • April-August: The steadiest pricing of the year. Base console prices rarely move unless a manufacturer announces a formal price cut or a new storage tier lands. This is a reasonable time to buy if a specific bundle you want is in stock, since you are not competing with sale-period demand.
  • September-October: Watch for hardware revisions here - slim redesigns or mid-generation "Pro" style models have historically launched in this window, which is also when the previous hardware revision (if any) gets discounted to clear.
  • November-December: Black Friday and Boxing Day. This is genuinely the best two-event window for console bundles in NZ - retailers compete hardest here, and bundle-in extra controllers, games or subscription credit are far more common than a straight price cut on the console itself.

Two different things get discounted, and they're not the same

The console hardware itself: genuine base-unit price cuts are rare and usually only happen once or twice across an entire console generation, announced formally by Sony, Microsoft or Nintendo rather than as a retailer sale.

The bundle around it: this changes constantly. The same console at the same headline price might come with a second controller, a hit game, or months of a subscription service depending on the week - that's where most of the real value swings happen.

Why "wait for it to get cheaper" often doesn't work with consoles

With TVs and laptops, waiting a few months for the next model-year refresh almost always gets you a cheaper equivalent. Consoles don't work that way - a PS5 or Xbox Series X sold in month one of a generation is functionally the same product sold three years later, and manufacturers are often reluctant to cut the sticker price because it affects perceived brand value. If you need a console now, buying during a bundle-heavy sale event usually beats waiting indefinitely for a price drop that may not come until a hardware revision ships.

New generation launches are the other big price mover

Once a successor console is confirmed or launched, the outgoing generation's remaining stock typically gets discounted harder than at any other point in its life, since retailers want shelf space for the new hardware. The catch is that this window is short and stock of the older console can dry up fast in NZ once it happens, so it rewards buyers who are watching closely rather than those checking back occasionally.

Common trap: a "bonus game bundle" advertised as a saving can sometimes cost more than buying the console and game separately once you check both against their individual price history. Always compare the bundle price to the sum of its parts - see our price tracking guide for how price history exposes this.

What to actually do

If you need a console now, Black Friday and Boxing Day bundles are your best realistic shot at genuine value in NZ - focus on what's included rather than waiting for the console itself to drop in price. If you can wait, keep an eye on hardware revision rumours in September-October, since outgoing models often get discounted once a refreshed version is confirmed. Either way, check the bundle's individual components against their own price history before assuming the "bundle saving" is real.
