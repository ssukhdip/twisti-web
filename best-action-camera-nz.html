Best Action Camera NZ: Which One to Buy

Whether it's surfing at Piha, mountain biking, or filming the kids at the pool, an action camera needs to survive conditions a phone can't. Here's what actually separates the good ones from the gimmicks.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

The big three: GoPro, DJI, Insta360

These three brands dominate the mid-to-premium action camera market, each with a slightly different strength. GoPro has the deepest mounting accessory ecosystem and the most brand recognition. DJI's Osmo Action range tends to edge ahead on battery life and low-light performance. Insta360 has carved out a niche with 360-degree capture and its bullet-time/reframing features, which let you shoot wide and crop the interesting angle later in editing — genuinely useful if you're not confident framing shots in the moment.

Beyond these three, budget brands exist at a much lower price point but generally compromise on stabilisation quality, waterproofing depth ratings, and low-light performance — fine for casual use, less so if you're filming fast action or need genuine underwater durability.

360-degree vs traditional action cameras

A 360 camera (like Insta360's X-series) captures the entire sphere around it, letting you choose the framing and angle after the fact in the app — useful when you can't predict the best shot in advance, like skiing or mountain biking through unpredictable terrain. Traditional single-lens action cameras require you to aim correctly at the time of filming but generally produce higher resolution footage in the direction you do choose, since all the sensor's resolution goes into one frame rather than being split across a sphere.

Waterproofing depth ratings

Most modern action cameras are waterproof without a housing to a moderate depth (commonly around 5-10 metres, but check the specific model), which covers swimming, surfing and light snorkelling. For serious diving beyond that depth, you'll need an additional dive housing accessory, sold separately by most brands. Don't assume "waterproof" alone means dive-ready — check the actual rated depth for the bare camera versus with an accessory case.

  • Swimming, surfing, watersports: most current models handle this without extra housing.
  • Diving beyond a few metres: check for a dedicated dive housing accessory for your specific model.
  • Cold weather/snow: battery life drops noticeably in the cold across all brands — carry spares if filming a full day of skiing or snowboarding.

Storage and accessories

Action cameras rely on microSD cards, and high frame-rate or high-resolution footage eats through storage fast — a fast, high-capacity card (check the manufacturer's minimum speed class recommendation) is worth budgeting for alongside the camera itself. Spare batteries are also worth considering, since most action cameras have modest battery life, especially when shooting 4K or high frame rates continuously.

Tip: Don't assume the newest, most expensive flagship is necessary. Last year's model from any of the major brands often drops significantly in price after a new release and still shoots excellent footage for the vast majority of everyday and adventure use cases.

Price range in NZ

Budget action cameras sit at the low end but come with real compromises in stabilisation and low light. Mid-range models from the major brands offer a strong balance of features and price. Flagship models with the best stabilisation, low-light performance and accessory ecosystems sit at the top of the market.
