Foldable Tablets NZ: Are They Worth the Price?

Book-style foldables like the Galaxy Z Fold-style tablets fold a full-size tablet down to phone-ish proportions. It's a genuinely clever party trick — but is it worth the premium over a normal tablet and a laptop?

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

What a "foldable tablet" actually is

We're not talking about foldable phones like the Z Flip here — this is the bigger book-style category: devices that open like a hardback into a roughly 8-inch tablet screen, or larger multi-fold devices that unfold into something closer to a small laptop display. The pitch is simple: carry one device that's pocketable (or close to it) folded, and a genuinely useful tablet unfolded.

The crease is real, and it doesn't go away

Every current book-style foldable has a visible crease down the middle of the unfolded screen. Manufacturers have got much better at minimising it — later generations are noticeably less obvious than the first wave — but under certain lighting angles, especially with a light background like a white document or web page, you'll see it. It's not a manufacturing defect, it's a physical consequence of a screen that has to bend. If that's something that will bother you every time you look at the display, no amount of marketing copy changes that.

Longer-term durability is the bigger unknown for NZ buyers, since these devices haven't been on the market long enough locally to have a large pool of multi-year owners. The hinge mechanisms have improved a lot generation to generation, but dust ingress and hinge wear are still the most commonly reported issues once a foldable is a couple of years old.

Software still isn't fully there. Android and the manufacturer's own apps (Samsung's One UI, for instance) handle the big unfolded screen well for split-screen multitasking. Plenty of third-party apps still just stretch a phone layout across the bigger canvas instead of using the space properly. It's improved a lot but it's not universal.

Who it actually suits

Foldable tablet vs regular tablet + laptop

  • Best for: people who genuinely need one device for meetings, note-taking and reading who don't want to carry two separate items.
  • Not ideal for: anyone doing heavy typing — a foldable's on-screen or clip-on keyboard is still more cramped than a proper laptop.
  • Price reality: foldables sit well above equivalent flagship tablets, and you're paying largely for the folding mechanism, not extra performance.
  • Longevity: a plain tablet from Apple or Samsung has years of track record; foldables are still proving themselves.

Our honest take

If you're drawn to the idea because it looks impressive, that's a fine reason to want one — but go in knowing you're paying a real premium for a form factor with genuine, unresolved trade-offs. If your actual use case is "read documents, browse, take notes, watch video," a good regular tablet (iPad, Galaxy Tab) plus a laptop for serious work will cost less combined and each device will do its one job better. Foldables make the most sense for people whose work genuinely revolves around mobile multitasking and who value carrying fewer things over raw value for money.
