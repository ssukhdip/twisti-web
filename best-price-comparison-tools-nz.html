Best Price Comparison Tools for NZ Shoppers

There's a range of ways to track electronics pricing in New Zealand, from dedicated comparison sites to browser extensions and manual tracking. Here's how the main approaches compare.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Dedicated price comparison sites

Sites built specifically to compare retailer pricing — like Twisti — pull listings from multiple NZ electronics retailers into one place, so you can see who's cheapest on a specific model without opening a dozen tabs. The main advantage is coverage and speed: a good comparison site checks retailers you might not think to visit directly, and surfaces price history so you can tell whether a "sale" price is actually lower than usual.

What to look for in a comparison site

Check how many retailers it actually covers, whether it shows price history (not just current price), and how recently prices were updated. A comparison tool with stale data can be worse than checking retailers directly.

Browser extensions

Browser-based price tracking extensions typically work by flagging price history or alternative retailer prices while you're browsing a product page. These can be convenient since they surface information without leaving the page you're on, though coverage of NZ-specific retailers varies a lot between extensions — many are built primarily for larger overseas markets and only partially support local sites.

  • Check whether an extension actually tracks NZ retailers specifically, not just international ones.
  • Be mindful of what data and permissions an extension requests before installing it.
  • Extensions can miss in-store-only promotions that don't show on the website.

Retailer newsletters and alerts

Signing up for email alerts or newsletters from specific retailers can surface deals early, but it's a one-sided view — you'll only see what that retailer wants to promote, not necessarily the best price across the market. This approach works best combined with a broader comparison method rather than as a sole strategy.

Manual tracking

Simply checking a handful of retailer sites yourself before a purchase is still a valid approach, particularly for a one-off buy, though it doesn't scale well if you're watching multiple items or want to catch a price drop the moment it happens. This is where a dedicated tool or a simple personal spreadsheet (covered in a separate guide) tends to save real time.

Practical approach: For anything above a modest price point, it's worth combining a comparison tool with a quick manual check close to purchase time, since prices can shift between when you first look and when you're ready to buy.

Bottom line

No single tool covers everything perfectly. A dedicated NZ-focused comparison site tends to give the broadest, most current view, while extensions and manual checks are useful supplements rather than replacements.
