Amazon Kindle NZ: Price, Models & Where to Buy

Amazon's Kindle range spans a budget entry model right up to the premium Oasis and Scribe. Here's how to pick the right one and where NZ shoppers can buy it.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

The Kindle lineup explained

Amazon typically sells several Kindle tiers side by side: the base Kindle (smallest, lightest, most affordable), the Kindle Paperwhite (larger screen, waterproofing, warmer light options), and premium models like the Kindle Oasis or Kindle Scribe, which add physical page-turn buttons or note-taking and stylus support respectively. Each generation tends to improve screen resolution, battery life, and storage rather than reinventing the core reading experience.

What to consider before choosing a model

  • Screen size and resolution — larger, sharper screens sit in the Paperwhite and above tiers
  • Waterproofing, useful if you read near a pool, bath, or beach
  • Storage capacity, which matters more if you read a lot of graphic-heavy books or audiobooks via Audible
  • Warm light adjustment for night reading, typically found on Paperwhite and above

For most casual readers, the base Kindle or Paperwhite covers the core need well; the pricier models add convenience features rather than fundamentally better reading.

Where to buy Kindle in NZ

Kindle devices are available through Amazon's own site with shipping to New Zealand, as well as local retailers including PB Tech, Noel Leeming, JB Hi-Fi, and Harvey Norman, who typically stock at least the base and Paperwhite models. Buying locally often means faster delivery and easier returns compared to ordering directly from Amazon overseas, though pricing can differ — it's worth comparing both.

Kindle vs tablet for reading

E-ink Kindles are purpose-built for reading — no backlit glare, weeks of battery life, and far less eye strain over long sessions compared to a tablet screen. If you only want a book reader, a Kindle is usually the better buy over a general-purpose tablet. If you also want to browse the web, watch video, or use apps, a tablet is more versatile but won't match a Kindle's battery life or outdoor readability.

Buying locally vs importing from Amazon

Because Kindle is an Amazon product, some NZ buyers consider ordering directly from Amazon.com or Amazon.com.au. This can occasionally offer a lower sticker price, but factor in shipping costs, potential customs/GST charges, and warranty support — locally purchased units from NZ retailers are usually easier to service or return if something goes wrong.

Before you buy: Check whether the model is a Wi-Fi only or Wi-Fi+cellular version, and confirm which storage tier you're getting — some retailers list multiple storage variants of the same model at different prices.

Getting the best NZ price

Kindle pricing tends to fluctuate around Amazon's own sales events (like their major shopping days) as well as local retailer promotions. Comparing current pricing across NZ retailers and Amazon directly before buying is the best way to catch a genuine deal rather than a false discount.
