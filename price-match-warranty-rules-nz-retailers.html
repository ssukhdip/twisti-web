NZ Retailer Price-Match and Warranty Rules Compared

Every major NZ electronics retailer advertises a price-beat promise, but the fine print — what's excluded, how long you have to claim, and what happens if something breaks — varies a lot more than the ads suggest.

Twisti guides · Updated August 2026

JB Hi-Fi

JB Hi-Fi NZ runs a straightforward price-beat policy: bring proof of a lower advertised price from an identical, in-stock item at a competitor and staff will typically beat it, often by a small margin rather than just matching it. It generally excludes clearance stock, run-out/ex-demo items, bundle or online-only deals, and prices from overseas or marketplace sellers (like AliExpress or eBay). Store warranty on most electronics defaults to the manufacturer's term — commonly 1–2 years depending on category — with JB Hi-Fi handling the initial claim intake in-store.

Noel Leeming

Noel Leeming's "Price Match Promise" is one of the more heavily advertised in NZ and covers matching (not always beating) a competitor's current lower price on an identical product, provided it's in stock at the competitor and not a clearance, liquidation or one-off online flash deal. Noel Leeming also sells its own extended "Care" warranty plans on top of manufacturer cover — genuinely useful on big-ticket appliances, but read what's actually added versus what the Consumer Guarantees Act already provides for free, since there's real overlap.

Harvey Norman

Harvey Norman operates on a franchise-store model in NZ, which means price-match willingness can vary noticeably store to store even though the general policy (match a genuine lower advertised price, excluding clearance and bundle pricing) is consistent brand-wide. It's worth calling the specific store rather than assuming national uniformity. Harvey Norman also pushes optional extended warranty add-ons at checkout — these are commissionable products, so it's worth comparing the add-on cost against what CGA protection already covers before paying extra.

PB Tech

PB Tech's pricing is already aggressive by default, so its price-match policy is narrower in practice — it will generally match another NZ retailer's identical in-stock listing but is stricter about excluding marketplace sellers, refurbished/open-box listings, and time-limited flash sales. PB Tech's standard warranty follows manufacturer terms, and it's known for being relatively fast and low-friction on straightforward DOA (dead-on-arrival) claims within the first 7–14 days of purchase, which matters more in practice than the price-match fine print for most buyers.

Price-match & warranty at a glance

JB Hi-Fi: Beats competitor price (not just matches), excludes clearance/bundles, standard manufacturer warranty.

Noel Leeming: Matches competitor price, excludes clearance/flash deals, sells add-on Care plans, standard manufacturer warranty.

Harvey Norman: Matches price but varies by franchise store, pushes optional extended warranty at checkout.

PB Tech: Matches select NZ retailers only, excludes marketplace/refurb listings, fast DOA claim turnaround.

Where the Consumer Guarantees Act fits in

Retailer warranty terms sit on top of — not instead of — New Zealand's Consumer Guarantees Act (CGA). The CGA requires goods to be of "acceptable quality" for a reasonable time given the price and type of product, which can mean cover extends well beyond a stated 1-year manufacturer warranty for durable goods like TVs, appliances or laptops. A retailer can't legally shorten your CGA rights with an in-store policy, and if a product genuinely fails outside the manufacturer's warranty window but within a "reasonable" CGA lifespan, you can still have grounds to ask the retailer for a repair, replacement or refund — the retailer is the one legally responsible, not the manufacturer.

Online-only versus in-store pricing

All four retailers now run online storefronts that don't always mirror in-store pricing, and this trips up a lot of price-match claims. Noel Leeming and Harvey Norman have occasionally run online-exclusive discounts that in-store staff won't match at the counter, and conversely, in-store clearance stock at a physical location generally can't be used as a price-match reference against another retailer's online listing. PB Tech, being primarily an online-first retailer, tends to have the most consistent single pricing across channels, which removes some of this friction but also means there's less room to negotiate at a physical counter the way you sometimes can with Harvey Norman's franchise stores.

Practical claim tips

  • Keep the receipt and price screenshot at time of purchase — most price-match claims need proof the competitor's price was live and in-stock, not just remembered.
  • Claim price-match before or at time of purchase where possible — post-purchase price-match windows are typically short (7–14 days) and not universally honoured.
  • Don't pay extra for extended warranty on appliances with an expected multi-year life without first checking what the CGA likely already covers for that product type and price point.
  • Escalate to the Disputes Tribunal (claims up to $30,000) if a retailer refuses a legitimate CGA-backed claim — it's a real, low-cost option many buyers don't realise exists.
Useful tip: If a retailer's price-match staff say "that's a clearance price so it's excluded," ask them to show you where the competitor's listing is actually marked as clearance — genuinely current stock at a lower advertised price is fair game even if the retailer's staff assume otherwise at the counter.

Bottom line

JB Hi-Fi tends to be most generous on the actual price-beat math, Noel Leeming is most heavily marketed but matches rather than beats, Harvey Norman varies by store so it pays to ask locally, and PB Tech is fastest on DOA claims but narrowest on who it will price-match against. Whichever retailer you buy from, remember the CGA sits above all of these policies and is often the stronger protection when something genuinely fails.
