Best Office Chair (NZ, 2026)

You don't need to spend Herman Miller money to sit properly. Here's what actually matters and which chairs are worth it at each price point.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

What actually matters in an office chair

Most chair shopping goes wrong because people buy on looks first. What actually decides whether you're comfortable eight hours a day is lumbar support that hits your actual spine (not just a foam bump in roughly the right area), seat depth you can adjust so the edge doesn't dig into the back of your knees, and armrests that move in more than one direction. Everything else - mesh vs fabric, headrest or not, the brand name on the box - is secondary.

The other thing that matters more than people expect: your body. A chair that's brilliant for a 6'2 frame can be genuinely bad for someone 5'2, because seat depth and armrest height won't be in range. If you can, sit in it before you buy - or buy from somewhere with an actual returns policy, because photos don't tell you this.

Our picks

Ikea Markus - around $299 NZD

The chair the internet quietly agrees is the best value in the category, and it's not close. Mesh back, decent lumbar curve built into the shape rather than a separate adjustable pad, and a seat that's comfortable for long sessions without the surgical adjustability of pricier chairs. Who it suits: anyone who wants 80% of the ergonomic benefit without spending four figures, including most home workers and students. The trap: the armrests are fixed height on some batches and not everyone's torso gets on with the seat pan shape - if you're petite, try before you buy where possible.

Ergohuman V2 / Elite - roughly $1,200-$1,800 NZD

The chair most NZ ergonomic specialists actually recommend when a client has a bad back and a real budget. Independently adjustable lumbar support (height and depth), synchro-tilt mechanism, and a seat depth slider that genuinely changes the fit rather than just looking adjustable. Who it suits: people with an existing back complaint, or anyone spending 8+ hours a day at a desk where comfort is a health issue, not a preference. The trap: buy from an authorised NZ dealer - grey-market imports can turn up without local warranty support, which matters given this is a mechanical product with parts that wear.

Herman Miller Aeron (remastered) - roughly $2,200-$2,900 NZD new, less on Trade Me

The chair everyone's heard of, and the reputation is mostly earned - the PostureFit SL lumbar system and 8Z Pellicle mesh are genuinely well engineered, and it's one of the few chairs with a size range (A/B/C) built for different body types rather than one-size-fits-all. Who it suits: people who plan to keep the chair for a decade-plus and want the resale value and 12-year warranty that comes with it. The trap: at NZ pricing, you're paying a real premium over chairs that get you 90% of the way there - and used units on Trade Me vary wildly in condition, so check the mesh and casters closely before paying near-new money for a worn one.

Autonomous ErgoChair Pro/Ultra - roughly $700-$900 NZD landed

Sits in the gap between Markus and Ergohuman - adjustable lumbar, headrest, and a tilt mechanism that's a genuine step up from budget chairs, at a price that undercuts most premium brands. Who it suits: people who want proper adjustability without Aeron money, and don't mind that it's typically ordered in rather than sitting on a shop floor. The trap: shipping and import costs to NZ can eat into the value proposition, and after-sales support is thinner than a local dealer network - check current landed cost and warranty terms before ordering.

Common mistake: buying a gaming chair for office work. They're built for reclining and short bursts, not sustained upright typing posture - the bucket seat shape and lack of true lumbar adjustment shows up as lower back fatigue within a few months for most people who use one all day.

If you're not sure where to start: try the Markus first. It's cheap enough that getting it wrong isn't a disaster, and good enough that most people never feel the need to upgrade. Only step up to Ergohuman or Aeron money if you already know - from an existing injury or a chair that's failed you - exactly what you need fixed.
