Kindle vs reMarkable: Which to Buy

Both use e-ink screens that are easy on the eyes, but they solve completely different problems — one is for reading books, the other for taking notes on paper-like pages.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
How we picked these: Pricing checked against Amazon NZ, Noel Leeming and the reMarkable NZ web store for current base and premium models of each device line.

Two different jobs

The Amazon Kindle is built to read books — nothing else about it competes for your attention. The reMarkable is built around handwriting and note-taking on an e-ink display that feels close to writing on paper, aimed at people replacing notebooks rather than novels.

NZ pricing

The entry-level Kindle starts around $179 in NZ, with the Kindle Paperwhite at roughly $279 and the premium Kindle Oasis-tier model near $399. The reMarkable is a different price bracket entirely — the current tablet starts around $549-$649, and reMarkable's Marker pen and folio cases are sold separately, easily adding another $150-$250 to the real cost.

What each is actually for

  • Kindle: built for reading ebooks purchased or borrowed through Amazon's store and library apps like Libby
  • reMarkable: built for handwritten notes, PDF markup, and sketching, with cloud sync to a companion app on phone and desktop
  • Kindle has a backlight for reading in the dark; most reMarkable models do not
  • reMarkable supports basic PDF and ebook reading but that's a secondary feature, not its main strength

The subscription question

reMarkable pushes its Connect subscription for full cloud sync and handwriting conversion to text, which is a genuine ongoing cost most reviewers flag as a downside for an already expensive device. Kindle has no required subscription, though Kindle Unlimited is an optional add-on for unlimited ebook borrowing if you read heavily.

Battery life and durability

Kindle devices routinely last weeks between charges thanks to their simple display refresh needs, while reMarkable's more complex handwriting-latency screen tends to need charging every 1-2 weeks with regular use. Both are built around e-ink's core durability advantage — no glare, and screens that are gentler on the eyes than an LCD tablet for long sessions.

Who should buy which

If you read a lot of books and want a distraction-free device that just works, Kindle is the obvious and far cheaper choice. If you're specifically trying to go paperless for handwritten notes, journaling or marking up PDFs, reMarkable does that job in a way no Kindle can, but you're paying a real premium for it.

Don't buy either if

You want a general-purpose tablet for browsing, video or apps — neither device does that well, and an iPad or Android tablet will serve that use case far better for similar or less money.
