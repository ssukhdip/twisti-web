Christmas in NZ: when tech prices actually drop

The run-up to Christmas is a blur of sale banners, but not all of them represent real discounts. Here's how the actual sale calendar breaks down, and where the genuine savings sit.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

The shape of the NZ Christmas sale season

For NZ shoppers, the "Christmas sale season" isn't a single event — it's a sequence that typically starts with Black Friday and Cyber Monday in late November, continues through December with general pre-Christmas promotions, and often peaks again around Boxing Day into early January. Each of these has a different character, and knowing which is which helps you avoid buying at the wrong time.

Black Friday / Cyber Monday

Black Friday has become one of the biggest single discount events on the NZ retail calendar, often rivalling or beating Boxing Day on specific items, particularly TVs, laptops and headphones. Retailers use it to clear stock ahead of the Christmas rush, which tends to produce genuinely deep one-off discounts. The trade-off is limited stock on the most popular deals, and a real risk of inflated "was" prices on less scrutinised items — always check recent pricing before assuming a Black Friday tag means a genuine saving.

Early-to-mid December

The weeks between Cyber Monday and Christmas are generally the weakest window for genuine discounts. Retailers know shoppers are buying on a deadline (needing gifts by December 25th) rather than shopping around, so pricing tends to sit closer to full price during this stretch, even with "Christmas sale" messaging attached to it.

Black Friday vs Boxing Day

Black Friday tends to win on electronics that are being cleared ahead of new models or holiday stock resets — TVs and laptops especially. Boxing Day tends to win on a broader spread of general stock as retailers reset for the new year, and can also catch post-Christmas gift-return stock being repriced. If you're chasing one specific item, it's worth comparing pricing across both events rather than assuming either one is automatically better.

Boxing Day and the January reset

Boxing Day remains one of the most reliable discount windows in the NZ calendar, extending into the first couple of weeks of January as retailers clear remaining Christmas stock and reset for the year ahead. This period often has the widest range of items on sale simultaneously, even if individual discounts aren't always deeper than Black Friday's best deals.

Caveat: "Christmas sale" pricing and discount percentages are marketing claims, not guarantees. Retailers set their own reference prices, and a headline discount doesn't always reflect what the item actually sold for in the weeks before. Check recent pricing on a comparison site before trusting a percentage-off claim at face value.

How to time your purchase

If you're buying a specific big-ticket item and can be flexible on timing, Black Friday and Boxing Day are the two windows worth planning around — check pricing across both if the purchase can wait. If you're buying gifts with a hard December 25th deadline, expect to pay closer to standard pricing during the mid-December window, and treat any "sale" messaging in that period with some scepticism.
