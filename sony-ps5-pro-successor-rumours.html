Next PlayStation Rumours: Everything We Know So Far

Sony hasn't announced a PS6, but its chip partner AMD has confirmed the co-engineering deal behind it. This roundup focuses specifically on the hardware architecture and supply-chain reporting, not the general release-date chatter you've probably already seen elsewhere.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
Why we're labelling this so carefully: There's a real, confirmed partnership underpinning next-gen PlayStation silicon, but almost every specific number attached to it — teraflops, release year, memory bandwidth — comes from unnamed leakers and analyst notes, not Sony. We're keeping those clearly separated.

What's confirmed

Confirmed

Sony and AMD jointly confirmed "Project Amethyst" in October 2025 — a co-engineering partnership building the graphics architecture for Sony's next console generation. This is a real, named, publicly announced deal, not a leak.

Confirmed

Reuters reported in September 2024 that AMD won the contract to design the PS6 chipset over a competing bid from Intel, dating the relationship back to at least 2022.

Confirmed

As of July 2026, Sony has not announced a PS6 name, price, release date, or final specifications. Everything about the console itself remains unconfirmed by Sony.

What's reported

Reported

Multiple outlets citing supply-chain sources report the CPU will use AMD's Zen 6 architecture and the GPU will use next-generation RDNA 5, the same GPU architecture reportedly shared with the next Xbox generation.

Reported

Leaks point to TSMC's N2 (2nm) manufacturing process, a jump from the PS5's 7nm node, and GDDR7 memory offering roughly 640GB/s of bandwidth — about a 43% increase over the PS5's 448GB/s. Rasterization performance leaks suggest roughly three times the base PS5's output, with teraflop estimates ranging from 34-40 TFLOPS versus the PS5's 10.28 TFLOPS.

Reported

A Bloomberg report in February 2026 cited sources saying Sony is now considering a 2028 or 2029 launch window rather than the previously rumoured 2027, attributing the delay to rising memory costs — part of the same industry-wide RAM price crunch affecting several hardware categories.

Twisti's opinion

Opinion

The Project Amethyst confirmation is genuinely significant — it's rare for Sony and AMD to publicly name a partnership this far ahead of a console launch, and it lends real weight to the architecture rumours (Zen 6, RDNA 5, GDDR7) even though the specific numbers are still leaker territory. The memory-cost delay story is also plausible given the broader RAM price pressure hitting laptops and consoles alike through 2026. Our honest read: treat 2027 as optimistic and 2028-2029 as the more likely window, and don't expect Sony to confirm anything officially before a proper reveal event.

What we'll update

We'll update this page the moment Sony makes any official statement about a PS6, or when a named analyst attaches a specific date to Project Amethyst's silicon.
