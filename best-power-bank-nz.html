Best Power Bank NZ (2026)

Capacity on the box rarely matches what actually reaches your phone - here's what to check before paying for mAh you won't get to use, with real NZ pricing.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

What actually matters

Three numbers decide whether a power bank is worth it, and none of them is the headline mAh figure by itself. Usable capacity is typically 60-70% of the rated mAh once you account for voltage conversion loss, so a 20,000mAh bank realistically gives a modern phone about two and a half charges, not four. Output wattage matters just as much as capacity - a bank with a huge battery but only a 5W output will trickle-charge your phone overnight rather than topping it up in the time it takes to eat lunch. Look for USB-C Power Delivery (PD) rated at 20W or higher if you want genuinely fast charging for a modern phone, and 65W+ if you also want to top up a laptop.

The other thing worth checking, especially on cheaper marketplace listings, is safety certification. A reputable power bank will carry proper battery safety certification and won't be flying with you in checked luggage anyway - airlines require power banks in carry-on only, and most cap capacity at 100Wh (roughly 27,000mAh) without special approval.

Our picks

Belkin 10K USB-C Power Bank - around $24-$30 NZD

The everyday budget pick. 10,000mAh with 15W output is enough for one and a bit phone charges without carrying much extra weight. Who it suits: anyone who just wants a backup in their bag for the occasional low-battery afternoon. The trap: 15W is fine for older phones but noticeably slower than PD-fast-charging on the latest handsets.

Anker 10,000mAh with USB-C PD - around $49-$85 NZD

A genuine step up in build and charging speed, with a digital charge display on most models so you're not guessing how much is left. Two or three ports mean you can charge a phone and earbuds case at once. Who it suits: daily commuters who want one reliable bank rather than the cheapest option. The trap: capacity is still modest - fine for one full phone charge, not a full weekend away from a wall socket.

Anker 20,000mAh PD - around $109-$139 NZD

The sweet spot for most people: enough capacity for two to three days of top-ups, fast enough PD output to actually matter, and still light enough to carry daily. Who it suits: frequent travellers and anyone who wants to stop thinking about charging for most of a week. The trap: at this capacity you're at the airline carry-on-only threshold, so it can't go in checked luggage.

Anker Prime 27,650mAh (250W, smart display) - around $275 NZD

Anker's flagship: enough output to fast-charge a laptop as well as a phone, with an app-connected smart display showing exact charge time remaining per port. Who it suits: people who travel with a laptop, phone and tablet and want one bank to cover all three. The trap: it's right at the 100Wh airline capacity limit, and the price only makes sense if you'll actually use the multi-device output.

Anker 60,000mAh - around $449 NZD

Less a power bank than a portable battery station - this is for camping, work sites, or charging multiple devices for days with no power source in sight. Who it suits: campers, tradies, and anyone genuinely off-grid for extended periods. The trap: it's heavy, it's expensive, and airlines generally won't let this capacity fly at all, so it's a car-and-campsite tool rather than a travel companion.

Common mistake: buying on mAh alone. A 20,000mAh bank with a 5W output charges a phone slower than a 10,000mAh bank with 20W PD - check the output wattage before the capacity, especially if the whole point is charging quickly between meetings or flights.

For most people, a 20,000mAh PD power bank hits the right balance of capacity, charging speed and price. Only go bigger if you're regularly away from power for days at a time, and only go smaller if you just want an emergency backup that lives in a bag.
