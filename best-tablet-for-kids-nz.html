Best Tablet for Kids NZ

Kids tablets live or die on two things: whether it survives being dropped, and whether the parental controls are actually usable. Here's how the main options stack up for NZ families.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
How we picked these: We looked at parental control depth, build durability, screen time management and NZ availability rather than raw specs, since kids tablets are rarely used for anything demanding.

Amazon Fire Kids tablets -- strong on parental controls, patchy on NZ availability

Amazon's Fire Kids tablets (Fire Kids for ages 3-7, Fire Kids Pro for 6-12) ship with a genuinely thorough parental control suite through Amazon Kids+, letting parents set daily time limits, filter content by age, and require "learn before play" educational goals before games unlock. The kid-proof case is one of the best in the category -- Amazon offers a "worry-free" 2-year guarantee that replaces the tablet for free if a kid breaks it. The catch for NZ buyers: Amazon doesn't run an official NZ storefront for these devices, so most Kiwi buyers are ordering via Amazon.com or Amazon.com.au with international shipping, meaning landed cost after shipping and currency conversion typically comes out around $150-220 NZD depending on model and current USD/AUD exchange rates -- treat that as an estimate rather than a fixed local price, since Amazon doesn't quote NZD directly.

Samsung Galaxy Tab A9 Kids Edition -- the easier local buy

Samsung's kids-focused Galaxy Tab A9 Plus Kids Edition bundles a proper Samsung Android tablet with a shockproof "Puffy Cover" case in blue, red or yellow, plus Samsung Kids software for content filtering and screen time limits, and typically ships with a stand/handle built into the case. In the US it's listed around $269.99 USD; NZ pricing through Samsung's local channel and retailers like Noel Leeming and JB Hi-Fi tends to land in a similar band once converted, roughly $300-380 NZD — check current listings since Samsung's kids-edition stock in NZ can be inconsistent between models and generations. The advantage over the Fire tablet is that it's a full Android tablet outside of Kids Mode, meaning it has a longer useful life once your child outgrows the parental controls -- you can just switch off Kids Mode and use it as a normal Android tablet.

Parental controls compared

Amazon's Kids+ subscription (bundled free for a trial period, then a recurring monthly cost of roughly $8-13 NZD equivalent depending on plan) is more mature and kid-specific than Samsung Kids, with better content curation and age-banding. Samsung Kids is free and built into every Galaxy tablet, not just the Kids Edition, and covers the basics -- app whitelisting, time limits, a simplified kid-friendly launcher -- without needing an extra subscription. If a subscription is a dealbreaker, Samsung's free approach is the more budget-friendly long-term option.

Durability -- what actually survives a toddler

Both the Fire Kids case and Samsung's Puffy Cover are rated for drops from typical child height (around 1.2-1.8m) onto hard floors, and in practice both hold up well to the kind of abuse a 4-8 year old dishes out. Screens on both are still glass, not plastic, so a hard enough drop directly onto a corner can still crack it -- the case protects against the far more common flat-drop scenario, not a worst-case throw.

Bottom line for NZ parents

  • Want the deepest parental controls and don't mind ordering from overseas: Amazon Fire Kids Pro, landed roughly $150-220 NZD.
  • Want a locally-available full Android tablet that outlives Kids Mode: Samsung Galaxy Tab A9 Kids Edition, roughly $300-380 NZD.
  • Tight budget: A base Galaxy Tab A9 or similar budget Android tablet plus a $20-30 third-party kid-proof case from Mighty Ape gets most of the durability benefit for less.
