Best Smart Blinds NZ: Which One to Buy

Smart blinds range from a small motor clipped onto blinds you already own, to a full replacement system wired into your walls. The right choice depends on whether you're solving a real problem or chasing a gadget.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Retrofit kits vs full replacement

If you already have blinds you're happy with, a retrofit motor — a small battery-powered unit that clips onto the existing chain or wand mechanism — is usually the cheaper and less disruptive option. These are widely available for roller and venetian blinds and typically pair over Wi-Fi or a hub, letting you schedule and control them from an app without replacing the blinds themselves.

Full smart blind systems, where the whole unit is replaced, make more sense in new builds or renovations where you're choosing window coverings from scratch anyway. They tend to look cleaner (no visible retrofit motor) and can be hardwired rather than battery powered, but the cost is significantly higher since you're paying for entirely new blinds, not just automation.

Battery vs hardwired: the real trade-off

Battery-powered smart blinds are far easier to install — no electrician required — but you'll be recharging or replacing batteries periodically, more often on blinds that open and close daily via schedule rather than occasionally by hand. Hardwired options need an electrician but remove the recharging chore entirely. For a couple of windows, battery power is a reasonable trade-off. For a whole house of automated blinds, the ongoing battery maintenance adds up and hardwiring starts to look more sensible.

Genuine use cases vs novelty

Smart blinds earn their keep in a few specific situations: automatically closing west-facing blinds in the afternoon to keep a room from overheating in summer, opening them on a schedule while you're away to make a house look lived-in, or syncing with sunrise/sunset for bedrooms without needing to remember each night. If your main reason for buying is "it'd be cool to control blinds from my phone," it's worth being honest that this is more novelty than problem-solving — which is a fine reason to buy something, just worth knowing going in.

What to check before buying

  • Blind type compatibility — roller, venetian, roman and vertical blinds often need different motor types; check the kit matches what you have.
  • Hub requirements — some systems need a separate smart hub to work with voice assistants, adding to the total cost.
  • Noise level — cheaper motors can be noticeably louder than premium ones, which matters for bedroom use.
  • Manual override — check you can still open and close the blind by hand if the motor or app fails.
  • Battery life claims — real-world battery life is usually shorter than the box states, especially with frequent scheduled use.
