Xiaomi in NZ: The Complete Buying Guide

Xiaomi is one of the widest product ranges sold in New Zealand — phones, scooters, robot vacuums, air purifiers and more all carry the same logo. Here's how to navigate it without overpaying or buying the wrong variant.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

One brand, a huge range of categories

Unlike most electronics brands that stick to one or two categories, Xiaomi sells phones, laptops, scooters, vacuum robots, security cameras, air purifiers and a long list of smart home accessories — often at prices noticeably below equivalent Samsung, Dyson or Ecovacs products. The trade-off buyers need to weigh is that NZ-specific software support, warranty servicing and app localisation aren't always as mature as bigger brands, so it pays to check reviews for the exact NZ-sold model rather than a global version.

Understanding Xiaomi's sub-brands

  • Xiaomi (numbered series) — the flagship and mid-range phone lines, generally the best value-for-spec phones sold in NZ.
  • Redmi — Xiaomi's budget phone sub-brand, aimed at price-sensitive buyers who still want a decent camera and battery life.
  • POCO — performance-focused phones, popular with buyers who want strong processors for gaming without flagship pricing.
  • Mi Home ecosystem — the umbrella for scooters, vacuums, air purifiers and smart plugs, most of which work through the same Xiaomi Home app.

Xiaomi vs Redmi: which phone line should you pick?

If camera quality, display brightness and overall polish matter most, the numbered Xiaomi series is the better bet and still undercuts Samsung and Apple at equivalent tiers. If you mainly need a reliable phone for calls, messaging and everyday apps and want to spend as little as possible, Redmi models regularly deliver acceptable performance for a fraction of the price. POCO sits in between and is worth a look if mobile gaming is the priority.

Scooters, vacuums and the smart home range

Xiaomi's electric scooters and robot vacuums have a genuine following in NZ, largely because they undercut Western competitors while offering comparable core features. Before buying, check that the specific model is sold with local warranty support through an authorised NZ retailer — grey-import listings can be cheaper upfront but leave you without local servicing if something breaks.

App and ecosystem lock-in

Most Xiaomi smart home devices route through the Xiaomi Home (or Mi Home) app, and pairing everything into one ecosystem does make day-to-day use simpler. The downside is that mixing Xiaomi smart home gear with other ecosystems like Google Home or Apple HomeKit can require extra bridging steps, so it's worth checking compatibility before you commit to a full smart home build-out.

Check the plug and charger. Some Xiaomi products are sold as grey imports with non-NZ plugs or chargers that aren't rated for our 230V supply. Buying through an authorised NZ retailer avoids this hassle and usually includes local warranty cover.

Finding the best NZ price

Because Xiaomi products are stocked by a wide mix of authorised retailers and importers, prices for the same model can vary substantially. Comparing listings before you buy — and confirming the seller offers NZ warranty support — is the safest way to get a genuine deal rather than a false discount from an unsupported import.
