Samsung Frame TV vs Standard LG TV: Which to Buy

Samsung's Frame TV sells a lifestyle as much as a screen, with art-mode design and a matte finish, while a standard LG TV usually gets you better picture performance per dollar. Here's how to decide which matters more to you.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

What the Frame is actually selling

The Frame TV's whole pitch is that it looks like a piece of art when not in use, using a customisable bezel, a matte anti-glare display finish, and Art Mode to display artwork or photos on the screen. It also uses a slim wall-mount design and a separate "One Connect" box to keep cables away from the screen itself, which genuinely helps if you're mounting it flush to a wall.

If display aesthetics matter to you — say the TV sits prominently in a living or dining space and you want it to blend in when off — this is a real, tangible benefit that standard TVs from any brand don't replicate.

Panel technology matters more than brand

The Frame typically uses Samsung's QLED panel technology, while LG's standard TVs span a range from budget LED models up to OLED at the premium end. Rather than comparing "Samsung vs LG" as brands, compare the actual panel tech and tier: an entry LG LED and the Frame are a fairer match than the Frame against an LG OLED, which will generally outperform it on contrast and black levels.

Picture quality trade-offs

Because the Frame prioritises the matte, glare-reducing finish for its art-display function, some reviewers note it doesn't quite match Samsung's own non-Frame QLED or Neo QLED models for peak brightness and contrast punch. A standard LG TV at a similar price point — particularly if it's an OLED model — will often deliver noticeably better contrast and black levels for pure viewing, since OLED doesn't need a backlight at all.

  • Bright rooms with lots of natural light: the Frame's matte finish genuinely helps reduce glare compared to glossy panels.
  • Dark home theatre style rooms: an OLED LG model will usually give a more impressive picture for movies.
  • Gaming: check refresh rate and input lag specs on the specific model rather than assuming by brand — both companies offer gaming-focused higher-refresh options.

Price premium

The Frame typically carries a noticeable premium over an equivalent-spec standard Samsung TV, and often over a comparable LG model too, largely because you're paying for the design elements — the customisable bezel, art mode content library, and cable management box. Whether that's worth it depends entirely on how much you value the design and art-display feature versus raw picture performance per dollar.

Consider: Interchangeable bezels for the Frame are sold separately and can add a meaningful amount to the total cost if you want to match your decor — factor this into your budget rather than just the base TV price.

Bottom line for NZ buyers

If the TV is a visible design feature in your living space and you like the idea of art mode, the Frame is a legitimate, unique product with no direct equivalent from LG. If you mainly care about picture quality and value, comparing a standard LG TV (LED or OLED depending on budget) against Samsung's non-Frame lineup will usually get you more performance for the same money.
