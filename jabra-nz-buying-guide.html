Jabra in NZ: The Complete Buying Guide

Jabra builds earbuds and headsets around call quality and durability rather than flashy features, which makes it the go-to brand for remote workers and hybrid teams in NZ. Here's the range.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
How we picked these: We compared current listings at PB Tech and Noel Leeming, along with PriceSpy NZ aggregated pricing, to reflect real street prices rather than US list prices.

Jabra's angle: calls first, everything else second

Unlike Sony, Bose or Apple, Jabra doesn't chase the best possible music listening experience — it optimises for voice call clarity, multi-microphone noise cancellation on calls, and rugged reliability. That's made it the default recommendation for people who spend their day on Zoom or Teams calls, and it shows up heavily in NZ workplace and IT-department purchasing, not just retail.

Elite earbuds range

The Jabra Elite 10 sits at the top of the consumer earbuds range, with active noise cancellation, Dolby Atmos spatial sound and an ergonomic "semi-open" fit designed for comfort over long wear — a genuine advantage for people wearing earbuds most of the working day. It's priced similarly to flagship earbuds from Sony and Samsung internationally, generally landing in the mid-to-high $300s NZD depending on retailer and sales. The Jabra Elite 8 Active is the sportier sibling, built around a more secure fit and IP68 military-grade durability rating for sweat, dust and drops, aimed at gym and outdoor use rather than office wear. Below both sits the Jabra Elite 4, a genuinely solid budget option with ANC that regularly undercuts equivalent Sony or Samsung earbuds by $50-100.

Work headsets: Evolve2 and Engage

Away from consumer earbuds, Jabra's Evolve2 range of over-ear and on-ear headsets is a fixture in NZ corporate and call-centre procurement — built for all-day wear, certified for Microsoft Teams and Zoom, with strong background noise suppression for open-plan offices and home setups alike. These aren't really sold as impulse buys; IT departments and remote workers buying their own gear are the main audience, and they're stocked at PB Tech alongside the consumer earbuds range.

Where retailers differ

PB Tech carries the deepest Jabra catalogue in NZ, including the work headset ranges that Noel Leeming and JB Hi-Fi mostly skip in favour of consumer earbuds only. For Elite 10 and Elite 8 Active specifically, it's worth comparing PB Tech against Noel Leeming and Dick Smick NZ, as pricing on current-generation Jabra earbuds moves with sales fairly often — a $30-40 difference between retailers isn't unusual.

Jabra vs the alternatives

If music quality and app polish are the priority, Sony's WF-1000XM5 or Bose's QuietComfort Ultra earbuds generally edge out Jabra. But for anyone whose earbuds spend more hours on calls than listening to music, Jabra's microphone array and call-focused tuning are hard to beat at the same price point, and the Elite range integrates cleanly with the same Jabra app used by their work headsets.

Who should buy Jabra

  • Spend most of the day on calls, want the clearest voice pickup: Jabra Elite 10 or an Evolve2 headset.
  • Gym and outdoor use, need a secure fit and durability: Jabra Elite 8 Active.
  • Budget earbuds with genuinely good ANC: Jabra Elite 4.
