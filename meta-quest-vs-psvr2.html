Meta Quest vs PSVR2

One is a standalone headset that needs nothing but itself. The other is a tethered PS5 accessory with the best haptics in consumer VR. Here's which makes sense for you.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
How we picked these: Comparison based on published specs and current international pricing for Meta Quest 3 / 3S and Sony PlayStation VR2, cross-checked against NZ retailer listings. Meta doesn't officially sell Quest hardware direct in NZ, so local pricing runs through resellers — we've flagged that clearly below.

What each one actually is

Meta Quest (currently the Quest 3 and cheaper Quest 3S) is a fully standalone VR headset — no console, no PC, and no wires required. It runs its own Android-based OS, has built-in cameras for mixed reality passthrough, and can also tether to a gaming PC for higher-fidelity VR titles via Air Link or a cable. PlayStation VR2 (PSVR2) is the opposite approach: a tethered headset that plugs into a PS5 and relies entirely on the console for processing power. You need a PS5 to use it — it doesn't work standalone or with a PC (officially).

Display and specs

PSVR2 uses OLED panels with genuinely excellent contrast and per-eye HDR support — noticeably better black levels than Quest's LCD panels. Quest 3's LCD display actually edges out PSVR2 on raw per-eye pixel count and uses sharper pancake lenses, so the older "PSVR2 has better resolution" claim floating around isn't accurate anymore — but OLED's contrast advantage is real and visible, especially in dark scenes. PSVR2 also has eye-tracking built in as standard, which Quest 3 lacks and Quest 3S lacks entirely. Quest 3S uses simpler Fresnel lenses (like PSVR2) rather than Quest 3's pancake lenses, and comes at a lower resolution than the standard Quest 3.

Pricing

PSVR2 has had a notable price cut internationally, now sitting around US$399 (roughly NZ$650-700 landed, though exact NZ retail varies by import reseller since Sony doesn't officially distribute it here). Quest 3 pricing has crept up to around US$599 for the entry storage tier due to component costs, translating to roughly NZ$950-1050 depending on the reseller and storage tier; Quest 3S is the budget option starting meaningfully lower. Neither Meta nor Sony sell VR hardware through an official NZ storefront, so actual landed NZ pricing depends heavily on which importer or retailer you buy from — always check current listings before assuming a number here is exact.

Game libraries

This is where the decision usually gets made. Quest has a much larger and more varied native library, plus access to the full PC VR catalogue (including SteamVR titles) if you tether it to a gaming PC. PSVR2's library is smaller but has genuine exclusives worth owning a PS5 for — Gran Turismo 7 in VR and Horizon Call of the Mountain are standout, console-quality experiences you can't get anywhere else. If you already own a PS5, that exclusivity is a strong pull; if you don't, buying a PS5 just for VR is a much bigger investment than the headset price suggests.

Comfort and controllers

PSVR2's Sense controllers have adaptive triggers and haptic feedback borrowed from the DualSense, which genuinely raises immersion in supported titles — it's still considered best-in-class haptics for consumer VR. Quest's Touch Plus controllers are solid and reliable but don't match that tactile feedback. Both headsets are broadly comparable for comfort out of the box, though aftermarket head straps are a near-universal upgrade for either.

Which one to buy

  • Already own a PS5, want the best visual fidelity and don't mind being tethered: PSVR2.
  • Want to walk around freely, play on the go, or don't own/want a PS5: Quest 3 or 3S.
  • Also want to dabble in PC VR: Quest, since it can tether to a gaming PC — PSVR2 officially can't (without unsupported workarounds).
  • Budget is the main driver: Quest 3S is the cheapest way into VR; PSVR2 is now competitively priced if you already have the PS5 half of the equation.
