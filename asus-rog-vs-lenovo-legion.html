ASUS ROG vs Lenovo Legion

ASUS ROG and Lenovo Legion are the two biggest names fighting over the mid-to-high-end gaming laptop shelf at NZ retailers right now. Neither is objectively "better" — they're built around different priorities.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
How we picked these: We compared current-generation ROG Strix and Legion Pro/5-series configurations using manufacturer specifications and NZ retailer listings, focused on the RTX 5060/5070-class models most NZ buyers are actually looking at.

The short version

Both brands now sell 2026 laptops built around Intel Core Ultra HX processors paired with NVIDIA RTX 50-series GPUs, so raw gaming performance between similarly-specced models is close. The real differences are in display refresh rates, cooling design, chassis weight, and price — and that's where each brand's personality shows.

Performance

ASUS's 2026 ROG Strix G16 pairs an Intel Core Ultra 9 processor with RTX 5060 or RTX 5070 GPU options, while Lenovo's Legion Pro line runs similar Core Ultra HX chips up to the Core Ultra 9 290HX Plus, with GPU options scaling as high as RTX 5090 in the flagship Legion Pro 7. At matched GPU tiers, expect very similar frame rates — the deciding factor is usually cooling sustain under long sessions rather than headline clock speeds.

Displays

ASUS pushes refresh rates harder, with ROG Strix models available up to 300Hz on some configurations — useful if you play competitive shooters where every frame counts. Lenovo Legion displays typically top out between 165Hz and 240Hz depending on model, still excellent for the vast majority of games, and Legion's panels are generally well regarded for colour accuracy out of the box.

Cooling and build

ASUS uses liquid metal thermal compound (Conductonaut Extreme) across both CPU and GPU on higher-end ROG Strix models, which tends to help with sustained performance during long gaming sessions. Lenovo's Legion Coldfront cooling system is engineered to run quieter under load, and Lenovo has trimmed chassis weight in recent Legion generations — the 15.3-inch Legion 5 line is noticeably more compact than ASUS's 16-inch Strix G16.

Price and NZ availability

Both brands sell directly to NZ through their own online stores as well as through PB Tech, Harvey Norman, JB Hi-Fi and Noel Leeming, so stock and pricing move often. As a rough guide, entry-level RTX 5060-class configurations from either brand tend to start from around $2,500-$2,800 in New Zealand, with RTX 5070 Ti and higher configurations pushing well past $4,000. Lenovo Legion models generally undercut similarly-specced ASUS ROG models by a meaningful margin — ASUS's premium materials, cooling and higher refresh-rate panels carry a price tag to match. Always check current listings, since GPU-tier pricing shifts regularly.

Who should buy ASUS ROG

Buyers chasing the highest refresh rates, premium build materials, and the most aggressive cooling for sustained performance — competitive gamers and anyone who wants the laptop to look and feel like a flagship gaming device.

Who should buy Lenovo Legion

Buyers who want the best performance-per-dollar, a lighter and more portable chassis, and quieter operation under load. Legion is consistently the value pick when you compare like-for-like GPU tiers.
