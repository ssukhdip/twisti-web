Smart Fitness Mirrors: Are They Worth Buying?

A touchscreen behind reflective glass that streams live workout classes sounds like the future of home fitness. It's also several hundred dollars plus an ongoing subscription — here's whether that beats a TV and a fitness app.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

How the format actually works

A smart fitness mirror looks like a normal full-length mirror when it's off. Switch it on and a screen behind the semi-reflective glass shows a live or on-demand instructor, while you can still see your own reflection overlaid to check form. The pitch is that it disappears into your home as furniture when not in use, unlike a treadmill or a rack of equipment.

The subscription is the real cost

The hardware price is only half the story. Every smart fitness mirror on the market requires an ongoing monthly subscription to access the class library — without it, the device is largely a dead screen. Over two or three years of ownership, subscription fees typically add up to more than the mirror itself cost. That's worth sitting with before you buy: you're not paying once, you're signing up to a recurring cost indefinitely, and if the company behind the mirror shuts down its service (which has happened to fitness hardware brands before), an expensive mirror becomes an ordinary mirror.

Space requirements are real too. These need a clear stretch of wall and enough open floor in front of them — realistically 1.5-2 metres — to do a full workout without hitting furniture. In a lot of NZ homes and apartments, that's a bigger ask than it sounds until you actually measure your intended spot.

Fitness mirror vs TV/tablet with a fitness app

  • Upfront cost: a mirror runs several hundred to well over a thousand dollars; a fitness app subscription on a TV or tablet you already own costs nothing extra upfront.
  • Ongoing cost: both need a subscription for premium content, but the app-only route means you're not also paying off dedicated hardware.
  • Form feedback: the mirror's reflection-plus-overlay is a genuinely nice touch for checking your own form live; a TV doesn't offer that at all.
  • Flexibility: a tablet or phone propped up works in any room, including outside; a wall-mounted mirror is fixed in one spot.
  • Resale/reuse: if you stop using a fitness app, your TV or tablet keeps doing everything else it always did; a fitness mirror that stops getting used is a large, mostly pointless mirror.

Our honest take

If real-time form feedback in the reflection genuinely matters to you — for strength training especially, where posture and alignment matter — the mirror format offers something a flat screen can't replicate. For most people doing general cardio, yoga, or class-style workouts, a decent TV or tablet with a fitness app (many of which are considerably cheaper than the mirror ecosystems) delivers 90% of the value without the upfront hardware cost or the risk of an expensive brick if the company folds.

If you do decide a mirror is right for you, compare pricing and check current stock across JB Hi-Fi, Harvey Norman, Noel Leeming and PB Tech, since availability for this category can be patchier in NZ than mainstream electronics.
