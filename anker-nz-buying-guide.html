Anker in NZ: The Complete Buying Guide

Anker has grown from a phone-charger brand into a wide catalogue of power banks, chargers, cables, and audio gear. Here's how to navigate it as an NZ shopper.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

What Anker actually makes

Anker's core business is still charging technology — wall chargers, power banks, and cables — but the company now sells under several sub-brands for different categories. Soundcore covers headphones, earbuds and speakers; Eufy covers smart home and security devices; Nebula covers portable projectors. When shopping, it helps to know which sub-brand you're actually looking at, since pricing, retailer availability, and warranty terms can differ slightly between them.

Power banks: what actually matters

  • Capacity (mAh) — higher capacity means more charges but also more size and weight; match it to how many days you're away from a power outlet
  • Output wattage — higher wattage charges devices faster and is essential if you want to fast-charge a laptop, not just a phone
  • Port count — useful if you regularly charge multiple devices from one bank
  • Passthrough charging — lets you charge the bank and a connected device simultaneously, handy for travel

Anker's range spans compact, pocket-sized banks up to large multi-device units aimed at laptop charging, so it's worth matching the product to your actual use case rather than just buying the highest-capacity option.

GaN chargers vs standard wall chargers

Anker's GaN (gallium nitride) chargers use a more efficient chip technology that allows higher power output in a smaller, cooler-running form factor compared to older silicon-based chargers. If you're replacing an old brick-style charger, a GaN charger from Anker's Nano or Prime lines typically charges faster and takes up less space in a bag — a genuinely worthwhile upgrade for frequent travellers.

Soundcore audio: where it fits

Anker's Soundcore line covers budget-to-midrange earbuds, headphones, and portable speakers, generally positioned as a value alternative to premium brands like Sony or Bose. Build quality and sound have improved significantly across generations, and Soundcore is often a reasonable pick if you want solid everyday audio without paying premium-brand prices — though top-tier noise cancellation and audio fidelity generally still favour the established premium brands.

Buying Anker in NZ

Anker and Soundcore products are stocked by major NZ retailers including PB Tech, Noel Leeming, JB Hi-Fi, Harvey Norman, and via Anker's official presence on marketplaces like Amazon and various NZ electronics resellers. Because Anker has a huge catalogue with frequent new releases and firmware-level product variants, it's worth double-checking the exact model number before buying, since very similar-looking products can have meaningfully different specs.

Watch for: Anker's warranty terms and lengths can vary by product category and retailer, and grey-market or third-party marketplace listings don't always carry the same warranty support as authorised NZ retailers. Buying through a recognised retailer is generally the safer option for warranty claims.

Getting the best price

Anker's catalogue is large enough that pricing can vary meaningfully between NZ retailers for the same model, and sales events (Black Friday, Boxing Day, and Anker's own periodic promotions) can offer solid discounts. Comparing current listings before buying — especially for higher-cost items like large power banks or Soundcore headphones — is worth the extra few minutes.
