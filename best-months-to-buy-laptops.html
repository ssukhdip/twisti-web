Best Months to Buy Laptops

Laptops have two very different discount seasons that pull in opposite directions. Knowing which one to chase depends on what you actually need.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Two competing cycles: chip refresh and school calendar

Laptop pricing is shaped by two overlapping forces. The first is the underlying chip refresh cycle - Intel, AMD and Apple all release new processor generations roughly once a year, and laptops built on the outgoing chip get discounted once the new generation is announced. The second is the NZ school and university calendar, which creates genuine retailer-driven demand spikes and matching discount pushes around back-to-school time.

The NZ back-to-school window

Late January through early-to-mid February is peak back-to-school in New Zealand, with the school year and most university enrolments starting in late January/February. Retailers know this and run heavy laptop promotions in this window specifically aimed at students and families - often bundled with student discounts, cashback offers, or free accessories. This is a genuine demand-driven discount period, not a fake sale event, because retailers are competing hard for a real seasonal spike in laptop purchases.

Chip refresh timing

  • Intel and AMD: new mobile processor generations typically land at CES in January, with laptops built on them arriving through the first half of the year - meaning laptops on the outgoing chip generation get discounted from around February onward as newer-chip models start appearing.
  • Apple: MacBook refreshes are less predictable in timing but tend to cluster in spring (March-May) or around WWDC in June, with occasional autumn refreshes tied to new chip generations. When a new MacBook chip generation lands, the previous generation drops in price fairly quickly since Apple's own lineup only has room for so many active models.

Back-to-school vs Black Friday - which is actually cheaper

Back-to-school (late Jan-Feb): best for mid-range, student-focused laptops - lots of competition between retailers for this exact segment, often with bundled extras (bags, software, cashback) rather than the single biggest headline percentage discount.

Black Friday (late November): best for the deepest percentage discounts across the widest range of models, including higher-end and gaming laptops that rarely get discounted as hard in the school season. If you don't need a laptop urgently for term start, Black Friday usually beats back-to-school on raw dollar savings.

Why waiting for the next chip generation can backfire

It is tempting to always wait for "the new chip" before buying, but new-generation laptops usually launch at a price premium over the model they replace, and that premium can persist for months before any discounting starts. If the outgoing generation already meets your actual needs - and for most everyday use, it will - buying it discounted right after a refresh announcement is usually the better dollar-for-performance move than paying full price for the newest chip.

How this differs from the general calendar

Our general seasonal sale calendar covers back-to-school and Black Friday broadly across all categories. Laptops are a special case because they have this extra chip-refresh dimension layered on top - a laptop can be discounted for a calendar reason (Black Friday) and a technical reason (chip refresh) at completely different times of year, and the two can occasionally stack for an especially good buy.

Common trap: "back to school" pricing on laptops is often genuinely competitive on mid-range models, but premium and gaming laptops are rarely discounted much in that window - retailers save the big cuts on those for Black Friday. If you want a high-end machine, it is usually worth waiting rather than buying in February.

What to actually do

Need a laptop for term start and shopping mid-range? Buy in late January-February and compare bundled extras, not just headline price. Want the best possible dollar discount on a higher-end machine and can wait? Target Black Friday. Either way, check whether a chip refresh has just happened - if so, the outgoing generation is very likely the best value option on the shelf right now.
