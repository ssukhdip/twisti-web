Whoop vs Oura Ring

Both track recovery, sleep, and strain without a screen — but they're worn completely differently and cost meaningfully different amounts to keep running. Here's the real comparison.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
How we picked these: Compared using Whoop's NZ storefront and JB Hi-Fi NZ pricing for Whoop 5.0, against Oura Ring 4 pricing from JB Hi-Fi NZ and Noel Leeming. Both companies run subscription-based models, so we've broken hardware and ongoing cost out separately.

Form factor: wrist band vs finger ring

Whoop 5.0 is a screen-free band worn on the wrist or, with accessory attachments, on the bicep or even clothing. Oura Ring 4 is worn on a finger, styled like a slightly chunky ring. Both are designed to be worn 24/7, including while sleeping and showering, and neither has a display — all your data lives in the phone app. The ring form factor tends to be less noticeable day-to-day for people who don't normally wear anything on their wrist, while the band gives Whoop slightly more surface area for sensors and, some users report, more consistent skin contact during workouts with lots of hand movement.

Pricing: hardware vs subscription

Both brands sell hardware cheaply (or bundle it into a plan) and make their money on the ongoing membership — this is the single most important thing to understand before choosing between them.

  • Whoop: device from around NZ$79 bundled with membership; membership renews annually at roughly NZ$359 (One), NZ$489 (Peak), or NZ$739 (Life).
  • Oura: ring itself costs NZ$454–$559 depending on finish/size, a genuine one-off hardware purchase; membership is NZ$11.99/month or NZ$139.99/year on top.

Over three years, Whoop's ongoing cost is substantially higher than Oura's, even accounting for Oura's upfront ring price — Whoop's cheapest tier alone (~NZ$359/year) outpaces Oura's membership (~NZ$140/year) by a wide margin. If total cost of ownership matters to you, Oura is the cheaper long-term option; if you want a fully-bundled "device is basically free" model, Whoop's approach has some appeal despite the higher recurring bill.

What each is actually good at

Whoop leans heavily into training load, strain scoring, and recovery coaching — it's built with athletes and structured training in mind, and its strain/recovery metrics are widely regarded as some of the most detailed in the wearable space. Oura leans into sleep architecture, general wellness, and long-term trend tracking (including women's cycle tracking), with a slightly gentler, less workout-obsessed framing. Neither does GPS-based run tracking or on-wrist notifications — for that, you still want a proper smartwatch like a Garmin or Apple Watch.

Comfort and battery life

Oura Ring's battery lasts roughly 4-8 days between charges via a small dock; Whoop 5.0's battery life has reportedly roughly doubled over the 4.0 generation, with Whoop's own figures suggesting multi-day life plus a wireless battery pack accessory that lets you charge on the body without taking the band off — a genuine convenience advantage over Oura, which must come off the finger to charge.

Which one to choose

  • Serious athlete or gym-goer tracking training load closely: Whoop, despite the higher ongoing cost — its strain/recovery coaching is more developed.
  • Want the lowest ongoing cost and don't need workout-specific coaching: Oura.
  • Dislike anything on your wrist but don't mind a ring: Oura.
  • Want to charge without ever removing the device: Whoop, via its clip-on battery pack.
