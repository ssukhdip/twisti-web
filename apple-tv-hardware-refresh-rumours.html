Apple TV Hardware Refresh Rumours: Everything We Know So Far

Apple's current Apple TV 4K dates back to 2022, and it shows. Consistent reporting throughout 2026 points to a new model finally arriving — but Apple has said nothing officially, and the timeline keeps sliding.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
Why we're labelling this so carefully: Apple has not announced a new Apple TV. Everything below the "confirmed" section comes from supply-chain and media reporting, primarily MacRumors, which has been consistently ahead of Apple hardware launches in the past but is still reporting, not confirming.

What's confirmed

Confirmed

Apple has not announced a new Apple TV model, a release date, or any specifications for one as of July 2026. The current Apple TV 4K (2022, 3rd generation) remains the newest model officially sold.

Confirmed

Apple raised prices on the existing 2022 Apple TV 4K, along with Macs and iPads, in a June 2026 pricing update — a move that makes a simultaneous price cut on a new model reported as unlikely.

Confirmed

Apple has publicly delayed its overhauled, AI-powered Siri to iOS 26.4/iOS 27, pushed out to around September 2026, after acknowledging ongoing development issues with the feature.

What's reported

Reported

MacRumors has reported consistently through April and July 2026 that the next Apple TV will use the A17 Pro chip (first introduced in the iPhone 15 Pro in 2023), bringing hardware-accelerated ray tracing and AV1 decoding to the set-top box for the first time.

Reported

Reports also point to Apple's own N1 networking chip landing in the new Apple TV, adding Wi-Fi 7 support — the same chip Apple has used in recent iPhone and AirPods hardware.

Reported

Coverage from MacDailyNews and Cord Cutters News suggests the external design will stay unchanged, and that the launch has effectively been tied to the delayed AI Siri rollout — meaning the hardware could arrive alongside new iPhones around September 2026, once Siri's AI features are ready to ship.

Twisti's opinion

Opinion

This is one of the more boring-but-believable Apple rumours going around: a spec bump using an already-shipping chip, tied to a software delay Apple has openly admitted to. It's consistent, unglamorous, and doesn't require any speculative leaks to make sense. If you're in NZ and your current Apple TV still works fine, there's a reasonable case for waiting until closer to September 2026 before buying, given how tightly this refresh appears linked to the Siri timeline.
