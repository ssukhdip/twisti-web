Palm Vein & Biometric Smart Locks: What's Coming to NZ

A technology that used to live in bank vaults and hospital server rooms — palm vein recognition — has quietly become a front-door feature in the US this year, and Kiwi shoppers are starting to notice.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
Why we're labelling this so carefully: Twisti's whole value is showing you real, current prices — not guesses. None of the pricing below is confirmed. We're publishing it because people are genuinely searching for this information already, but we'd rather be upfront that it's industry speculation than let a rumour read like a fact.

What's confirmed

Palm vein locks are real, shipping products in the US as of 2026 — not vaporware.

Products like the eufy FamiLock E34, Tapo DL130, Wyze Palm Lock and TCL's new D2 Pro (unveiled at CES 2026) all use near-infrared light to map the unique vein pattern beneath your palm rather than reading a surface fingerprint. It's contactless — you hover your hand near the sensor rather than pressing it — and vein patterns require live blood flow, which makes them considerably harder to spoof than a fingerprint lifted off a glass.

Prices have collapsed from enterprise territory into consumer territory.

What was a $10,000-plus commercial access-control system a few years ago is now a residential deadbolt. The eufy FamiLock E34 has been tracked at retail prices from roughly US$180 to $280, with sale pricing regularly dropping it to the $180–$200 US mark. TCL's D2 Pro, announced at CES 2026 with Matter over Thread support and BHMA Grade 3 certification, is slated for Q2 2026 availability in the US market.

What's reported

None of the major palm vein lock brands have confirmed official New Zealand retail availability.

eufy, TCL, Wyze, Tapo and VenoTech (which sells through Home Depot in the US) all currently market these locks as US or North America releases. There's no NZ launch date, no NZD pricing, and no confirmation from any of these brands that local certification or distribution is even in progress.

If and when these locks do arrive in NZ, expect a price bump beyond the simple currency conversion.

Smart home hardware that launches in the US at US$180–$280 has historically landed in NZ and Australia anywhere from 30% to 60% above a straight FX conversion once freight, GST, and local distributor margins are added — a pattern seen with other Anker/eufy and TP-Link (Tapo) products sold through NZ retailers. Nobody has stated a specific NZD figure for palm vein locks yet, so treat any number you see online as a guess dressed up as a fact.

Matter over Thread support could make NZ compatibility easier once these locks do land.

TCL's D2 Pro and several eufy models now support Matter over Thread, which is a genuinely good sign for New Zealand buyers — it means the lock could integrate with Apple Home, Google Home and Amazon Alexa hubs already common in NZ homes, rather than requiring a proprietary bridge. This is a technical detail rather than a confirmation of local retail plans, though.

Biometric data privacy is being positioned as a selling point, not an afterthought.

Manufacturers describe palm vein data as processed and stored locally on the lock itself rather than uploaded to the cloud. That's a meaningful distinction for NZ buyers given how the Privacy Act 2020 treats biometric information, but no brand has published an NZ-specific privacy statement because none have confirmed an NZ launch.

Twisti's opinion

We think palm vein locks will reach NZ eventually, but probably via grey-import and parallel-import retailers before any official brand launch.

Kiwi shoppers already routinely import US-spec smart home gear through resellers and freight-forwarders, and a Wi-Fi deadbolt with Matter support doesn't need much local infrastructure to work here. Our guess is enthusiasts will be unlocking doors with their palms well before eufy, TCL or Tapo announce a formal ANZ retail push — but "our guess" is exactly that, not a forecast you should shop around.

What we'll update

We'll revise this page the moment any of eufy, TCL, Tapo, Wyze or VenoTech confirms NZ or Australian retail availability, local pricing in NZD, or a Bunnings/Mitre 10/JB Hi-Fi style distribution deal — whichever comes first.
