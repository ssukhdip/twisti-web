Best Gaming Headsets NZ

A gaming headset lives or dies on mic quality and comfort over long sessions, not just the driver spec sheet. Here's what we'd actually buy in each price band.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

What makes a gaming headset different from regular headphones

Regular headphones and earbuds are tuned for listening; gaming headsets have to do double duty as a microphone as well. That changes what actually matters: a boom mic with good noise rejection is often more important day to day than marginally better driver quality, especially if you play with a squad or take work calls on the same headset. Clamping force and weight matter more too, since gaming sessions run far longer than a commute or workout.

The other big fork in the road is platform. PC gamers can lean on software-driven surround sound and equalisation that consoles don't always support, while PlayStation and Xbox players need to check compatibility carefully — some headsets need a proprietary USB dongle that only works with one platform, and Bluetooth headsets often can't be used for game audio on consoles at all due to latency and licensing restrictions.

Our picks for 2026

SteelSeries Arctis Nova Pro — best overall, PC and multi-platform

NZ price: around $449 to $549.

The Nova Pro's swappable battery system means you're never stuck charging mid-session, and the included base station makes switching between PC and console audio sources simple. Mic quality is a genuine step up from most gaming headsets, clear enough that it doubles fine as a work headset. It's a premium price, but the build quality and comfort over multi-hour sessions justify it for anyone who games most days.

Who it suits: serious PC gamers, streamers, and anyone who wants one headset that works properly across PC and console.

HyperX Cloud Alpha — best budget wired option

NZ price: around $129 to $159.

A simple 3.5mm wired headset with no dongles, no battery to manage, and near-universal compatibility across PC, console and even a phone in a pinch. The dual-chamber drivers give it a noticeably clearer sound than most headsets at this price, and the detachable mic means you can use it as regular headphones when you're not gaming. The trade-off is no wireless freedom and a more basic mic than the premium options above.

Who it suits: anyone who wants reliable sound and a decent mic without spending big, or as a solid backup headset.

PlayStation Pulse Elite — best for PS5 owners

NZ price: around $249 to $279.

Built specifically around the PS5's audio hardware, the Pulse Elite gets you Sony's Tempest 3D audio processing without any setup faff, plus a genuinely good noise-cancelling mic and a separate charging dock. Because it's built for one ecosystem, it doesn't need proprietary software or a base station taking up desk space. The catch is it's a weaker fit if you also game on PC or Xbox, where you lose some of the platform-specific features.

Who it suits: PS5-first households who aren't juggling multiple platforms.

Logitech G435 — best value wireless

NZ price: around $99 to $129.

A genuinely lightweight wireless headset that skips a lot of gimmicks in favour of doing the basics well: solid battery life, decent sound, and support for both a USB dongle and Bluetooth simultaneously so it works across PC, console and phone. The mic is fine rather than exceptional, and there's no active noise cancelling, but for casual to mid-level gamers who don't want a premium price tag, it covers the essentials.

Who it suits: anyone who wants wireless convenience across multiple devices without paying flagship prices.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Buying a PC-focused headset for console-only use. Check the box specifically states PS5 or Xbox compatibility — some PC gaming headsets need software that doesn't exist on consoles, limiting features like surround sound.
  • Ignoring clamping force and weight for long sessions. A headset that feels fine for twenty minutes in-store can become uncomfortable after a three-hour raid. Heavier headsets with tight clamping are the most common source of returns.
  • Assuming Bluetooth means it works for console gaming. Most consoles don't support Bluetooth audio for game sound due to latency, so a Bluetooth-only headset often only works for chat, not game audio, on PlayStation or Xbox.
  • Overlooking mic quality in favour of driver specs. If you play with friends or stream, a clear mic matters more day to day than marginal audio fidelity gains.
Worth knowing: if you wear glasses, look specifically for headsets with softer, wider ear cushions — the standard foam pads on many gaming headsets can create uncomfortable pressure points against glasses arms during long sessions.
