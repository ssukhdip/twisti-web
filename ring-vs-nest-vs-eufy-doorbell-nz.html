Ring vs Nest vs Eufy: Best Video Doorbell for NZ

The doorbell itself is only half the cost — the subscription you'll be nagged into for months afterward is the part that actually determines what you pay over three years. Here's how Ring, Nest and Eufy really compare for NZ homes.

Twisti guides · Updated August 2026

Upfront pricing in NZ

  • Ring Video Doorbell (wired, entry): Roughly $130-160, the basic wired model with no battery to manage.
  • Ring Video Doorbell Plus / Pro: Roughly $250-350, adds 1536p video, head-to-toe framing and better night vision.
  • Google Nest Doorbell (battery): Roughly $270-320, wireless and battery-powered, easier for renters since it doesn't need existing doorbell wiring.
  • Google Nest Doorbell (wired, 2nd gen): Roughly $320-380, hardwired for continuous power and generally sharper video.
  • Eufy Video Doorbell (battery or wired): Roughly $200-320 depending on model (E340 dual-camera, or the simpler single-camera models), positioned as the "no subscription needed" option.

The real cost driver: subscriptions

This is where the three brands genuinely diverge, and it's the number that matters most over a 2-3 year ownership period.

Cloud subscription costs in NZD/month

Ring Protect (Basic, single device): Around $4-5/month or roughly $40-50/year, required for any video recording history, person alerts and event-triggered clips. Without it, Ring functions only as a live-view doorbell with no recording.

Google Nest Aware: Around $10-12/month or roughly $100-120/year for the standard tier (30-day event video history), with a pricier tier for 24/7 continuous recording. Nest Aware also covers all Nest/Google Home devices on the account, so it's better value if you already run other Nest cameras.

Eufy: No subscription required for core functionality — recordings are stored locally on the doorbell's built-in storage or an optional HomeBase hub with expandable storage. Eufy does offer an optional cloud backup subscription for off-site storage, but it's not required to access recorded footage or receive alerts.

Concrete tip: Over 3 years, Ring Protect costs roughly $120-150 and Nest Aware costs roughly $300-360 on top of the hardware price. A Eufy doorbell costing $50-100 more upfront than a base Ring but requiring zero subscription typically becomes cheaper overall by year two — do this maths before assuming the cheapest sticker price is the cheapest total cost.

Local storage vs cloud: the privacy tradeoff

Eufy's local-storage approach means your doorbell footage never has to leave your home network to be reviewed — it's stored on-device or on your HomeBase hub, and you access it via the Eufy app over your own network or a secured remote connection. This appeals strongly to NZ buyers wary of ongoing subscription creep and to anyone uncomfortable with footage of their front door sitting on a US company's cloud servers indefinitely. Eufy has had past security research findings about cloud-connectivity claims that it addressed via firmware updates — worth a quick search for the current firmware version's status before buying if this concerns you, but the core local-storage functionality itself doesn't require cloud upload.

Ring and Nest both default to cloud storage, meaning footage is encrypted and stored on Amazon's and Google's respective servers. This is generally convenient (footage survives even if your doorbell is stolen off the wall) but means you're trusting a US tech company's data policies and, in Ring's case, a documented history of law enforcement data-sharing arrangements in the US that have drawn privacy criticism — a legitimate consideration for privacy-conscious NZ buyers even though NZ Police data requests would follow separate, NZ-specific legal processes.

NZ wiring and Wi-Fi considerations

Wired vs battery power

Most NZ homes built before the 2000s have simple low-voltage doorbell wiring (usually 8-24V AC via a transformer) that's compatible with wired Ring and Nest doorbells, but many newer builds and most apartments have no doorbell wiring at all — in that case, a battery-powered model (Ring battery, Nest battery, or Eufy battery) is the only realistic option without an electrician visit. Battery models need recharging every 1-3 months depending on usage and weather-triggered motion events (windy sites near the coast, common in NZ, drain batteries faster from constant motion triggers).

Wi-Fi range at the front door

Front doors are often the furthest point in the house from the router. All three brands need a reasonably strong 2.4GHz Wi-Fi signal (5GHz range is usually too short) at the doorbell's mounting point — if your router is at the back of the house, budget for a Wi-Fi extender or mesh node near the entryway, regardless of which brand you choose, or you'll get dropped connections and delayed notifications.

Bottom line

If you want the lowest ongoing cost and value keeping footage off third-party cloud servers, the Eufy Video Doorbell is the clear pick for NZ buyers — higher upfront cost, zero required subscription. If you're already deep in the Google Home ecosystem with other Nest devices, Nest Aware's shared subscription makes the Nest Doorbell better value than it looks alone. Ring remains the most polished app experience and easiest for first-time smart home buyers, but budget for Ring Protect from day one — the doorbell is close to useless for reviewing missed visitors without it.
