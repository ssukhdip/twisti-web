Garmin vs Apple Watch: which smartwatch actually earns its keep

One charges weekly and trains you like a coach. The other charges nightly and disappears into your iPhone life. Here's who should actually buy which.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

The short version

This is the smartwatch comparison where the answer depends almost entirely on what phone is in your pocket and how seriously you train. A mid-range Garmin Venu 4 runs $897-$1,099 NZD depending on size, while the Apple Watch Series 11 starts around $749 NZD. Garmin costs more at this tier and only works properly if you actually use its fitness depth — otherwise you're overpaying for a watch face.

Battery life: it isn't close

Garmin wins this category outright and it isn't subtle. Depending on the model, Garmin watches run anywhere from 11 to 28 days on a charge in smartwatch mode, versus 18 to 36 hours for the Apple Watch Series 11 (with the newer low-power mode). That's the difference between charging roughly weekly versus charging every single night. For anyone who's ever forgotten to charge their Apple Watch before a big run, that alone might settle the decision.

GPS accuracy

Garmin's mid-range and premium watches use multi-band GPS with SatIQ technology — purpose-built for accurate route tracking in tricky conditions like forest trails or city canyons with tall buildings. In independent half-marathon testing, a Garmin watch logged 13.15 miles against an official 13.1-mile course, while an Apple Watch logged 13.22 miles on the same run — Garmin was marginally closer to the real distance. Apple's Watch Ultra 3 has closed the gap significantly with dual-frequency GPS, but standard Series 11 models still trail Garmin's dedicated sports hardware slightly. For casual walks and everyday tracking, both are close enough that you won't notice.

Health tracking depth and accuracy

Apple has pushed hard into clinical-grade features: next-gen optical heart sensors, deeper sleep staging, blood oxygen tracking (region-dependent), and tight integration with the Health app that most iPhone owners already check daily. It's polished, easy to understand, and backed by genuine medical research partnerships.

Garmin goes deeper on sports-specific metrics: training load, recovery time, running dynamics, VO2 max estimates, and long-term trend data built for people who actually structure their training around numbers. If you're a serious runner, cyclist or triathlete, Garmin's data is more actionable — it tells you when to back off, not just what your heart rate was.

Ecosystem lock-in: the deciding factor for most people

  • Apple Watch only pairs with an iPhone — full stop. No iPhone, no Apple Watch. If you're an iPhone owner, the integration (notifications, Messages, Find My, unlocking your Mac) is seamless in a way no other watch can match.
  • Garmin works with both iOS and Android, with near-identical functionality on either. If you're on Android, or you switch phones between platforms, or you simply don't want your watch tied to one ecosystem, Garmin is the only serious option between these two.
Mythbusting: Garmin's advertised battery life is a best-case number. Garmin's up to 28 days ratings are measured in smartwatch mode with GPS, always-on display and most sensors dialled back or off. Turn on always-on display, use GPS for training sessions regularly, and leave notifications flowing, and real-world battery life typically drops by 25-40% from the marketing figure. A Venu 4 rated for 11 days might realistically deliver 7-8 days of normal mixed use — still miles ahead of Apple, but don't expect the number on the box.

The verdict

If you're an iPhone owner who wants deep integration with your existing Apple devices, cleaner everyday notifications, and don't mind nightly charging — get the Apple Watch Series 11. It's also the cheaper entry point at this comparison's tier.

If you're a serious runner, cyclist or multi-sport athlete who wants a watch that survives a week of training without a charger, works on any phone, and gives you data you'll actually use to train smarter — get the Garmin. Venu 4 for a smartwatch-first experience with strong fitness chops, or step up to a Forerunner or Fenix if running or multisport is genuinely your main focus.

Don't buy Garmin just for the battery life bragging rights if you're not going to touch the training features — at that point the Apple Watch's software polish and ecosystem integration make more sense for less money.
