Ring vs Arlo

Ring is cheaper to buy but leans hard on subscriptions to be useful. Arlo costs more upfront but gives you more for free. Here's the real breakdown for NZ homes.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
How we picked these: Compared current Ring and Arlo hardware ranges (doorbells and cameras) and their respective cloud subscription plans, using published pricing cross-checked against NZ retailer listings and both brands' own sites. Both ecosystems are sold in NZ through mainstream retailers and direct import channels.

Hardware pricing

Ring's camera and doorbell lineup runs roughly NZ$89 to NZ$399 depending on model — the Ring Video Doorbell sits at the affordable end, while the Ring Spotlight Cam Pro and other premium models sit toward the top. Arlo's range runs higher, roughly NZ$149 to NZ$699, reflecting Arlo's positioning as the more premium option with better base image quality across the range, even before subscriptions are factored in.

Subscription costs — where the real difference is

Ring Protect plans range from about $4.99 to $19.99/month (Basic, Standard, Premium tiers), and most of Ring's useful features — cloud video history, person detection, and professional monitoring — sit behind at least the Basic plan. Without a subscription, a Ring camera mostly gives you live view and basic motion alerts, but no recorded video history to review later, which limits its usefulness as an actual security tool.

Arlo Secure plans run from about $7.99/month per camera or $12.99/month for unlimited cameras, up through Secure Plus (~$17.99/month) and Safe & Secure (~$24.99/month) tiers. Arlo's cameras generally offer more usable features without any subscription at all — local storage options and some live-view/alert functionality work out of the box — making it the better choice if you specifically want to avoid an ongoing bill.

Total cost of ownership

Ring is meaningfully cheaper to operate over time if you're comfortable running the cheapest subscription tier or skipping it for lower-priority cameras. Arlo costs more upfront and its subscriptions aren't cheap either, but you get more baseline functionality without paying monthly, which matters if you only want occasional review of footage rather than full-time cloud recording.

Ecosystem and integration

Ring is owned by Amazon and integrates tightly with Alexa and Amazon's neighbourhood-watch style Neighbors app (which has limited relevance in NZ compared to the US, where it originated). Arlo is a standalone company with broader third-party smart home integration, including Apple HomeKit support on select models — a genuine advantage if you're already in the Apple ecosystem and don't want to rely on Alexa.

Which one to choose

  • Want the cheapest entry point and don't mind a subscription: Ring.
  • Want better image quality and more functionality without paying monthly: Arlo.
  • Already using Alexa/Amazon smart home gear: Ring integrates more tightly.
  • Using Apple HomeKit or want broader third-party compatibility: Arlo is the stronger fit.
