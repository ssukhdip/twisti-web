Best Portable Power Station NZ

From weekend camping trips to keeping the fridge running through a storm outage, here's how to pick the right capacity power station without overpaying.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
How we picked these: Picks reflect current NZ retail pricing at Mighty Ape, Noel Leeming and Harvey Norman, cross-checked against manufacturer capacity specs and independent capacity/runtime tests.

Work out your capacity needs first

Power stations are sized in watt-hours (Wh) — the bigger the number, the longer it runs your gear before needing a recharge. A phone charge uses about 15-20Wh, a laptop around 50-70Wh per charge, while a compact fridge draws roughly 40-60Wh per hour. Work backwards from what you actually need to run and for how long, rather than buying the biggest unit on the shelf.

Best for camping: Jackery Explorer 300 Plus

The Jackery Explorer 300 Plus runs about $399 at Mighty Ape and is the right size for a couple camping for a weekend — enough to keep phones, a camera and a small light charged for several days. It's light enough to carry one-handed and Jackery's build quality remains a benchmark in this category.

Best mid-size all-rounder: EcoFlow River 3 Plus

EcoFlow's River 3 Plus sits around $549 at Noel Leeming and adds significantly more capacity and faster recharge times via its X-Stream fast charging, going from flat to 80% in under an hour on mains power. It's the better pick for families or anyone running a CPAP machine overnight on camping trips.

Best for blackout backup: Bluetti AC180

For home blackout preparedness rather than just camping, the Bluetti AC180 (around $1,299 at Harvey Norman) has enough capacity to run a fridge, some lighting and charge devices through a multi-hour outage. Bluetti's units also support solar panel input, so you can extend runtime during a daytime outage if you pair it with a portable panel.

Best budget option: Anker SOLIX C300

Anker's SOLIX C300 undercuts the competition at around $379 at PB Tech for similar capacity to the Jackery 300 Plus. Anker's app and battery management have caught up significantly to the established camping brands, making this a genuinely solid budget pick rather than a compromise.

Solar charging — is it worth adding?

A matching solar panel (most brands sell 100-200W folding panels for $300-$600) extends a power station's usefulness a lot for multi-day trips off-grid, but for pure blackout backup at home it's rarely worth the extra cost — you'll just plug in from the wall before and after an outage. Save the solar panel spend for genuine camping or off-grid use.

What matters more than the marketing numbers

  • Check the actual continuous output wattage (not just peak/surge) against what you plan to run — some smaller units can't start a fridge compressor.
  • LiFePO4 (lithium iron phosphate) batteries last far more charge cycles than older lithium-ion units — worth paying extra for if you'll use it often.
  • Weight matters more than people expect if you're carrying it any distance from the car to a campsite.
