Jackery NZ Buying Guide

Jackery basically created the modern portable power station category, and its NZ lineup now spans tiny weekend units to whole-home backup systems.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
How we picked these: This guide is based on Jackery's current NZ product range as listed at Mighty Ape, Noel Leeming and Jackery's own NZ store, checked against published capacity and output specs.

Jackery's lineup at a glance

Jackery splits its range into the Explorer power stations (portable batteries with inverters), SolarSaga solar panels, and increasingly a home backup line aimed at whole-house circuits. For most NZ buyers, the decision comes down to matching an Explorer model's capacity to what you actually plan to run — a phone and camera on a weekend trip is a very different job to keeping a fridge alive through a multi-hour outage.

Explorer 300 Plus — best for light weekend use

At around $399 from Mighty Ape, the Explorer 300 Plus is Jackery's most compact serious option — enough for phones, cameras, a laptop and small lighting across a few days of camping. It's light enough to carry easily and remains one of the best-built units in its size class.

Explorer 1000 v2 — the all-rounder

The Explorer 1000 v2 (around $999 at Noel Leeming) is the model most families should actually consider. Its capacity comfortably covers a fridge, lighting and device charging through several hours of a power outage, and it recharges quickly enough from mains power that you're not left waiting overnight.

Explorer 2000 v2 — serious home backup

For genuine multi-day blackout resilience or running larger appliances, the Explorer 2000 v2 costs around $1,899 at Jackery's NZ store and JB Hi-Fi. It supports expansion batteries too, so you can start with one unit and add capacity later rather than buying your maximum need upfront.

SolarSaga panels — worth adding?

Jackery's SolarSaga 100W panel runs about $399 and pairs well with the smaller Explorer units for multi-day off-grid trips, recharging a 300 Plus from empty in a few hours of good sun. For pure home blackout backup where you'll recharge from the wall before and after outages, the solar panel is a nice-to-have rather than essential.

Which Jackery model fits your situation

  • Weekend camping, phones and cameras only: Explorer 300 Plus.
  • Family camping with a portable fridge or CPAP: Explorer 1000 v2.
  • Home blackout backup for a few key circuits: Explorer 2000 v2, ideally with an expansion battery.
  • Frequent off-grid trips: any Explorer unit paired with a SolarSaga panel.

How Jackery compares to EcoFlow and Bluetti

Jackery tends to prioritise simplicity and rugged build quality over the fastest recharge speeds or the most feature-dense app, which EcoFlow leans into more. If ultra-fast recharging from mains power matters most to you, it's worth cross-shopping EcoFlow's River and Delta ranges before settling on a Jackery model.
