Best Hair Dryer NZ: Which One to Buy

Every hair dryer box now claims "ionic technology" like it's a revolutionary feature. Some of it is genuine, some of it is marketing filler. Here's what actually affects your hair and your morning routine.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Ionic technology — genuine benefit or marketing buzzword?

Ionic hair dryers emit negative ions that are supposed to break down water droplets on your hair faster, reducing frizz and drying time. There's some legitimate science here — negatively charged ions can neutralise the positive charge that causes hair strands to repel each other and frizz up. In practice, the effect is real but modest, and it varies a lot by hair type. Fine, frizz-prone hair tends to notice more benefit than thick or already-smooth hair.

The problem is nearly every dryer on the market now slaps "ionic" on the box regardless of actual ion output or quality, so the label alone tells you very little. It's a nice-to-have feature, not a reason on its own to pay significantly more for one dryer over another.

Wattage and dry-time trade-offs

Higher wattage (1800W-2400W+) generally means faster drying and more consistent heat, which matters if you have thick or long hair and want to minimise total heat exposure time. Lower wattage dryers (under 1600W) take longer but are gentler and often quieter — reasonable for fine or short hair that dries quickly anyway.

Matching wattage to your hair

Thick, long or curly hair: look for 2000W+ for genuinely faster, more even drying — less time with hot air near your scalp overall.

Fine, short or already-fast-drying hair: 1600-1800W is plenty; you won't notice much benefit from higher wattage and you'll save on power draw.

Is a premium Dyson-style dryer worth it?

Dyson's Supersonic and similar premium dryers (also increasingly copied by other brands at lower price points) use a smaller, more efficient motor housed in the handle rather than the dryer head, which makes them noticeably lighter to hold for long styling sessions and generally quieter than typical dryers at the same airflow. They also tend to include smarter heat sensors to avoid extreme heat damage.

Whether that's worth the price difference over a solid mid-range dryer depends on how often you use it. If you're drying and styling hair daily, the reduced arm fatigue and gentler heat control add up to a genuinely better daily experience. If you dry your hair a couple of times a week, a well-reviewed mid-range dryer will do the job at a fraction of the cost — the marginal difference in hair health from a premium dryer used occasionally is small.

Honest take: the biggest factor in hair health isn't the dryer brand — it's using a lower heat setting and not holding it too close to your hair for extended periods. A cheap dryer used carefully often beats an expensive one used carelessly.

What actually matters when comparing dryers

  • Multiple heat and speed settings: genuinely useful for finishing on a cool shot to set your style without extra heat damage.
  • Weight and handle balance: matters more than most buyers expect for long styling sessions — pick up the dryer in-store if you can before buying.
  • Noise level: some high-power dryers are genuinely loud; check reviews if this matters to you or housemates.
  • Attachments included: a diffuser (for curly/wavy hair) and a concentrator nozzle (for smoother styling) should be included, not sold separately.
  • Cord length: a short cord is a common but easily overlooked annoyance — check this before buying, especially for bathrooms with awkward outlet placement.

Where to buy in NZ

Noel Leeming, Harvey Norman and JB Hi-Fi all stock everything from budget to premium hair dryers, including Dyson's Supersonic range. PB Tech occasionally carries a smaller electronics-focused selection. Compare prices across retailers — premium models in particular can have noticeable price swings between stores during sales periods.
