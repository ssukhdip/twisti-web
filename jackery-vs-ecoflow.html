Jackery vs EcoFlow: Power Stations

Both brands sell the portable power stations NZ campers and preppers reach for during outages — here's how they differ on capacity, solar speed and price.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026
How we picked these: Comparison based on similarly-sized mid-capacity models (around 1000Wh) from each brand, priced against current Noel Leeming, Mighty Ape and direct-to-consumer NZ store listings.

Why these two dominate the category

Jackery and EcoFlow are the two names NZ shoppers land on first when searching for portable power stations, whether for camping, tramping trips off-grid, or as home backup during storm-related outages — a real consideration given how often Cyclone-season weather knocks out power in parts of the North Island.

NZ pricing at similar capacity

A roughly 1000Wh Jackery Explorer unit typically costs $1,299-$1,499 through Jackery's NZ store and Mighty Ape. The equivalent-capacity EcoFlow River/Delta series model runs slightly higher at $1,399-$1,599, though EcoFlow more frequently runs direct-site discounts of 15-20% that can close the gap.

Solar charging speed

This is where EcoFlow tends to pull ahead — its X-Stream fast-charging tech generally recharges from solar panels faster than comparable Jackery units, useful if you're relying on panels through a shorter NZ winter day rather than plugging into mains. Jackery has closed some of this gap with its newer SolarSaga panel pairings, but EcoFlow's peak charge rates are usually still the faster of the two on paper.

Capacity and expandability

  • Jackery units are generally simpler — fixed capacity per unit, straightforward app, fewer configuration options
  • EcoFlow Delta series supports extra battery modules to expand capacity well beyond the base unit, useful for longer outages
  • Both offer 1000W+ output on mid-tier models, enough for a fridge, lights and device charging simultaneously
  • EcoFlow's companion app has more detailed real-time power flow monitoring

Build and portability

Jackery units are generally a touch lighter and simpler to grab-and-go for weekend camping trips, which matches its brand focus on outdoor use. EcoFlow leans a little more into the home-backup use case with its expandable battery approach, which adds bulk but suits people setting up a semi-permanent home power buffer.

Warranty and support in NZ

Both brands sell through NZ-specific online stores with local warranty support, typically 2-3 years depending on model, and both are also stocked through Mighty Ape and Noel Leeming for buyers who'd rather deal with a familiar retailer for returns.

Which to buy

Choose Jackery if you want the simpler, lighter option mainly for camping and tramping trips. Choose EcoFlow if you want faster solar charging and the option to expand capacity for home backup duty during outages.
