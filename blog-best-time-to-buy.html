When's the Best Time to Buy Electronics in New Zealand?

Prices don't drop randomly — they follow a pretty predictable calendar. Here's when to actually expect a genuine deal, category by category.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

"Is now a good time to buy?" is the single most common question in electronics shopping — and it's exactly what Twisti's AI verdict tries to answer on every search. But even without checking a specific product, NZ retail follows a few reliable patterns worth knowing.

Late November
Black Friday / Cyber Monday

The single biggest sale event of the year across every category — TVs, laptops, phones, gaming, appliances. Genuinely the best window for big-ticket electronics, though stock on the most popular specific models can move fast.

Boxing Day – Mid January
Boxing Day and New Year sales

A second major discount window, close behind Black Friday in size. Retailers are also clearing Christmas stock, so this is often the best time for TVs and general home electronics specifically.

January – February
Back to school

Laptops specifically get pushed hard here — student/education pricing and bundle deals show up across JB Hi-Fi, Noel Leeming and PB Tech. Worth waiting for if a laptop isn't urgent in November/December.

Ongoing — tied to launches, not a fixed date
The model-refresh discount

This is the one people miss most often: whenever a new iPhone, Samsung Galaxy, or major laptop line launches, the previous model usually gets discounted immediately to clear shelf space — often 15–25% off, sometimes more. If you don't need the absolute newest model, buying right after a new launch is consistently one of the best-value moments, any time of year.

Mid-year
Mid-year and Father's/Mother's Day promos

Smaller than the big two, but audio gear, small appliances, and gift-shaped tech (watches, earbuds) often get targeted promotions around these dates.

What doesn't reliably discount

Brand-new flagship releases in their first few weeks rarely move on price — demand is highest right when supply is tightest. If you want the newest model on day one, expect to pay close to RRP regardless of season.

The honest caveat: these are general patterns, not guarantees. Actual pricing depends on stock levels, what a specific retailer is trying to clear that week, and genuine one-off promotions that don't follow the calendar at all. Seasonal timing narrows down when to start checking — it doesn't replace actually checking.
