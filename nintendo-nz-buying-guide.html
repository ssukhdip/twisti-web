Nintendo in NZ: consoles, games and accessories explained

From the handheld-only Lite through to Switch 2, Nintendo's hardware lineup has more overlap than most console makers. Here's how to make sense of it before you buy.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Nintendo's current hardware lineup

Unlike Sony and Microsoft, which typically sell one console generation at a time, Nintendo tends to keep multiple hardware variants on shelves simultaneously. In NZ that usually means some combination of Switch Lite, the original/base Switch, Switch OLED and, more recently, Switch 2 — all sold alongside each other, sometimes for years. Knowing which generation you're looking at matters, since game compatibility and performance can differ.

Picking a console

  • Switch Lite: handheld only, smallest and usually cheapest, best for portable-only play or as a kids' second console.
  • Switch OLED: full hybrid (handheld, tabletop, docked TV) with an upgraded screen — the most balanced option for most households.
  • Switch 2: Nintendo's newer-generation hardware, generally offering a meaningful step up in performance and features over the original Switch family — worth comparing directly if budget allows and you want future-proofing for newer game releases.

Buying digital vs cartridge games

Digital game purchases are convenient and avoid running out to a store, but they're tied to your Nintendo account and console, and NZ digital pricing doesn't always track physical retail discounts. Cartridges can be resold or lent, and NZ retailers frequently discount physical copies of older titles well below the digital eShop price. If you're budget-conscious, checking physical prices before buying digital is usually worth the extra minute.

Accessories worth budgeting for

A microSD card is close to mandatory given Nintendo consoles' modest built-in storage. Extra Joy-Con or Pro Controllers are common for multiplayer households, and a screen protector plus carry case are cheap insurance for a console that travels. Official Nintendo accessories cost more than third-party alternatives, but compatibility and durability are generally more consistent — worth weighing up per item rather than assuming one option is always right.

Where to buy in NZ

Nintendo hardware and games are sold through Noel Leeming, JB Hi-Fi, EB Games, The Warehouse, Harvey Norman and PB Tech, among others. Because Nintendo controls console RRP fairly tightly, the real savings usually come from bundle deals, trade-in promotions, or timing a purchase around major sales periods rather than expecting a straight discount on the console alone.

Check hardware generation carefully: Older Switch stock can still be sold as "new" at a reduced price well after a newer model launches. That's not necessarily a problem, but confirm which generation you're buying and that it supports the games or accessories you plan to use with it.
