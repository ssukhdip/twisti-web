iPad Pro in NZ: what to know before you buy

The iPad Pro line sits at the top of Apple's tablet range, aimed at creative and professional workflows. Here's how to choose the right configuration and where to find it cheapest.

Twisti guides · Updated July 2026

Where iPad Pro fits in the lineup

Apple's tablet range typically spans the entry-level iPad, the iPad Air, and the iPad Pro, with the Pro model positioned as the premium option featuring the best display technology, fastest chip, and most storage and RAM headroom available in a tablet. If your use case is mostly browsing, streaming and light note-taking, the Air or standard iPad usually delivers similar day-to-day performance for meaningfully less money. The Pro earns its price premium for people doing demanding creative work — video editing, 3D rendering, or heavy multitasking with external displays.

Screen size and storage decisions

iPad Pro models are generally offered in two screen sizes, with the larger one commanding a price premium. Storage tiers typically range from a modest base capacity up to very large options aimed at video professionals — and the jump between the lowest and highest storage tier can add a substantial amount to the price, often more than doubling the base cost. For most users, a mid-tier storage option balances cost and headroom better than either extreme.

  • Base storage suits light users who rely on cloud storage and don't keep large media libraries on-device.
  • Mid-tier storage is the sensible default for most buyers mixing work and media use.
  • Top-tier storage is really only worth the premium for video editors or those working with large project files locally.

Wi-Fi only vs cellular models

Every iPad Pro generation is available as Wi-Fi only, with a cellular-enabled variant costing more upfront and typically requiring a compatible NZ SIM or eSIM plan. Most buyers who mainly use their iPad at home or with a phone hotspot nearby can skip the cellular premium. It's worth paying for only if you regularly need standalone connectivity away from Wi-Fi.

Accessories add up fast

The Apple Pencil and a keyboard case (such as the Magic Keyboard) are sold separately and are not cheap — together they can add a significant amount on top of the tablet price. If you're budgeting for an iPad Pro as a laptop replacement, factor the keyboard in from the start rather than treating it as an afterthought, since it materially changes the total cost.

Where to buy in NZ

iPad Pro is sold through the Apple Store NZ online, and through major retailers including Noel Leeming, JB Hi-Fi, Harvey Norman and PB Tech. Pricing across these tends to track fairly closely to Apple's RRP, but promotional periods, bundle deals, and trade-in offers can create meaningful gaps between retailers — worth checking before you commit to a configuration.

Check before buying: Apple's product lineup and pricing are updated periodically, and configurations available at any given time can shift. Always confirm current specs, storage tiers and pricing directly with retailers rather than relying on older information.
